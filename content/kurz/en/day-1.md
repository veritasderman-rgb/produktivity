Welcome to day one. Seven days, seven short emails, each with one idea and one prompt you'll try right away. Nothing more, nothing to print out.

Let's start with the most important thing, because without it the rest of the course won't make sense.

## Today's idea

Most people try AI like this: they open a blank window, type “write me something about team motivation,” get five paragraphs about how motivation is key and requires an individual approach, and decide the hype isn't for them.

The problem isn't the tool, and it isn't you. The problem is one word: **topic**.

“Something about team motivation” is a topic, and it returns fluff. “I have a meeting Monday with five people after we lost a bid — give me a structure that keeps it from turning into a blame session” is a task, and it returns something you can use.

The difference between someone AI saves hours for and someone who closes it after a week isn't which tool they picked. It's that the first person assigns work — the way you'd brief a colleague, telling them what, why, and what situation you're in.

And one more thing nobody says out loud: **the model doesn't know when it doesn't know.** When it doesn't know an answer, it won't say “I don't know” — it'll write something plausible. That's why one rule holds for the whole week and will keep coming back: **the machine proposes, the human approves.** Your name goes under the output, not the model's.

## Today's prompt

Take a task you have to do today anyway and start with this. It's the fastest way to turn an average answer into a good one — by letting it ask you questions first.

```
I need you to help me with [describe the task, e.g. draft an
email to a client who hasn't paid an invoice in a month].

Don't write anything yet. First ask me 5 questions whose
answers would most affect the result — the kind you'd
otherwise have to guess at. Be specific, not general ("what
tone" is a weak question; "should this sound conciliatory, or
already like a final notice" is a good one).

Once I answer, write the result, and below it list the
assumptions you still had to fill in yourself.
```

You'll get five questions — two you could have guessed yourself, and three that honestly wouldn't have occurred to you. Sometimes the model answers its own questions and just keeps going — say “wait, I'll answer those questions” and next time put “don't write anything yet” on its own line.

If you haven't picked a tool yet: pick one (ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini) and stick with it for the whole week. The difference between tools is smaller than the difference between a good brief and a bad one. And sensitive data — personal information, private contracts, credentials — doesn't belong in a free chat at all; that only belongs in a paid account with a data-protection agreement, and even then, preferably anonymized.
