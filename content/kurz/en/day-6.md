For six days we've been asking questions. Today let's look at the level where AI works even when no one's sitting at the computer. Think of this as a preview — you don't have to set anything up today.

## Today's idea

Most people stay on AI's first level: open a chat, ask something, close the window. The real jump happens once AI stops waiting for your question.

Today's tools can do two things that make that possible. **Connectors** — you link your email, calendar, or documents, so AI sees your actual data instead of what you paste in by hand. And **routines** — you describe a task once, and it runs on a schedule. At 6:30 a.m. your inbox is already sorted and drafts are started; in the evening you have an overview of what you promised whom.

That sounds like a big leap, but it only works if you make it a small one. Here's the whole secret: **the first routine must not do anything.** It only looks and reports. Nothing gets labeled, moved, deleted, or drafted.

Let it run for a week, and every morning ask yourself one question: **does this sorting match how I'd sort it myself?** If yes, give it permission to draft. If no, adjust the rules and wait another day.

And the rule that has no exceptions with routines, and that matters more than anything else in this course: **sending, paying, deleting, and signing are always approved by a human.** A routine may prepare, sort, and propose. It may never send. The scope of permission you give it is the scope that applies — start with the smallest possible one and expand it only once you've verified it.

## Today's prompt

This is the brief for the first routine — the one that only looks. If you don't have connectors set up yet, read it as an example of how a brief like this is written.

```
Every weekday at 6:30 AM, go through my messages from the
last 24 hours that have the label [Routine].

Sort them into three groups:
A) needs my reply
B) FYI only
C) newsletter or automated, no action needed

For each message in group A, write one line:
sender — what it's about — what they want from me — how
urgent (today / this week / can wait).

Just count and list senders for groups B and C.

Don't label anything, don't move anything, don't delete
anything, don't draft any replies yet. The output is a
summary only.
```

Notice that label. The routine doesn't get your whole inbox — only what you've tagged yourself. That's the simplest way to get comfortable with a limited scope.

Once the sorting checks out three days in a row, you can add the next step. Not sooner. Connecting things before you know how to brief a task just speeds up the production of unusable output.
