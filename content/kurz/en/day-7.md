Last day. Behind you are six skills: briefing instead of topics, the four parts of a prompt, iteration, email, verified documents, numbers, and routines. Today we tie them into one thing that will actually stick.

## Today's idea

Most people don't quit AI because it doesn't work. They quit because **they forget it exists**. The course ends, the excitement fades, and three weeks later everything is back to how it was.

A habit doesn't come from deciding “I'll use AI.” It comes from one specific activity you start doing differently.

So pick **one mini-project for next week.** Not three. One. It should have three properties: you do it at least twice a week, you can judge for yourself whether the output is good, and you don't have to upload any sensitive data to do it.

Typical good choices: first drafts of email replies, meeting notes written up from your own scribbles, meeting prep, summarizing documents before a meeting.

And two things that turn a one-off experiment into a system:

**Save what worked.** A prompt that once returned a great result is a tool, not a throwaway note. Keep one file called “AI prompts” and copy in anything you've used at least twice.

**Write your own standing instructions.** If you keep correcting AI on the same thing (“shorter,” “no intro like that”), tell it once, permanently — most tools have a setting that applies to every new conversation.

## Today's prompt

This one turns the course into a plan.

```
I'm a [profession] and spent a week trying AI on my everyday
work. My typical week looks like this:

[describe 5-8 activities you repeat, and how long each takes]

Suggest one mini-project for the next 7 days: ONE activity
I'll do repeatedly with AI. Pick it so it repeats at least
2x a week and so I can judge the quality myself, without
expert review.

Along with that, give me:
- why this one and not the others (briefly),
- a ready-to-use prompt I can save and reuse every time,
- how I'll know after a week that it's working — one metric
  I can actually measure,
- what to do if it isn't working after three days.

Don't include anything that would require me to paste in
personal data or non-public documents. Keep any savings
estimates conservative.
```

Cut your estimates in half anyway. The first week will take more time than it saves — that's normal, and it's the only investment this course asks of you.
