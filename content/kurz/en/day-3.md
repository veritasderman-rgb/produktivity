Of the whole course, today is the day people most often say “oh.” Because the payoff is immediate, and you'll feel it before the day is out.

## Today's idea

The hardest part of an awkward email isn't the content — it's the first sentence. You know exactly what you want to say — decline, follow up, apologize — and you still sit in front of a blank window for twenty minutes hunting for an opening that won't sound like an excuse or an attack.

That delay is expensive. The email stays a draft, it goes out late, and in the meantime it nags at you all day.

The solution isn't letting AI write your emails. It's letting it write a **first version**, which you then edit into your own in two minutes. The blank-page slowdown disappears, and what's left is the part you're best at anyway — editing.

Watch out for one thing: “reply to this email” isn't enough. The model doesn't know your relationship with this person, what someone else already promised them, or what you absolutely do not want to promise. That's why the single most important line in the whole brief is **“what must not be said”** — without it, models love to tack on an extra reassuring sentence (“of course we can handle that”) that commits you to something you never wanted to promise.

## Today's prompt

This one covers eighty percent of everyday emails. Save it as the first entry in your collection.

```
Draft a reply to this email. I'll still edit it myself, so
I want a draft, not a final version.

INCOMING MESSAGE:
[paste the email text]

WHO I'M REPLYING TO: [relationship — e.g. a client I've
worked with for three years; we're on a first-name basis]
WHAT I WANT TO HAPPEN: [the outcome for the recipient, not
a topic]
FACTS THAT MUST BE INCLUDED: [dates, numbers, terms —
only what's actually true]
WHAT MUST NOT BE SAID: [what not to promise, what not to
touch]
TONE: [e.g. matter-of-fact and warm, no apologetic opener]
LENGTH: [max 6 sentences]

Rules:
- don't invent dates, prices, or circumstances; if
  something's missing, add TO FILL IN: [what] at the end
- don't open with phrases like "thank you for your email"
- don't close with "please don't hesitate to reach out"
- suggest a subject line too, if it should change
```

For a truly sensitive message, ask for three versions right away — conciliatory, neutral, and firm — and pick one. It saves a round of rewriting, and more importantly it shows you where your actual line is.

Take that rule about not inventing things seriously: the model will fill in a date or an order number with total confidence, even though it's never seen either. And before you hit send, read the whole email — **AI drafts, you send.** Always.
