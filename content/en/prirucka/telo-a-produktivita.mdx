Have you ever tried coding at three in the morning, or writing an important report right after lunch? Then you know it isn't the best idea. It isn't your fault — it's biology. Let's put it to work for us instead of fighting it.

## Circadian rhythm: your internal calendar

Your body's internal clock doesn't just govern tiredness and alertness — it drives body temperature, hormones and cognitive performance. And everyone's is set a little differently:

**How to do it:** track your energy and productivity for a week, find the recurring peaks — and schedule demanding tasks into your “peak” hours. If you can't adjust your working hours, at least adjust the order of tasks within the day.

## Sleep: the foundation of performance

Quality sleep isn't a luxury. It's the time when your brain literally flushes out toxins and processes the day's information.

In the morning, daylight as soon as possible after waking helps — and skip the snooze button.

## Food: fuel for the brain

<Donut value={20} label="of the body's energy is consumed by the brain" sub="even though it's only 2% of your weight. What you eat directly shapes how you think." />

- **Breakfast:** complex carbs (oats), protein (eggs, yogurt), healthy fats (avocado, nuts) — within an hour of waking.
- **Lunch:** light but nourishing. Plenty of vegetables, good protein. Heavy, fatty meals = an afternoon crash.
- **Snacks:** nuts and seeds, fruit, veggie sticks with hummus.
- **Timing:** main meals every 3–4 hours, the last one 2–3 hours before bed.
- **Hydration:** 8–10 glasses of water a day, the first one right after waking. Cut caffeine after 2 p.m.

## Movement: body in motion = brain in action

Exercise directly improves memory, focus, creativity and problem-solving.

- **Aerobic activity** (walking, running, swimming, cycling): 30 minutes a day at moderate intensity, ideally in the morning or early afternoon.
- **Strength training** 2–3 times a week: it boosts BDNF — a protein critical for learning — and improves sleep quality.
- **Stretching** for 10–15 minutes daily: it counteracts the aches of desk work and lowers stress.

And during the workday: a morning stretch (5 minutes), a walk at lunch (15–30 minutes), micro-breaks every hour (2–3 minutes), and an evening walk for better sleep.

## Putting it into practice

1. **First week:** track your natural rhythm, set up an evening routine, start taking morning walks.
2. **First month:** adjust your meal timing, establish regular exercise, optimize your sleep environment.
3. **Long term:** tune your daily schedule around your energy and experiment — a sleep tracker and an energy journal will show you what works.

This isn't about radical changes. It's about gradually building habits that support the biology you already have.
