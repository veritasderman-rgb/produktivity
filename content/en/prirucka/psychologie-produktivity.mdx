You probably know the feeling: you're at your computer with an important task in front of you — and yet you're scrolling social media or reading the news for the fourth time. It isn't because you're lazy. It's because you're working **against** your psychology instead of putting it to work for you.

## Procrastination isn't laziness

Procrastination is an emotional response to a task that triggers fear of failure, perfectionism, uncertainty or a sense of overwhelm. That's why “just push through it” doesn't work — you're fighting an emotion, not a shortage of willpower. These three techniques do work:

> **Personal tip:** When I don't feel like starting a big project, I open a blank document and give it a name. That small step alone is often enough to get the engine running.

## Flow: your productivity superpower

Flow is the state where you're fully immersed in your work, time flies and the work seems to do itself. Like reading a gripping book and forgetting the world exists. You can't force it, but you can prepare the ground:

1. **Prepare the environment** — remove distractions, set out your tools, find quiet.
2. **Set the right challenge** — the task can be neither too easy nor too hard. Mild pressure yes, stress no.
3. **Protect your time** — turn off notifications, put your phone on Do Not Disturb, tell your colleagues.

An **entry ritual** helps too: a favorite drink, a specific playlist, a clear desk. Your brain learns to associate the ritual with focus and switches gears faster.

## Stress: a shield, not an enemy

Stress in small doses is motivating. The problem is when there's too much of it or it lasts too long. Three levels of defense:

1. **Prevention:** regular breaks (the Pomodoro technique), clear boundaries between work and rest, realistic planning — the stress of falling behind is most often the stress of a bad plan.
2. **Instant relief:** the 4–7–8 breathing exercise (inhale for 4, hold for 7, exhale for 8), a short walk, a change of scenery. The body can calm the nervous system faster than the mind can.
3. **Long-term solutions:** regular exercise, quality sleep, mindfulness or meditation.

Start keeping a **stress journal**: what stresses you, how the stress shows up, what helps. After a few weeks you'll see patterns and triggers — and those can be solved systematically instead of firefighted.

## Burnout: spotting it before it arrives

Burnout isn't just tiredness. It's a state of physical, emotional and mental exhaustion. Warning signs: chronic fatigue, cynicism, loss of motivation, reduced productivity, sleep problems, headaches or stomach trouble.

**Prevention:**

- **Boundaries** — working hours you actually keep; the ability to say no; delegation.
- **Maintenance** — weekly reflection, a monthly satisfaction check, regular (even short) time off.
- **Resilience** — hobbies outside work, social contact, personal growth.

**First aid when things are already on fire:**

1. **Immediate measures** — take time off, cut back your commitments, seek support (colleagues, family, a therapist). Burnout isn't weakness and it won't disappear by gritting your teeth.
2. **Long-term solutions** — reassess your priorities, change your work habits, build a sustainable way of working. Returning to the same regime that burned you leads to the same place.
