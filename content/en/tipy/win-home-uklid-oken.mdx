You're writing an important document and chaos flickers around it: mail, chat, three browser windows, a spreadsheet. The well-known Win+D shortcut clears the whole desktop — but it also hides the one window you're actually working in. This is exactly what Win+Home is for: it minimizes every window except the active one. All that's left is your work, and quiet.

## How to do it

1. Click into the window you want to keep and press <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>Home</kbd> — every other window minimizes to the taskbar in a single instant.
2. Press <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>Home</kbd> again and the minimized windows return exactly where they were — so it's a temporary tidy-up, not a destruction of your layout.
3. You can achieve the same trick with the mouse: grab a window by its title bar and quickly **shake** it side to side — the other windows drop to the taskbar; shake again and they come back.
4. In Windows 11, shaking is off by default: turn it on in **Settings → System → Multitasking** under the title bar window shake option. In Windows 10 it works out of the box.

## A typical scenario

You have to finish a message for leadership before the end of the hour. But Teams, Outlook, and six browser tabs are all lit up on screen — and every blink in your peripheral vision pulls your eyes away from the sentence you're writing. Win+Home: only the document remains. You write. When you need to look something up, the same shortcut brings the windows back — all in their places, nothing to reopen or rearrange.

The student version: while writing a term paper, only the editor stays on screen while sources wait minimized — zero distraction in sight, everything ready on the taskbar. And because the second press restores windows to exactly their original layout, you also skip the usual tax of tidying up: the five minutes of rearranging windows back to the state where you were working comfortably.

## What you get out of it

- **Focus without closing anything:** distracting windows disappear from view but don't disappear entirely — you return to them with one press once you're done.
- **Speed over manual clicking:** minimizing eight windows one at a time means eight precise clicks on a small button; here it's one shortcut.
- **A complement to Win+D, not a replacement:** [Win+D clears straight to the desktop](/tipy/win-d-plocha) — useful, say, when you need to find something on the desktop; Win+Home instead clears around your unfinished work and leaves that one important thing right in front of you.

## Pro tip

If windows distract you on a regular basis — every morning while writing, every Friday during a report — a more systematic solution is worth considering: [virtual desktops](/tipy/win-virtualni-plochy) permanently separate work and communication windows, so Win+Home then only needs to handle the occasional emergency.
