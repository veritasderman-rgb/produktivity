Paper is great for thinking but bad for archiving — notes get lost, notebooks fill up, and whatever was in last year's notebook is simply gone. A tablet, on the other hand, is great for archiving but full of distraction — notifications, a browser, social networks, all one tap away. E-ink tablets (like the reMarkable 2 or the Kindle Scribe) are a compromise that actually works: the feel of paper while writing, the benefits of digital while storing.

## A typical scenario

You're in a meeting, or you need to quietly think through a complicated problem, and you want to take notes. With a regular tablet or laptop, there's a real risk a notification pops up between the lines, or your hand drifts to the browser “just for a second.” With a paper notebook, you risk not being able to find important notes later among dozens of scribbled-on pages, or losing them entirely. An e-ink tablet combines both: the screen doesn't glow, there's no browser or notifications, so you focus purely on writing — and everything you write gets saved automatically and stays searchable even a year later.

## How to do it

1. Write by hand with a stylus directly on the display, the same way you'd write in a paper notebook — the screen's response and the pen's surface are designed to feel as close to real paper as possible, including a slight resistance as you write.
2. Turn on **automatic sync** with the device's cloud service — your handwriting gets digitized in the background, meaning it becomes searchable as text, and it syncs across every device linked to your account.
3. Once a week (or on whatever regular interval works for you), go through your accumulated notes and export what matters into your main information system, whether that's Notion, Obsidian, or another tool you use long-term.
4. Most e-ink tablets can also read PDFs and e-books, letting you write notes directly into the text — useful when studying or reading professional materials.
5. E-ink tablet batteries typically last weeks, not hours like a regular tablet — which turns it into a device you just automatically bring with you, without worrying it'll be dead when you actually need it.

## The best tools

- **reMarkable 2** — a slim e-ink tablet focused purely on writing and reading, with no browser or apps to distract you.
- **Kindle Scribe** — Amazon's equivalent, focused on reading e-books and taking notes directly in them, great for readers.
- **Boox** (tablet lineup) — e-ink devices with a more open system that allows installing some apps, for users who want more flexibility than a purely “paper” device.
- **A digital note archiving tool** (Notion, Obsidian, or similar) — the destination where you regularly export what matters from your e-ink tablet for long-term storage.

## What you get out of it

- **Focus:** the absence of notifications, a browser, and social networks means there's nothing to distract you while writing.
- **Less eye strain:** an e-ink display doesn't glow the way a regular screen does, which is gentler on your eyes during longer reading or writing sessions.
- **Notes that don't get lost:** unlike a paper notebook, everything gets digitized and stays searchable even months later.
- **Smoother thinking:** according to many people, writing by hand supports deeper memory and thinking through a topic better than fast tapping on a keyboard.

## Pro tip

Build templates for recurring note formats — meeting notes, a weekly plan, a brainstorm for a new idea. Most e-ink tablets can load templates as a page background, so you don't have to redraw the same layout every time and immediately know where everything belongs. Try the tablet out on less important notes first before bringing it into, say, client meetings — it takes a while to get used to writing with a stylus and find the pace that works for you.
