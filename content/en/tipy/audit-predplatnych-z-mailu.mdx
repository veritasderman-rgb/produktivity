Subscriptions have one sneaky trait: each one individually is "small," but nobody can name every single one they're paying for off the top of their head. Streaming, cloud storage, phone apps, a tool you needed for one project last March… You don't actually have to reconstruct the full list from memory — it already exists, scattered across three places: your email, your phone's system subscriptions, and your bank statement. Ten minutes of searching puts it back together.

## How to do it

1. In your email, search in turn for: `payment confirmation`, `subscription`, `receipt`, `renewal`, `invoice`. Write every service that shows up onto a simple list — a note on your phone is fine.
2. Open the subscriptions managed through app stores: on iPhone, **Settings → your name → Subscriptions**; on Android, **Play Store → profile picture → Payments & subscriptions → Subscriptions**. This is where apps your email never mentions hide.
3. Go through the last two months of transactions in your banking app and note recurring charges not already on the list (typically services billed directly to your card).
4. For each item on the list, answer one question: **did I use this in the last month?** No → cancel right now, while the screen is still open. Hesitation counts as no — if you're missing the service, you can always subscribe again.
5. For services that survive, consider switching from monthly to annual billing — it's usually noticeably cheaper, but only makes sense for the ones the audit confirms you actually use. And check whether you're paying for two services doing the same thing (two cloud drives, two music libraries), or whether a family plan would cover something on the list.

## A typical scenario

A freelancer makes the audit the first item in her Friday admin block. Result after ten minutes: eleven subscriptions, two of which she no longer uses (a tool for a one-off project, a course from last winter), one she's paying for twice with a colleague even though the service has a team plan, and three she uses daily — those she switches to annual billing. Three cancellations, one merge, fifteen minutes of work.

## What you get out of it

A one-time cleanup of payments quietly draining your card for nothing. Long term, you build a habit: an audit repeated twice a year (put it on your calendar) keeps subscriptions in line with what you actually use. To make sure nothing slips through between audits, pair it with the tip [turn on a notification for every card payment](/tipy/upozorneni-na-kazdou-platbu).

## Pro tip

Don't forget the fourth hiding place for subscriptions: PayPal and similar payment services. In the account settings you'll find a section for automatic or recurring payments — and it sometimes holds a subscription that only shows up in your bank statement as an anonymous charge from a payment gateway. You can cancel it right there, without hunting down the account with the service itself.
