You're sharing your screen at a meeting and a notification pops into the corner — a private message, an email subject line, a reminder from the doctor's office. The whole room reads it along with you. Manually turning on Do Not Disturb before every meeting is a solution you only ever remember after the embarrassment already happened. But Windows can watch out for you: Do Not Disturb can turn itself on automatically in exactly the situations where notifications have no business on screen — and turn itself back off once they're over.

## How to do it

1. Open **Settings → System → Notifications** and expand the **Automatically turn on do not disturb** section.
2. Check the situations that apply to you: **when duplicating my display** (that is, when projecting or screen-sharing), **when playing a game**, **when using an app in full-screen mode**, and **during these times** — say, every day from 6 PM to 8 AM.
3. In the **Set priority notifications** section, define the exceptions that get through even with the mode on: alarms and reminders, calls, or specific apps — say, a company chat you can't afford to miss.
4. You can toggle the mode manually at any time from the notification center: click the clock on the taskbar (or press <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>N</kbd>) and flip the bell switch at the top.
5. Missed notifications don't disappear — once the mode turns off, they're waiting for you in the notification center, where you can go through them calmly and all at once.

## A typical scenario

A salesperson is presenting a proposal to a client. In the past, right as he was scrolling through the pricing, notifications from the internal chat would fly across the screen — including one with a sentence the client definitely shouldn't have seen. Now he has the “while duplicating my display” rule turned on: the moment he connects a projector or shares his screen in Teams, notifications go quiet on their own. No ritual, no relying on memory.

The evening rule solves a bit of psychology too: after six in the evening, the laptop simply doesn't beep. Whoever needs to reach him will call — everything else waits until morning.

## What you get out of it

- **No more sharing embarrassments:** private notifications in front of an audience stop being a risk, because the protection doesn't depend on your memory.
- **Focus on a schedule:** set hours make quiet the default state, not an exception you have to switch on every time.
- **Nothing slips through:** priority exceptions let through what matters, and the notification center holds the rest for later.

## Pro tip

For focused work, Windows 11 has an even stronger option: [focus sessions](/tipy/win-focus-sessions) turn on do-not-disturb for a set work block and also measure how much you got done undisturbed. Automatic rules and focus sessions complement each other — one protects situations, the other protects your time.
