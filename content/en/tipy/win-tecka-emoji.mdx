An em dash, an arrow, a degree symbol — you don't need to copy them from Google. Windows has a built-in panel with all the common and less common characters your keyboard doesn't offer on its own, and it opens with a single press anywhere you're typing.

## A typical scenario

You're writing and need a proper em dash "—" instead of a plain hyphen, or a degree sign "°" for a temperature, or an arrow "→" for a diagram. The usual fix is to open a browser, google "degree symbol," copy it, and switch back to the document — an unnecessary detour for one character.

With <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>.</kbd> (period), the character panel opens right inside the text field you're typing in. Use its search to find the character you need in seconds and insert it with a click, without leaving your document.

## How to do it

1. In any text field (document, email, chat, browser), press <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>.</kbd> (period) — <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>;</kbd> works the same way too.
2. A panel opens with several tabs. The default tab offers emoji sorted into categories, with a search box at the top.
3. Switch to the symbols tab (marked Ω) for em dashes, arrows, math symbols, and other special characters not commonly found on a keyboard.
4. Clicking a character or emoji inserts it directly wherever your cursor was.
5. The panel remembers your most recently used characters in the top bar, so using the same character again is a matter of seconds.

## The best tools

- **The built-in Win + period panel** — free, works in any app with a text field, the fastest way to special characters.
- **Character Map** — an older built-in Windows tool for finding a character by its Unicode code, useful for really rare characters.
- **AutoHotkey** — for power users who want custom shortcuts for specific characters or strings they use very often.
- **Alt code shortcuts** — an alternative without the panel, where a character is inserted directly by typing its numeric code with the <kbd>Alt</kbd> key.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** roughly 1–2 minutes a day saved on copying characters from the internet, which adds up to several hours a year if you write regularly.
- **Consistent typography:** a proper em dash and other correct characters instead of workarounds (two hyphens instead of one dash) make your text look more professional.
- **Less distraction:** you don't leave your document to search in a browser, so you don't lose your train of thought.
- **A broader character vocabulary:** the panel also includes kaomoji and symbols you might not otherwise know or find.

## Pro tip

If you use the same character or emoji often, keep an eye on the panel's top bar of recently used characters — after a few days of regular use, you'll find exactly what you need there without browsing categories or searching again.
