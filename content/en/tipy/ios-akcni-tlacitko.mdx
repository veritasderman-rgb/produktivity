Starting with the iPhone 15 Pro (and on every iPhone 16 and newer), the classic ring/silent switch was replaced by what's called the Action Button — and plenty of owners still only use it for that mute toggle, simply because that's how it came out of the box. In reality it's a full-featured physical shortcut: a press-and-hold triggers any action you want, from a voice memo to a custom shortcut, and it works even with the screen locked and your phone still in your pocket.

## How to do it

1. Open Settings → **Action Button** — the settings appear as a gallery you swipe through sideways.
2. Choose what a press-and-hold should do:
   - **Voice Memo** — starts recording instantly; the fastest way to capture a thought or a note from a meeting.
   - **Camera** — from pocket to viewfinder without unlocking.
   - **Flashlight**, **Silent Mode**, a specific **Focus mode**, **Translate**, **Magnifier**, or **song recognition**.
   - **Shortcut** — the most powerful option: the button triggers anything from the Shortcuts app, including a menu with multiple actions.
3. If you pick something other than silent mode, you can still control ringer silencing from Control Center — you don't lose that function.
4. For power users: build a menu-style shortcut in the Shortcuts app ("What do you want to do?" → new task, recording, document scan) and assign it to the button — one button, a whole manual index of actions.

## A typical scenario

You're walking out of a meeting carrying three tasks in your head that you can't afford to lose on the way to the elevator. It used to mean: unlock your phone, find the app, tap it out. Now you press and hold the Action Button, a voice memo starts (or straight into a "new task by voice" shortcut), and you dictate it while walking. You never even had to unlock your phone — that's exactly the magic of a physical button over an icon on the home screen.

Then have the recording [transcribed and cleaned up by AI](/tipy/ai-diktat-ucesat) and the stream of sentences becomes a task list.

## What you get out of it

One action you perform many times a day moves off the screen and onto hardware: no unlocking, no searching, no looking at the phone at all. It pays off most for capture — notes and ideas have a short shelf life, and every second between the thought and recording it means a risk of losing it. And if you have an older iPhone without an Action Button, [back tap](/tipy/ios-poklep-na-zada) does a similar job — less reliable, but available to everyone.

## Pro tip

Pick the action based on what you actually do most, not what sounds cleverest. A good test: for a week, notice which action on your phone you start most often manually from the home screen — and put that one on the button. For a lot of people, it ends up being the plain camera or flashlight; for people who live in their notes, it's the voice memo. There's no wrong choice that can't be fixed — changing the setting takes ten seconds.
