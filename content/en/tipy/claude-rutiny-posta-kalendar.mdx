Most people stay on the first rung of AI use: open the chat, ask something, close the window. The real leap happens once AI stops waiting for your question. Claude can run a task repeatedly on a schedule — and when it has connector access to your mail and calendar, it keeps working even when nobody's at the keyboard. **Describe the rules once, in plain language, and from then on finished drafts are waiting in the morning and a summary of what you promised whom is waiting in the evening.** It isn't another tool to learn. It's an agreement the machine keeps every single day.

The general idea behind routines is covered in [the drafts routine](/tipy/rutina-ai-drafty-mailu) and [the evening summary](/tipy/rutina-vecerni-shrnuti-slibu); the full ladder from chat to agent is in [the AI chapter](/prirucka/ai-a-automatizace). This tip is about how to build it specifically in Claude.

## A typical scenario

Hana, a project manager, gets into the office at 8:15. By then about thirty emails have piled up — roughly ten need a reply, and four ask about a date she'd have to check against her calendar. Mornings eat up an hour on mail alone, even though she isn't coming up with anything new — she's just retyping what she already wrote last week.

After setting up two routines, her day looks different. At 6:30, Claude goes through the new messages, separates newsletters from things that actually need something, and drafts a reply for nine of the emails — four of them with a date it already checked against her calendar. Hana opens the Drafts folder at 8:15, sends six as-is, tweaks two, deletes one. Done in 25 minutes. At 17:30 the second output arrives: what she promised whom today, what she's waiting on, and four suggested to-dos flagged “needs approval.” Two minutes of clicking and she heads home with a clear head.

## How to do it

1. **Connect the connectors.** In claude.ai's connector settings, turn on Gmail and Google Calendar. Sign-in runs through your own account, only you grant the permissions, and you can revoke them at any time — a connector isn't a permanent concession, it's a borrowed key.
2. **Write down the rules and save them in a project.** A Claude project holds persistent context that every conversation and every routine can see. Write one page: how you sign off, what tone you use with clients versus colleagues, what shouldn't get a reply at all, which time slots you're allowed to offer, and when your afternoon focus block is off-limits.
3. **Set up the morning task.** Describe it in sentences: “Every weekday at 6:30, go through messages from the last 24 hours. Sort them into needs a reply, FYI only, and ignore. For the first group, draft a reply following the rules in the project. If an email asks about a date, check my calendar availability and offer two concrete times. Save everything as a draft — never send.”
4. **Set up the evening task.** A second routine at 17:30: a summary of today's mail, a list of promises — yours and other people's — and a set of suggested to-dos. Add the line “propose to-dos as a list awaiting approval, don't create them as done.”
5. **Limit the scope and mind your data.** Start with one label or one folder, not the whole inbox. Sensitive matters (HR, health information, finances, contracts) don't belong in the routine — and if you do work with them, do it only on a paid or business account with contractual data protection, never in a free chat, and anonymize whatever you can.
6. **Tune it over a week.** Every correction you make to a draft, add as a sentence to the rules in the project. After five days, accuracy is in a completely different place than it was on the first morning.

## The best tools

- **Claude with connectors (MCP)** — links to Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Notion, and hundreds of other services; the foundation of the whole routine.
- **Projects in Claude** — persistent context with your rules and sample replies that the routine draws on every single time.
- **Scheduled tasks in Claude** — runs on a schedule; you find the result finished without having to be there.
- **Zapier, Make, or n8n** — an alternative when you want to assemble the routine step by step yourself, or run it on your own server.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** a conservative 40–50 minutes a day. Reading and approving a draft takes a fraction of writing one from scratch.
- **Peace of mind:** mornings don't start with a blank page, and evenings don't end with the nagging feeling you forgot something.
- **Quality:** replies keep the same tone and the same conditions, because they all come from one set of rules.
- **Control:** you see exactly what the routine did — drafts sit in your mail, tasks wait for approval.

## Pro tip

Hold to one hard line: **Claude may prepare anything; only a human sends it.** Write an explicit no-sending rule into the routine's instructions and never lift it, no matter how tempting. Once a quarter, review the list of connected connectors and remove any you haven't used — permissions have a way of quietly piling up, and the best data protection is the kind you never had to grant in the first place.
