The average American spends well over three hours a day on their phone. You don't have to. Most of that time doesn't go toward anything you consciously planned — social apps open “for a second” and that second regularly stretches into twenty minutes, because the apps are designed exactly to keep you there. An app limit doesn't add willpower — it adds friction, and that's enough.

## A typical scenario

Tereza noticed she was opening Instagram dozens of times a day without even realizing it — it had become almost a reflex while waiting for the bus, standing in line, even in the middle of work. She consciously wanted to scroll less, but the decision to “just not open it” failed every time, because opening the app never required a decision — her thumb just did it on its own.

After setting a daily 30-minute limit on the social media category, nothing changed about whether she opens the app — but once the limit runs out, a lock screen appears. That moment, when she has to actively tap “ignore limit” to keep going, is exactly the second she becomes aware of what she's doing. Most of the time, she just puts the phone down instead.

## How to do it

1. **iOS:** Settings → Screen Time → App Limits. Choose a category or a specific app and set a daily time limit.
2. **Android:** Digital Wellbeing → App timers — same idea, just a different spot in settings.
3. Set the limit on the Social category as a whole, not just one app — otherwise your brain will simply find its way to another app in the same group.
4. Once the limit runs out, the icons lock and opening the app requires an extra step (confirming you want to ignore the limit). That extra step is the entire point of the feature.
5. Try starting with a more generous limit (say, an hour) and lowering it gradually — jumping straight to 30 minutes after months of unlimited scrolling tends to be frustrating and easier to work around.
6. You can also set a limit for only certain hours of the day (say, working hours), if evening scrolling doesn't bother you but daytime scrolling does.

## The best tools

- **Built-in iOS and Android limits** — free, nothing to install, good enough for most people to start with.
- **One Sec** — adds a short mindful pause (a breathing exercise) before a chosen app opens, going beyond a simple time limit.
- **Opal** — more sophisticated blocking with scheduled blocks and stats, popular with students during intense study periods.
- **Freedom** — works across phone and computer, useful if you want the same rules on both devices at once.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** roughly an hour a day for people who used to spend significantly more on social apps — several extra hours a week for anything else.
- **Attention:** less mindless reaching for the phone means longer uninterrupted stretches of focus on work or study.
- **Peace of mind:** less comparing your life to other people's on social media, less endless aimless scrolling.
- **Sleep:** a limit set for the evening too curbs the typical “just a little longer” right before bed.

## Pro tip

The limit is easy to bypass with an “ignore” button — but that's not really the point. The point is creating one second of conscious decision where there used to be only mindless reaching for the phone. Even if you skip past the limit sometimes, that one second of awareness still does its job.
