"I know it's in here somewhere." Turning off autocorrect in Word, changing the default browser, blocking an app's microphone access, finding where Teams hid the status settings. The settings menus in modern apps are five-story labyrinths, and searching them is a special kind of micro-torture: you know exactly what you want, just not where it is. AI works like a universal manual for every app at once — and unlike a googled tutorial, it can adapt to what you're actually looking at on screen.

## How to do it

1. Be specific, and always name the app, version, and system — paths differ between versions, and that's the most common reason for a wrong answer:

```text
Where in [app, version, system — e.g. Outlook on Windows 11,
the Microsoft 365 version] do I turn off [what — e.g. sound
for incoming mail]?

Walk me through the exact path: menu → item → tab → option,
one step per line. If it moved in recent versions, describe
the older location too.
```

2. When the described path doesn't match what you're seeing, don't argue with reality — describe what's actually on your screen: "I don't have a Mail tab, I only see General and Accounts." The model will reorient and guide you from a different starting point.
3. For system settings, the reverse question works too: "What can I break in this menu? I just want to turn off the sound, nothing else touched."
4. If the second attempt still doesn't match, the answer is probably for a different version — ask for the path through the built-in settings search that nearly every app has today.

## A typical scenario

Teacher Marie needs to project a slideshow before class in a way that doesn't pop up notifications on screen. She knows Windows has some do-not-disturb mode, but where? She used to click through Settings for ten minutes, or give up and hope nobody's phone buzzed.

She types into a chat on her phone: "Windows 11, I want to turn off all notifications for one class period, where do I find that?" She gets a three-step path to Focus Assist, plus a note that it turns on automatically while projecting if she checks one extra option. Since then she's solved everything the same way — from sharing a calendar to setting up parental controls on the kids' tablet.

## What you get out of it

The whole category of small frustrations — "I know what, I don't know where" — disappears, and it eats up more time and confidence than it should: plenty of useful features stay switched off simply because nobody found them. The trick is naming the version and describing what you actually see when things don't line up: AI proposes the path, but you're the one with eyes on the screen. And while you're at it, have it explain what each option does — a manual that answers follow-up questions was never something paper could do.

## Pro tip

When you get completely lost in a settings menu, stop describing and send a screenshot with the question "where do I find…" The model sees the same thing you do and guides you based on the real state of your screen, not on whatever version it remembers from documentation.
