An unfamiliar word doesn't have to mean a trip to the browser. macOS has a built-in dictionary, thesaurus, and quick Wikipedia preview, all of which can be summoned right over a word — in any app where you can select text, from PDFs to Mail to Notes.

## A typical scenario

A student is reading an academic PDF and runs into an unfamiliar word. The usual route is to open a new browser tab, retype or copy the word, search for it, wait through results full of ads and links — and then come back to the text, often having lost their place.

With the built-in preview, all it takes is hovering the cursor over the word and tapping the trackpad with three fingers. A definition, and possibly a translation, appears right above the text in a small window — the student is reading again within three seconds, with no window switching and no lost focus.

## How to do it

1. Hover your cursor over the word whose meaning you want to check — it works in text, mail, a PDF, or a web page.
2. Tap **three fingers** on the trackpad, or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd>.
3. A small window pops up with tabs: a dictionary definition, a thesaurus with synonyms, and a Wikipedia preview if it's a proper noun or a known term.
4. Close the window by clicking outside it or pressing <kbd>Esc</kbd> — the text underneath stays exactly where it was, no app switching involved.
5. If you have additional language dictionaries installed on your system (**Dictionary → Preferences**), the preview window switches between them automatically based on the language of the text, or you can pick one manually.
6. The preview also works on a trackpad without a physical click — and in the Dictionary app, you can browse word pronunciations too, if you're curious how a given word is actually said.

## The best tools

- **The built-in dictionary preview** (three-finger tap or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd>) — free, works offline, ideal for a quick meaning check.
- **The Dictionary app** (Spotlight → "Dictionary") — the same entries in a standalone window, handy for a longer lookup or browsing related terms.
- **DeepL or Google Translate** — for full sentences and longer passages, where it's not just about one word but understanding the context.
- **Dictionary extensions in the browser** — for users who mostly read on the web and want the same preview even where the system shortcut doesn't work reliably.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** no switching to a browser and back — with a text full of unfamiliar words, that adds up to several minutes a week.
- **Uninterrupted reading:** your attention stays on the text, not broken up by opening new windows and tabs.
- **Broader context:** besides a definition, you also see synonyms and a short Wikipedia preview, which helps more than a bare definition for technical terms or proper nouns.
- **Better comprehension:** checking an unfamiliar word quickly, right on the spot, helps a student keep the thread of what they're reading, instead of having to reorient themselves after a browser detour.

## Pro tip

It also works on foreign-language words mixed into English text — if you have a bilingual dictionary installed in System Settings, the preview offers a translation right away, so you don't have to switch between a dictionary and a translator app. You can add or remove language dictionaries anytime in System Settings, depending on which languages you work with most.
