Web apps you spend your whole day in — mail, calendar, chat, a task tracker — don't have to live as tabs among thirty others. Chrome and Edge can “install” any website as a standalone app: it gets its own window with no address bar, its own taskbar icon, and shows up in Alt+Tab switching like a full-fledged program. The site remains a site, it just behaves outwardly like a real application — no store install, no download.

## How to do it

1. Open a site you use every day — typically webmail, a calendar, a task tracker, or a company chat.
2. **In Chrome:** the three-dot menu → **Save and share → Install page as app**. On sites set up for it, the same option also appears as a small icon right in the address bar.
3. **In Edge:** the three-dot menu → **Apps → Install this site as an app**.
4. The site opens in its own clean window. Pin the icon to your taskbar (right-click the icon → Pin) — from then on you launch the app with one click, like Word or Excel. Sign-in and settings are shared with the browser, so you don't start from zero.
5. Uninstalling is just as easy: open the app's menu inside its window and choose uninstall. Firefox doesn't have this feature on desktop — there, [pinned tabs](/tipy/pripnute-panely) are the closest substitute.

## A typical scenario

A manager has his calendar open as the seventeenth tab from the left. When he needs it, he hunts for it in the tab bar; twice a day he closes it by accident while tidying up tabs; and when the browser crashes, the calendar disappears with it. After installing it as an app, the calendar gets its own icon next to mail, switches via Alt+Tab, and the browser window itself can be closed entirely — the calendar keeps running in its own world.

There's a psychological difference too: open a task tracker as an app, and you see the task tracker. Open it as a tab, and you see all twenty other tabs clamoring for attention too — and the path to “I'll just check messages real quick” is one click shorter than it should be.

## What you get out of it

Your most-used tools get a one-click launch and a spot in Alt+Tab, where they belong. They stop getting lost among tabs, can't be closed by accident during cleanup — and work inside them gets noticeably more focused, because the rest of the browser isn't flickering around it.

## Pro tip

Installed apps work great combined with split-screen: calendar as an app on the right, mail on the left, no extra browser chrome. The windows behave like any others, so you can arrange them with the [Win+arrows](/tipy/win-sipky-okna-vedle-sebe) shortcut.
