Describing a format in words is hard. Showing it is easy. When you write "make it concise and professional," AI pictures concise and professional differently than you do. When you paste one finished example instead and say "like this," there's no room for misunderstanding — AI can see exactly the length, tone, and structure you want.

## A typical scenario

Tereza, a marketer, writes a weekly summary for leadership. When she just tells AI "write me a summary of the week," she gets a generic text she has to rewrite from scratch — different headings, different paragraph lengths, missing the numbers table leadership expects. Fixing the format takes almost as long as writing the summary herself.

When she instead pastes an older, approved summary and writes "write this week's summary in the same structure," she gets a text with the right headings, the right paragraph length, and the table in the right place from the start. All that's left is filling in this week's numbers.

## How to do it

1. Find one finished output that has exactly the format you want — an old meeting note, an email, a report, a product description, a review.
2. Paste it into the conversation with AI and write: "Here's the notes from last week's meeting. Write today's meeting notes from this transcript in the same structure and tone."
3. Add the new input (transcript, numbers, notes) — AI will fit it into the pattern instead of inventing its own.
4. If the result still feels slightly off, name the specific deviation: "shorter headings," "no bullet points in the intro."
5. Two or three examples work even better than one — AI can tell what's constant across the document (structure, tone) from what varies (the actual content), and it stops accidentally copying details that should change.
6. If the example contains sensitive information (client names, contract numbers, internal data), anonymize it before pasting it in — replace it with made-up values that keep the same format.

## The best tools

- **Whatever AI chat you already use** — ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini, the principle works the same in all of them, no special tool needed.
- **Your own library of examples** — a folder or note (Notion, Google Docs) with two or three proven examples for each type of document, ready to copy and paste anytime.
- **Saved conversations / projects** — in ChatGPT (Projects) or Claude (Projects) you can upload an example once and AI will know it in every new conversation within that project.
- **Custom instructions / system prompt** — if you always use the same example (say, a company report template), save it directly in the settings so you never have to paste it again.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** you skip the "write it — reformat it — write it again" loop, which for recurring documents easily eats up 10 minutes or more a day.
- **Consistency:** every output (meeting notes, reports, descriptions) reads like it came from one author, even though the request was worded slightly differently each time.
- **Less frustration:** instead of repeated "no, I meant it differently," you get something usable on the first try.

## Pro tip

Mark the example right in the text with a note like "this is the reference format" and keep it in one fixed place. Next time you need something similar, you're not hunting for an old conversation — you have the example on hand and just paste it in. It's worth keeping several such examples for different situations — one for a formal email, another for an informal message to a colleague, another for a product description — because format and tone shift with the situation, even when it's technically the same type of text.
