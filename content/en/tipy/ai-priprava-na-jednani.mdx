You don't have to walk into an important conversation without practice. A price negotiation, feedback for a direct report, or a job interview — you can rehearse all of it beforehand, with AI as the other side, playing a realistic reaction and not going easy on the uncomfortable questions — exactly the ones you're most afraid of.

## A typical scenario

A manager has to tell an employee next week that his project didn't pan out and he needs to move to a different team — a conversation she's been putting off all week because she doesn't know how to handle the reaction. Without this tip, she walks in with only a vague plan, and in the real conversation gets caught off guard by a question she has no answer ready for, and the conversation slides into defensiveness instead of a productive discussion. With this tip, the day before, she runs the scenario with AI, which plays the unhappy employee and asks exactly the uncomfortable questions she was afraid of — and walks into the real conversation with rehearsed answers, calmer and less thrown off.

## How to do it

1. Describe the situation to AI as specifically as possible: “Play an unhappy client I have to tell about a one-month delay” or “play an employee I need to give feedback to about weak performance.”
2. Carry the conversation as if it were the real thing — respond naturally, don't cut corners.
3. Ask AI to react realistically and push back with tough, counter-arguing questions, rather than making it easy on you.
4. When the conversation is over, ask for an evaluation: “Assess my answers and suggest better phrasing.”
5. Repeat the hardest part as many times as you need, until the phrasing feels natural.
6. Before the real conversation happens, run through the key points of your response in your head one more time — the exercise works best when you don't let it go cold, so do it as close to the actual meeting as possible.

## The best tools

Choose the format that most closely matches how the real conversation will happen — written or spoken.

- **Text chat** (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) with the other side's role set in the prompt — a built-in capability, just describe the scenario.
- **An AI app's voice mode** — for practice where tone of voice and response speed matter too, not just the words.
- **A recording of an actual practice conversation with a colleague, as a supplement** — AI is a good first test, but a real person catches things text can't, like body language or tone.
- **Your own notes from previous similar conversations** — if you keep a record, you'll increasingly know which questions tend to come up for you.

Whichever method you choose, the important thing is to let AI actually be difficult, not just nod along.

## What you get out of it

- **Confidence:** you walk into the conversation with rehearsed answers, not a blank mind.
- **Sanity:** you've already heard and handled the worst possible questions once — the real conversation catches you off guard less.
- **Better phrasing:** AI's feedback shows you where your answers sound too defensive or unclear.
- **Time:** prep takes ten minutes instead of hours of worrying about what the worst-case question might be.

Over time you'll notice which types of questions you're most sensitive to, and next time you can aim the scenario right at them.

## Pro tip

The exercise works best when you repeat it with a different setting for the other side — once milder, once more aggressive — so you're ready for a wider range of how the real thing might go. When describing the scenario, don't use the real names of colleagues or clients — a general role is enough — it protects their privacy and the scenario stays just as useful.
