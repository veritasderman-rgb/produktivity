Dragging on a trackpad is the most uncomfortable gesture on the whole Mac: you have to press down on the trackpad, hold it down, and drag at the same time — for a longer move across the whole screen, your fingers slip and the selection breaks halfway there. Yet macOS has an elegant solution tucked away: three-finger drag. Put three fingers on a window and just move them — no pressing, no holding. The option is buried in Accessibility settings, where a regular user would never think to look, and yet it's one of the best trackpad upgrades there is. Try it once and you won't want to go back.

## How to do it

1. Open **System Settings → Accessibility → Pointer Control**.
2. Click **Trackpad Options…**.
3. Turn on **Use trackpad for dragging** and set the dragging style to **Three Finger Drag**.
4. From then on, you move a window by placing three fingers on its title bar and moving them — the window travels with them. Lift your fingers and the window stays put.
5. The same works for dragging files in Finder, selecting text (three fingers swept across text selects it), and moving sliders in apps.
6. Just note that gestures originally assigned to three fingers (switching Spaces, Mission Control) automatically move to four fingers — it takes a few days to adjust, then it becomes automatic.
7. Classic click-and-drag doesn't stop working, either — both methods run side by side, so you're not risking anything and can go back to the old habit any time.
8. A small thing that helps at first: if your three fingers reach the edge of the trackpad mid-drag, just lift them briefly and set them back down — the drag in progress continues, the selection doesn't drop.

## A typical scenario

A graphic designer on a MacBook spends all day rearranging windows and dragging files from Finder into emails. With classic click-and-hold dragging, her thumb hurts by the afternoon, and she does long moves in two steps because the selection "drops" halfway. After turning on three-finger drag, a window slides over with just a light touch of her fingertips, a file lands in an in-progress message with one smooth motion, and selecting long paragraphs of text stops being an exercise in precision. Grip strength no longer matters — the trackpad doesn't get pressed even once all day. And because it's a system setting, it behaves the same in every app: a graphics editor, spreadsheets, or rearranging tabs in a browser.

## What you get out of it

Less strain on your hand for the most frequent gesture you make on a trackpad, and noticeably more reliable long drags — a three-finger drag doesn't "let go" the way a held-down click does. Anyone who works on a MacBook without a mouse all day will notice the difference in hand comfort within a single afternoon — and selecting text with three fingers quickly becomes as natural as a click.
