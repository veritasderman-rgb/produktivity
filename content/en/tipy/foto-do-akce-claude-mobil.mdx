Every phone's gallery is a graveyard of good intentions: photos of a whiteboard after a meeting, a poster for an event you meant to go to, a restaurant receipt, a handwritten list from a meeting. You photographed it meaning to “transcribe it someday.” You never did. **But a phone with AI isn't a scanner that just saves an image — it's a scanner that processes the content on the spot.** The gap between a photo and a usable output used to be an hour of transcribing. Now it's a single request.

## A typical scenario

Project manager David wraps up a two-hour meeting on a Friday. There are fifteen items on the whiteboard, seven with initials next to them, four with dates, three crossed out. The usual routine: photograph it, spend ten minutes staring at the photo on Monday, then half an hour writing up notes. Sometimes he never writes them up at all, and the tasks just dissolve.

The new routine: still in the conference room, he opens the photo in the Claude app and adds two sentences — pull the tasks off the whiteboard in a who/what/by-when format; ignore anything crossed out; where a deadline is missing, write “no deadline set.” Twenty seconds later he has a list of eleven tasks with owners attached. He fixes two entries where the initials were ambiguous, adds one that was missing. By the time he reaches his car, the notes are done and sent out. Notes that go out within ten minutes of a meeting get read. Notes from Monday afternoon don't interest anyone anymore.

## How to do it

1. **Photograph directly in the app, not into your gallery.** Open the Claude app on your phone, attach or take the photo, and write what should happen with it. The critical moment is while you're standing at the whiteboard or holding the receipt — not that evening at home, once the context has faded.
2. **Say what output you want.** A photo with no instructions is just a photo. The second sentence is what makes the difference: “turn this into a who/what/by-when task list,” “list the items and amounts as table rows,” “pull out the event name, date, time, and location.” The more specific the format, the less work is left for you.
3. **Don't be shy about handwriting.** Claude can read handwritten notes too — a list on paper, margin notes, labels on a whiteboard. Ask it to flag illegible parts as uncertain rather than guess; a single extra sentence in your request is enough.
4. **Route the output to where you'll actually use it.** A receipt belongs in a budget spreadsheet ([a personal budget from a bank statement](/tipy/osobni-rozpocet-z-bankovniho-vypisu)), a poster in your calendar, tasks in your task list. On a phone with calendar or drive connected, it can do that directly; otherwise you copy the finished text over.
5. **Check the numbers and names.** Text recognition is good, not infallible — amounts, phone numbers, and dates are worth eyeballing against the original. The basic rule applies: **AI proposes, the person approves.** Don't let any event, payment, or task get created without your confirmation.
6. **Clean out your gallery.** As a one-off, go through the photos that have been sitting in your phone for months and process them in a batch. Odds are you can delete half of them outright, since they're already outdated — and that's a result too.

## The best tools

- **[Claude](https://claude.ai) on mobile** — reads both printed text and handwriting, and above all turns it into the structure you asked for; works on a photo taken a second ago.
- **Your phone's built-in scanner** — for a cleaner source image: the app crops and straightens the shot, see [scanning with your phone](/tipy/skenujte-telefonem).
- **A note-taking app with sharing** — a destination to save the output to right away, so it doesn't stay stuck in a chat.
- **Voice input** — when you'd rather not type: attach the photo and dictate the instruction, see [voice mode](/tipy/ai-hlasovy-rezim).

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** not minutes saved, but the end of an entire activity. Transcribing from photos disappears from your day.
- **Speed of impact:** notes sent within ten minutes of a meeting carry completely different weight than notes sent three days later.
- **Peace of mind:** your phone stops being a warehouse of unfinished business quietly weighing on you every time you open the gallery.
- **Accuracy:** manually retyping numbers is the most common source of typos. Checking a finished output is faster and more reliable than copying by hand.

## Pro tip

You'll get the most out of a photo nobody ever thinks to take: **the table at the end of a meeting.** Business cards, a notepad, a conference program, a flyer — one shot from above and one request: “list the contacts, dates, and company names in this photo.” And be careful what you photograph: a whiteboard with internal strategy, a receipt with someone's name on it, a document with an ID number on it — all of that is sensitive data. Upload it only to a paid account with contractual data protection, never a free chat, and cover or crop out anything you can before you upload it.
