The layout of your home screen decides what your phone actually is: a tool, or a slot machine. You unlock your phone and the first thing you see is either a social app icon calling out “open me,” or a calendar showing what's on today. That small detail decides dozens of mindless opens a day — and it's one of the few things about your phone that's fully within your control with a single setup.

## A typical scenario

Klara had a standard grid of icons on her home screen — Instagram, TikTok, email, games, all mixed together. She unlocked her phone to check the time, and five minutes later realized she was scrolling Instagram without even remembering tapping into it. The icon was simply the first thing her eyes landed on, and her thumb reacted before she'd consciously decided anything.

After rearranging her home screen, only calendar, tasks, notes, maps, and banking — things she genuinely wanted to use deliberately — stayed on the first screen. Social media and games moved to the last page, reachable only with a deliberate extra tap. Unlocking her phone today shows the shape of her day, not temptation, and opening entertainment apps takes a conscious decision instead of a reflex. Within a few weeks, she noticed she was opening the apps she used to tap automatically noticeably less — not because she'd banned them, but because they weren't the first thing she saw anymore.

## How to do it

1. Put only the tools you use with intention on your home screen: calendar, tasks, notes, maps, your banking app, weather.
2. Move social media, games, and entertainment apps to the last page, or leave them only in the app library (App Library on iOS, the full app list on Android), with no icon on any home screen page.
3. A calendar or tasks widget in the middle of your home screen works better than just a row of icons — the first glance at your phone then shows the shape of your day, not a menu to tap into.
4. If you genuinely need to use a social app (send a message, check an important group), search for it via on-screen search instead of tapping an icon — it adds a small but effective extra step.
5. After a week, assess whether anything on your home screen feels missing or unnecessary, and adjust the layout — it's not a one-time setup for good, but an ongoing tweak based on what you actually use.
6. The same principle works on a computer desktop too — fewer icons and shortcuts at first glance means fewer chances for mindless clicking there as well.

## The best tools

- **Built-in home screen editing (iOS, Android)** — nothing extra to install, just hold your finger on an icon and move or remove it from the page.
- **App Library (iOS) / app drawer (Android)** — a built-in home for apps you don't want on any screen but still occasionally need.
- **Niagara Launcher (Android)** — a minimalist launcher replacing the icon grid with a clean list, good for a more radical simplification of your home screen.
- **Before Launcher (Android)** — a similar idea, where the home screen shows only the time, date, and a handful of chosen shortcuts instead of icons.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** roughly 20–30 minutes a day saved on mindlessly opening apps you didn't actually mean to open.
- **Focus:** after unlocking, your phone shows tasks and plans, not temptation — your mind tunes right into what needs doing.
- **Less impulsive scrolling:** apps that require an extra step to open get opened noticeably less than ones sitting a thumb's reach away.
- **A view of your day:** a calendar or tasks widget on the home screen works as a reminder of priorities, not just decoration.
