Hold down an arrow key, Backspace, or Delete, and after a moment the key starts repeating. How long that moment is, and how fast the repeat then runs, isn't fixed — they're two sliders in your settings, and both Windows and Mac ship with them set very conservatively by default. Most people don't know they exist, so for years they wait for the cursor to “take off” and then watch it crawl lazily along the line. Yet one minute of setup makes moving through text and deleting noticeably snappier — it's one of those changes that makes a computer feel brand new.

## How to do it

1. On Windows, press <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>R</kbd>, type `control keyboard`, and confirm — the **Keyboard Properties** window from Control Panel opens (this option isn't in the modern Settings app).
2. Drag the **Repeat delay** slider toward **Short** and **Repeat rate** toward **Fast**. Hold down a key in the test field below the sliders and you'll see the difference immediately.
3. On a Mac, open **System Settings → Keyboard** and set **Key Repeat Rate** to the fastest and **Delay Until Repeat** to the shortest value.
4. Try normal work for a few days. If keys start repeating even during ordinary typing, dial the delay back one notch — but feel free to leave the repeat rate at maximum.

## A typical scenario

Accountant Jane is fixing numbers in long forms: she holds down an arrow key to get to the middle of a line, and waits. The cursor sits still for a second, then crawls character by character. With a short delay and fast repeat, she's at the same spot in a fraction of the time — and holding Backspace deletes the wrong entry before she'd even have reached for the mouse. For a programmer or a student writing a paper, the same tiny difference repeats a hundred times a day: moving along lines, deleting, indenting, scrolling through long lists with arrow keys.

## What you get out of it

Each individual speedup is tiny, but these are movements you make constantly while typing — together they add up to minutes a day, and more importantly, that faint feeling of always waiting on the computer disappears. Text under your hands responds instantly, so you handle more from the keyboard instead of reaching for the mouse.

## Pro tip

On a Mac, holding down a letter with diacritics brings up a character picker with accents instead of repeating — that's a different feature, unrelated to the sliders, and doesn't affect arrow keys or deleting.
