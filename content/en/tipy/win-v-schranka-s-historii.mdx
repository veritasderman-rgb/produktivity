How many times a day do you copy something, then copy something else — and the first thing is gone? It doesn't have to be. Windows has a built-in clipboard history that remembers the last several dozen items you've copied — text, links, and images. Most people don't know about it, and yet it's one of the most useful features in the system.

## A typical scenario

You're putting together a proposal for a client. You copy a price from a price list, a paragraph of terms from a previous email, a link to a reference from a website, and an order number from a spreadsheet. Without clipboard history, that means: copy the price → paste → switch windows → copy the paragraph → paste → switch windows... eight round trips, and if you accidentally copy something extra, the original content is gone and you're starting over.

With clipboard history, you go through all your sources **once**, copying everything in sequence — then in your target document you just open the <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>V</kbd> panel and paste item after item. Eight round trips become one.

## How to do it

1. Press <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>V</kbd> instead of the usual <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>V</kbd>.
2. The first time, confirm **Turn on clipboard history** — from that point on, Windows remembers what you copy.
3. A panel opens with everything you've recently copied. Select an item with the arrow keys and paste it with Enter, or click it with the mouse.
4. History holds dozens of items, including images; the oldest ones drop off over time. You can clear it with one button in the panel (handy on a shared computer).
5. In **Settings → System → Clipboard** you can also turn on **sync across devices** — copy something on your work computer, paste it at home on your laptop with the same Microsoft account.

## The best tools

- **Windows' built-in history (Win+V)** — free, no install, covers 90% of needs. Start here.
- **PowerToys Advanced Paste** — a free add-on from Microsoft; it can paste as plain text, JSON, or markdown, which anyone copying from the web into documents will appreciate.
- **Ditto** — a free, open-source clipboard manager for power users: unlimited history, full-text search across everything you've ever copied, and custom keyboard shortcuts.
- **On a Mac:** Raycast or Maccy do a similar job — macOS doesn't have built-in clipboard history of its own.

## What you get out of it

Copying and pasting is probably the single most common action you perform on a computer — a typical office day involves dozens to hundreds of them. Clipboard history saves you:

- **Time:** roughly 3–5 minutes a day on unnecessary window switching and repeated copying — over 20 hours a year.
- **Sanity:** an end to "damn it, I overwrote my clipboard" moments. Nothing gets lost.
- **Errors:** when retyping data (account numbers, tax IDs, codes), every extra copy is a risk of a typo. Pasting from history is always exact.

## Pro tip

Pin the items you use constantly (a tax ID, an account number, a signature, a frequently sent link) with the **pin icon** in the panel — they survive even a restart, and you have them forever with two keystrokes. It works like a mini database of the things you'd otherwise keep hunting for.
