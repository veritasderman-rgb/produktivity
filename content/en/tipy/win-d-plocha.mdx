Fifteen windows open and you need a file from the desktop? Or a coworker walks by and there's something on screen you'd rather they didn't see — a half-written email, a private message, a document that isn't ready to be shared yet? Either way, the fix is exactly the same: one keypress, nothing to close or move by hand.

## A typical scenario

Marek, an accountant, works across three apps at once — a spreadsheet, a mail client, and a browser with ten tabs. He needs to quickly check a file saved on the desktop, but with fifteen windows open, there's no way to get there except minimizing each window one by one, or clicking the tiny button all the way at the right end of the taskbar, which is easy to miss.

With <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd>, every window vanishes at once and a clean desktop appears. Once he's found the file, he presses the same shortcut again and every window comes back exactly where it was — nothing got closed, just hidden for a moment. The whole thing takes less time than moving the mouse to that corner button and hitting it precisely.

## How to do it

1. Press <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd> — every open window minimizes at once and the desktop appears.
2. Press the same shortcut again — every window comes back exactly in the state and layout it was in before.
3. Works regardless of how many windows are open or how many apps are running — everything minimizes at once, even ten or twenty windows.
4. The shortcut doesn't close or save anything — it's purely a hide, so unsaved work in any app stays exactly as you left it.
5. If you work across multiple monitors, windows on every screen minimize at once — the shortcut isn't limited to just the one your cursor happens to be on.

## The best tools

- **Windows' built-in shortcut** (<kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd>) — works instantly, nothing to install or configure.
- **Show Desktop button** — the same feature available via mouse too, a thin strip at the far right of the taskbar next to the clock.
- **Win + M** — an alternative that also minimizes every window, but unlike <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd>, restoring them needs a separate shortcut, <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>M</kbd>, not a repeat press of the same combo.
- **Virtual desktops** (<kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd>) — when instead of temporarily hiding windows you want a genuinely separate, empty desktop with its own set of windows.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** a few seconds saved every time you need to reach the desktop, multiplied by how often that happens during the day.
- **Discretion:** an instant way to clear the screen when a coworker or client walks by and the screen's contents aren't meant to be seen.
- **Clarity:** quick access to desktop files and shortcuts without closing or moving open windows one by one.
- **A safe way back:** unlike closing windows, nothing gets lost — the second press restores everything exactly where it was, including unsaved text or an unfinished form.

## Pro tip

A related shortcut, <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>,</kbd> (comma), only **peeks** at the desktop while you hold the keys down — release them and the windows come right back. Handy for a quick glance at the desktop, like checking the clock or a widget, without actually minimizing anything or having to restore it afterward.
