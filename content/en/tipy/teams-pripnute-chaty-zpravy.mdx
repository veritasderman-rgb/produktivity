The chat list in Teams sorts by most recent activity — which means the conversation with your boss or a key project team slides down every time someone less important pipes up. And inside a chat, information ages just as fast: a link to a shared document or an agreed deadline gets buried under a hundred newer messages within two days. Pinning solves both problems: chats can be pinned to the top of the list, and individual messages can be pinned above the whole conversation, where every participant sees them.

## How to do it

**Pinning a chat (for you only):**

1. In the chat list, **right-click** a conversation (or use the three dots) and choose **Pin**.
2. The chat moves into the **Pinned** section at the very top and stays there regardless of activity elsewhere. You can pin up to 15 chats; drag to reorder them within the section, so the most important conversation really can sit first.

**Pinning a message (for everyone in the chat):**

1. Hover over a message, click **More options → Pin**, and confirm.
2. The message appears at the top of the conversation for every participant — each person reaches it with one click, until someone unpins it.

You'll find the same pin, via right-click, on **channels** in the teams list too — favorite channels move into their own section at the top, so daily work doesn't require expanding the whole team tree.

## A typical scenario

A homeroom teacher juggles three standing conversations in Teams: the staff room, school leadership, and the subject committee. She pins them — and no longer has to fish them out among dozens of student threads that keep pushing to the top. In the committee chat, she also pins a message with a link to the grade spreadsheet and the grading deadline: any colleague can find it in a second from then on, instead of asking “can you send me that link again?” for the third time this week.

## What you get out of it

Important conversations always sit in the same place, so you open them on autopilot instead of scanning the list — the brain gets used to a fixed position the same way it does with a keyboard layout. And a pinned message works like a team bulletin board: the links, deadlines, and rules everyone keeps asking about hang visibly at the top — the number of “send that to me again” questions drops noticeably.

## Pro tip

Everyone in the chat sees a pinned message — which is exactly why you should pin sparingly: a bulletin board with twenty items stops guiding anyone. One to three items, rotated as what's currently live changes, is the ideal count. A proven rhythm: pin a message with goals and links at the start of a sprint or project, unpin it when it wraps up, and replace it with the next one. The board then always says what's true right now — and that's something people can trust.
