Nobody worries about reaching the end of a sheet with a few dozen rows — the scroll wheel is enough. Once a sheet has thousands of rows, scrolling turns into a slow, imprecise hunt for where the data actually ends. Ctrl combined with the arrow keys solves exactly that: one keypress jumps you to the edge of a contiguous block of data, whether the sheet has a hundred rows or a hundred thousand.

## A typical scenario

Tomáš, a warehouse manager, gets an order export with 4,812 rows and needs to check the last entry. Scrolling with the mouse wheel would take tens of seconds and he'd easily miss the exact last filled row — either overshooting into blank space or stopping a bit too early.

With Ctrl+Down Arrow, he just clicks any cell in the data column and presses the combo — the cursor jumps straight to the last filled row of the block. He gets back to the top just as fast, or selects the whole block for copying with Ctrl+Shift+arrow, without dragging the mouse at all.

## How to do it

1. Click any cell inside a contiguous block of data.
2. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>↓</kbd> (or ↑, ←, →) — the cursor jumps to the last filled cell in that direction, wherever the data block ends or runs into an empty cell.
3. Add <kbd>Shift</kbd>, i.e. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>↓</kbd>, and Excel **selects** the whole stretch between the starting and destination cell — ideal right before copying a column or row.
4. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Home</kbd> takes you back to the very start of the sheet (cell A1), and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>End</kbd> jumps to the last cell on the sheet that contains any data or formatting.
5. The combo also works across blocks separated by empty cells — if there's a gap below the current block followed by more data, pressing Ctrl+arrow a second time carries you over the gap to the next block.

## The best tools

- **The built-in Ctrl+arrow shortcut (Excel)** — works instantly in every version, nothing to set up.
- **The Name Box** to the left of the formula bar — to jump to a specific, exactly known cell (say, Z5000), just type the address there and press Enter.
- **Google Sheets** — the same Ctrl+arrow combo works identically, so the habit carries over between apps unchanged.
- **A formatted table (Ctrl+T)** — if your data keeps growing, a table makes orientation even easier, since the headers stay visible while scrolling.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** easily 5 minutes a day on larger sheets that would otherwise go to scrolling and hunting for where the data ends.
- **Accuracy:** no risk of accidentally missing the last few rows because you stopped scrolling too soon.
- **Smoother work:** the combo chains with Shift for selection, so navigating and selecting data becomes one fluid operation instead of two.

## Pro tip

<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>End</kbd> selects everything from the current cell to the last used cell on the sheet — ideal right before copying an entire table, last row included, without needing to know exactly where the table ends.
