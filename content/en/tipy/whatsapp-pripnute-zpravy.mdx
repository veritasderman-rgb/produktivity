A group agrees on a meeting time and place — and by evening it's buried under fifty other messages. The ritual follows: “when was that again?”, someone scrolls, someone reposts it. WhatsApp has two features that put an end to that folklore. **Pinning a chat** keeps selected conversations at the top of the list, no matter what else happens. And **pinning a message** posts a specific message into a bar above the conversation, where every member of the chat can see it — complete with a timer that unpins it automatically.

## How to do it

**Pinning a message in a chat:**

1. Long-press the message (on a computer, the arrow next to the message) and choose **Pin**.
2. Choose a duration: **24 hours, 7 days, or 30 days**. The message shows up in the bar at the top for every participant; tapping the bar takes anyone straight to it.
3. Once the time's up, it unpins itself — the bulletin board doesn't get clogged with old notes.

**Pinning an entire chat:**

1. Long-press a conversation in the chat list and tap the **pin** icon — the chat stays at the top regardless of newer messages elsewhere (you can pin up to three).

**Bonus — starring, just for you:** long-press a message and choose **Star**. A private bookmark you'll find in the chat settings under Starred Messages — ideal for codes, addresses, and arrangements only you need to come back to. Unlike pinning, nobody else sees it and it has no timer, so it stays until you remove it yourself.

## A typical scenario

An organizer pins a message into the class-reunion group with the date, restaurant address, and time — for 30 days. Anyone who asks “where is it again?” from then on gets the answer “top of the bar.” She also stars a message where she promised to bring the projector, so she doesn't forget. And she pins the reunion chat itself with the icon, because she's opening it daily for the next month now.

## What you get out of it

Key information stops competing with the flow of conversation: whatever's pinned can be found in a second, by anyone, without scrolling. Repeated questions drop off — and the timer ensures the bulletin board clears itself after the event, so pinning doesn't degenerate into just another cluttered list. In work groups (shifts, temp staff, a construction site), the same trick works for schedules and contacts: one pinned message saves ten private questions about the same thing.

## Pro tip

The same principle exists in [Teams pinning](/tipy/teams-pripnute-chaty-zpravy) — the habit of “important stays on top, not in the flow” is worth carrying into every chat where deadlines get arranged. And in groups where things get pinned and unpinned constantly, agree that one person manages the board — typically whoever's organizing the event. Otherwise, after a week nobody's sure which pinned version of the meetup actually holds.
