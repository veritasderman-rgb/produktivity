The colors of icons and previews are designed to pull you in. Turn them off. Saturated red notification badges, bright icon colors, vivid photo previews on social media — none of that is an accident. It's the result of deliberate design meant to catch your eye and keep you on your phone as long as possible. Grayscale simply switches that mechanism off, without you having to ban or delete anything.

## A typical scenario

Ondřej noticed he reached for his phone far more often during focus blocks when his home screen had colorful icons and notification previews. Even a passing glance at the display during some other activity — say, when it was lying next to his laptop — was enough for a bright icon to pull him into opening the app “just for a second.” A saturated red badge with a number on an icon acted like a small, constant demand for attention that was hard to ignore.

After switching on grayscale during focus blocks (and switching back to color afterward), he noticed a clear difference: a gray social media icon simply doesn't look as tempting as a colorful one. He didn't lose any functionality — he just made what used to distract him visually unappealing.

## How to do it

1. **iOS:** Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Color Filters → turn it on and select Grayscale.
2. **Android:** Digital Wellbeing → Bedtime mode includes grayscale as an option, or go directly to Accessibility → Color correction → Grayscale, depending on your phone's manufacturer.
3. Add a grayscale toggle to your quick settings (Control Center on iOS, Quick Settings on Android), or set up a triple-click shortcut on the side button (iOS) to switch it on and off instantly.
4. Use grayscale deliberately during focus blocks or study sessions, and switch back to color when using your phone for photos, navigation, or evening downtime — color itself isn't the enemy, just at the wrong moment.
5. Try combining grayscale with a scheduled notification summary — together they make your phone significantly less appealing during work.
6. Don't be put off by an initial “this is annoying” reaction — after a few days of adjustment, a grayscale phone stops feeling distracting while its effect on curbing mindless reaching for it remains.

Your phone's functionality stays exactly the same — photos, maps, and apps all work completely normally, just without the color lure. Whenever you need to see actual colors (say, for taking or editing photos), you simply switch the mode off for a moment and back on right after.

## The best tools

- **iOS Color Filters** — a built-in accessibility feature that works system-wide, including inside every app.
- **Android Grayscale** — a similar built-in feature; its exact location varies by manufacturer (Samsung, Pixel, and others each have slightly different menus).
- **Triple-click side button shortcut (iOS)** — a built-in way to toggle it quickly without digging through settings every time.
- **Automation via Shortcuts (iOS) or Tasker (Android)** — for advanced users who want grayscale mode to switch on automatically by time or location.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** an estimated 20-30 minutes a day saved from impulsively opening apps that simply don't tempt you as much in gray.
- **Focus:** focus blocks with a grayscale display get interrupted less often by casual glances at the phone sitting next to you.
- **Calm:** less visual “noise” from colorful icons and previews throughout the day.
- **Flexibility:** switching back to color is one tap, so you lose none of your phone's functionality — you're just temporarily dialing down its pull.
