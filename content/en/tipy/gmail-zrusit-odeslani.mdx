"Sent" doesn't have to mean "irreversibly gone." Gmail gives you a few seconds to reconsider even after clicking Send — you just need to know it and set a big enough buffer. By default it's only 5 seconds, which is too little time to react; it can be extended up to 30.

## A typical scenario

You're writing a reply to an entire thread, hit Send, and right then notice you forgot to attach the document you're referencing in the email. Or worse: a message with sensitive content accidentally goes to the wrong address, because a similar name from your history got autofilled into the "To" field. Without a buffer to cancel sending, that means hastily typing an apologetic follow-up or hoping the recipient doesn't notice. With a thirty-second buffer set, you click **Undo**, the email goes back to drafts, and you fix the mistake before anyone ever sees it.

## How to do it

1. Open **Settings → See all settings**.
2. In the **General** section, find **Undo Send** and set the time limit — the recommended value is **30 seconds**, the maximum available.
3. Save your changes with the button at the bottom of the page.
4. From then on, a short bar with an **Undo** button appears in the bottom left after every send — clicking it returns the email to drafts so you can edit or delete it.
5. If you miss the bar, once the time limit passes the email really has been sent and can't be pulled back.

## The best tools

- **Undo Send in Gmail** — a built-in feature, set up once, works forever.
- **Outlook — Recall Message** — a similar feature, but only reliably works between mailboxes within the same organization.
- **Scheduled send** — combined with Undo Send, you can schedule an email with a multi-minute delay for even more time to intervene if needed.

## What you get out of it

- **Reputation:** an embarrassing email with a typo in the recipient or a missing attachment never actually gets sent.
- **Peace of mind:** you don't have to nervously double-check an email after sending it — you have a guaranteed few seconds to fix it.
- **Less damage:** for sensitive information (contracts, personal data, internal figures), a few seconds is the difference between "nobody noticed" and "now I have to deal with a leak."

## Pro tip

Set the full thirty-second buffer right away — the default five seconds is barely enough time to even notice your mistake. And if you're sending an important email, hold off on clicking anything else until the **Undo** bar disappears — only then is it truly gone.
