Fixing a typo should take two seconds, not a minute spent figuring out where to even click to edit the message. Slack has a single key for exactly this — learn it once and you'll never need to open a menu again just because a letter's missing. It's one of those small shortcuts that don't look important on their own, but you use them so often that the difference adds up to noticeably smoother communication.

## A typical scenario

A colleague sends a message to a client channel and notices right after sending it that they typed the wrong order number instead of the correct one. Without knowing the shortcut, they hunt with the mouse for where to click — hover over the message, look for the three dots, click through the menu, and only then edit. An unnecessary round trip over a single digit.

With the shortcut, all it takes is pressing the up arrow — the message opens for editing right away, the number gets fixed, and Enter confirms it. The whole thing takes about as long as it would have if the mistake had never happened. The client won't notice a thing — the message in the history looks like it was typed correctly from the start.

## How to do it

1. In the channel or conversation where you were just typing, press <kbd>↑</kbd> — this only works on your most recent sent message in that conversation, not on anyone else's messages or your own older ones from elsewhere.
2. Your last message opens directly in edit mode, exactly as it was sent.
3. Fix the text and press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to save the change, or <kbd>Esc</kbd> if you want to discard the edit and leave the message unchanged.
4. The edited message stays in its place in the conversation history, just with a small label marking that it was edited, so it's clear the content changed after sending.
5. The shortcut only responds to the message you sent as the very last one in that conversation — if someone else has written in the meantime, use the three-dot menu instead to edit an older message of your own.

## The best tools

- **The built-in up-arrow shortcut** — the fastest route, no menu, no clicking.
- **Three-dot menu → Edit message** — the alternative route for when you want to edit an older message, not just the very last one.
- **The Slack mobile app** — a long press on a message offers the same editing option on your phone.
- **A brief pause before sending** — for important messages, it's worth reading them once more before hitting Enter; the shortcut is then just a safety net, not a substitute for attention.

## What you get out of it

- **Speed:** fixing a typo doesn't take any longer than making it did.
- **Less embarrassment:** a misspelled client name or a transposed number gets fixed before anyone notices.
- **A more professional impression:** a quickly corrected mistake looks better than one that lingers in the history and needs a follow-up “correction:” message.
- **Smoother writing:** knowing a small mistake is always one keypress from fixed means you don't have to slow down and double-check every quick message.
- **Fewer interruptions to your flow:** the fix doesn't require a mouse click or hunting through a menu, so it doesn't pull you away from what you were doing for long.

## Pro tip

An edited message gets a small “(edited)” label next to its timestamp — so colleagues can see the content changed after sending, which matters most to watch for in messages with numbers, deadlines, or other facts where transparency counts.
