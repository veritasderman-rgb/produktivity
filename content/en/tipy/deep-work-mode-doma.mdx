The hardest part of deep work is getting into it. Your brain can't easily tell itself “time to focus now” — it needs a clear signal that something is changing. An automated ritual shrinks that transition down to a single click or a single word, instead of eating up the first ten minutes before you even find your rhythm.

## A typical scenario

You sit down at your computer to write an important piece of text or dive into an analysis. Without a ritual, it plays out like this: the light in the room stays the same as it's been all day, your phone keeps buzzing with notifications, some random playlist plays in the background or nothing plays at all, and you spend the first ten minutes just easing into the work. With a **Deep Work** scene triggered by a single command, the lights dim, your phone switches to do-not-disturb, focus music starts, and a timer kicks off — all the signals at once tell your brain “it's work time now,” and getting into focus is noticeably faster.

## How to do it

In whatever smart home app you use — Home Assistant, Apple Shortcuts, or Google Home — create a scene called **Deep Work** that triggers several steps at once with a single run:

1. dims the lights to a color temperature suited for focused work (usually warmer and less harsh than regular daytime lighting),
2. turns on do-not-disturb mode on your phone and computer so notifications stop popping up,
3. starts a background soundscape based on your preference — white noise or a focus playlist,
4. starts a timer, for example in the Pomodoro style, so the focus block has a clearly defined length.

You can trigger the scene with a voice command, by tapping a widget on your phone, or automatically based on time (say, every weekday at 9:00), if you'd rather have the ritual start itself without any action from you.

Alongside it, create a second, opposite scene called **Break**: it brings the lights up fully, plays energetic music, and reminds you to stretch — a clear signal that the focus block has ended and it's time for a break.

## The best tools

- **Apple Shortcuts** — a built-in app in the Apple ecosystem that can trigger scenes with a single tap or by voice through Siri, free for Apple device owners.
- **Google Home** — the equivalent for devices with smart bulbs and speakers from Google, controlled by voice through Google Assistant.
- **Home Assistant** — an open-source platform for more advanced users who want to connect devices from different brands into a single scene without depending on one manufacturer.
- **A physical button or NFC sticker** (used together with the apps above) — triggers the scene with a single touch, no need to reach for your phone.
- **Smart bulbs with adjustable color temperature** — the basic hardware a dimmed work-lighting scene can't do without, available from a range of manufacturers and compatible with the apps above.

## What you get out of it

- **A faster start:** a clear ritual shortens the time your brain needs to shift gears from “anything at all” to focused work.
- **Less distraction:** automatically turning off notifications at the start of a block means nothing interrupts you during the most vulnerable early phase of focus.
- **A clearer boundary between work and rest:** the opposite Break scene gives your body and mind a clear signal that it's time to stop, which also helps guard against overwork.

## Pro tip

The trigger doesn't have to be a voice command the whole room can hear — an NFC sticker stuck to your desk or a physical button next to the keyboard works faster, doesn't require speaking out loud, and doesn't distract others in the room, whether that's an open-plan office or home with family around. Try the scene on one shorter work block first before rolling it out across your whole day — you'll quickly find out whether dimmed or neutral lighting works better for you.
