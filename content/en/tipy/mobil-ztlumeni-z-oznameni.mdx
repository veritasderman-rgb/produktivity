When an app bombards you with notifications, most people either suffer through it or set off on a long trek through settings they end up abandoning anyway. Yet the fastest route runs straight through the notification itself: every notification carries controls for its own source. On iPhone you can silence it for an hour or a day; on Android you can even turn off one specific category — marketing messages from your bank, say, while payment confirmations keep coming through.

## How to do it

**iPhone:**

1. On a notification (on the lock screen or in Notification Center), **swipe left** and tap **Options**.
2. Choose **Mute for 1 Hour** or **Mute for Today** — the app will deliver quietly, with no sound or banner, and return to normal on its own by evening.
3. The same menu also has **Turn Off** to end notifications from that app for good, and **View Settings** for finer control.

**Android:**

1. **Hold your finger on a notification** — quick options appear: switch to **Silent** delivery, or **Turn Off Notifications**.
2. Tap the gear icon or **More Settings** to see that app's **notification categories** (Android calls them channels): a delivery app might split "order status" from "deals and news," a bank might split "account activity" from "offers."
3. Turn off just the annoying categories and leave the useful ones alone — this is a feature iPhone doesn't have, and it's what makes Android the king of notification cleanup.

## A typical scenario

A group chat about a weekend event kicks off Tuesday morning — thirty messages an hour, all inconsequential, but you don't want to leave the group. On iPhone, you swipe left on the first notification, tap Options, Mute for Today. Done — you'll read the chat that evening. Another time, an online store starts sending "today only" discount pings — on Android, you hold the notification, open categories, and turn off "offers and promotions," while package delivery updates keep coming through.

## What you get out of it

Notification cleanup stops being a Sunday-afternoon project and becomes a reflex: you handle every annoying notification the moment it interrupts you, with two taps. Within a few weeks you'll have quietly cleaned up your entire phone this way — only what you actually want to see remains. And temporary muting means you don't have to choose between "everything" and "nothing": you survive a noisy group-chat day with no permanent changes.

## Pro tip

Set yourself a simple rule: any notification that interrupts you and isn't worth it gets muted or turned off on the spot. No "I'll go through settings when I have time." Your phone cleans itself up gradually and effortlessly this way — within a month, you'll be left with notifications worth looking up for, and nothing else.
