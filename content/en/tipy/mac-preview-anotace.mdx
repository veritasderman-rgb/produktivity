"Circle where I'm supposed to click" or "mark the error in the document" — you can do it in Preview in under a minute, without opening any graphics editor at all. Most people only know Preview as a PDF and image viewer and have no idea it can also edit. Preview, which comes preinstalled on every Mac, can draw arrows and boxes, add text, and blur sensitive information — more than enough for the vast majority of everyday PDF and image edits.

## A typical scenario

A coworker asks which button to click in an app. Without annotation tools, you'd have to describe it in words — "the button in the top right, right next to the gear icon" — and she'd probably still spend a while hunting for it.

With Preview's tools, you take a screenshot, circle exactly that button with a red arrow, and send the image — the confusion disappears completely. It works just as well in reverse, when someone sends you a screenshot with an error message and you type directly into the image with the text tool to tell them what to try next.

## How to do it

1. Open the image or PDF in **Preview** — double-click it, or use Open With.
2. In the menu bar, click the marker-pen icon (Show Markup Toolbar) — this opens the panel with annotation tools.
3. The panel offers arrows, rectangles and other shapes, text boxes, a highlighter, and a tool for blurring or blacking out sensitive information (like an ID number or account number on a scanned document).
4. Draw or type whatever you need — you can fine-tune color, line thickness, and text size for each tool in a small dropdown menu next to its icon.
5. Save your changes with <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>S</kbd> — for PDFs, annotations are embedded directly into the file, so anyone who opens the document will see them, even in a different app.
6. The blur tool is usually found in the panel under a target or magnifying-glass icon — select the area, and the sensitive information is irreversibly obscured before you save.

## The best tools

- **The built-in Preview with its Markup panel** — free, no installation, handles drawing, text, and signatures.
- **Screenshot (Cmd+Shift+4) combined with Preview** — for quickly marking a specific spot on screen and annotating it immediately.
- **The Markup panel right from Quick Look** — for images and PDFs, you can open annotation tools straight from a Space-triggered preview, without opening the full app.
- **Skitch or similar third-party tools** — for users who want faster access to their favorite annotation tools or one-click sharing.
- **Built-in annotation tools right inside Mail** — for attachments, you can open the Markup panel directly in a new-message window, without saving the file first.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** explaining something "on the picture" is faster to draw than to describe in words, and the recipient gets it at a glance.
- **Clarity:** visual markup eliminates the misunderstandings that come from describing a position on screen in words.
- **Protection of sensitive data:** the blur tool can hide account numbers or ID numbers before you send a document on.
- **Independence from other software:** for everyday annotations and simple edits, you don't need to install or learn a paid graphics editor.

## Pro tip

A great trick for cropping: use the selection tool to mark just the area of the image you want to keep, then press <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> — the image crops exactly to the selected area, with no pixel-counting or separate editor required.
