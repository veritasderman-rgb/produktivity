A full-screen Print Screen is rarely what you actually want. Most of the time you need just a piece — one error message, one row of a table, one chat window — not the entire desktop with every open window and icon thrown in, which you then have to crop in another program.

## A typical scenario

A colleague asks about an error you're seeing on screen. The classic approach: hit Print Screen, open Paint or another editor, paste the whole image, crop it down to the part you need, save it, and only then attach it to the message. Five steps for one small thing, and by the time you're done, the conversation has already moved on.

With <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>S</kbd>, you press three keys, drag to mark exactly the area you want to capture, and the shot is already sitting in your clipboard — just <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>V</kbd> it into a chat or document. Five steps become two.

## How to do it

1. Press <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>S</kbd>. The screen dims slightly and a toolbar with selection modes appears at the top.
2. Choose the mode you need: **rectangular selection** (drag to mark an area), **freeform** (draw any shape with your mouse), **window** (click once to select an entire app window), or **full screen**.
3. Once you've made your selection, the shot is automatically saved to your clipboard — no need to save it anywhere first, just <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>V</kbd> it wherever you need it (chat, email, document, image editor).
4. A notification briefly appears in the bottom-right corner confirming the clip was captured. Click it to open the snip editor, where you can mark it up further.

## The best tools

- **Windows' built-in screen clip (Win+Shift+S)** — free, no install, covers the vast majority of everyday screenshots.
- **Snipping Tool** — the same feature, also launchable from the Start menu, with the extra option of delayed captures and simple annotations.
- **ShareX** — a free, open-source tool for power users, with automatic upload to a server, annotations, screen GIFs, and custom keyboard shortcuts.
- **Greenshot** — a lightweight free alternative with quick access to an editor and export to multiple formats, popular in IT environments.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** roughly 3–4 minutes a day if you take screenshots regularly for talking with colleagues or documenting bugs — dozens of hours a year.
- **Precision:** you capture exactly what's needed, with no cropping in a second program.
- **Faster communication:** a colleague or client gets a reply within seconds instead of the minutes it takes to edit a screenshot.
- **Fewer extra windows:** no need to launch a separate editor just for one screenshot.

## Pro tip

Clicking the notification after a clip opens the editor, where you can crop the image, annotate it with arrows or highlights, and save it straight to a file — handy when you need to not just paste a screenshot into chat, but also archive it.
