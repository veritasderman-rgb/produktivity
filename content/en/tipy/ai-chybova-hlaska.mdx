The classic move with an error message: type it into a search engine, open five results, find that three are ten-year-old forum posts, one is for a different version, and the last one tells you to reinstall Windows. Chatbots are actually built for this better than a search engine: they understand the message, know the context of your system, can ask follow-up questions — and, crucially, you can just photograph the message or paste a screenshot, even for messages in a language you don't understand at all.

## How to do it

1. Copy the whole message (it's often behind a "details" or "more information" button), or take a screenshot and paste it into the chat — Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini all read images.
2. Add context and this prompt:

```text
Here's an error message. Context: [Windows 11 / macOS,
which app, and what I was doing when it appeared — e.g.
printing from Word]. I'm not a technician.

1. What the message means, in one plain sentence.
2. The most likely cause in my situation.
3. Steps to fix it, starting with the gentlest — for each,
   say exactly what to click where and how I'll know it worked.
4. What I should NOT do, so I don't make it worse (anything
   that could delete data or break something else).

Walk me through it one step at a time, not all at once.
```

3. Do the step, report the result, and continue. That "one step at a time" matters: when a step doesn't work, the model adjusts its diagnosis instead of you blindly working through the whole list.
4. Take point 4 seriously — for steps like deleting folders, editing the registry, or "reset to factory settings," stop and think it over. AI proposes, the human approves; backing up before a bigger intervention always pays off.

## A typical scenario

The parents' printer stops working and an error code in English lights up on the screen. It used to mean an evening phone call and remote troubleshooting in the dark: "and what does it say now?" This time the daughter gave her parents a simpler process: photograph the message with your phone, send it into a chat, and say you're not technicians.

The model reads the code from the photo, explains that the printer is waiting on a stuck job in the queue, and walks them through it: what to click, what should appear, how to confirm it. Ten minutes later it's printing. The phone call still happens — just a happy one this time.

## What you get out of it

Most error messages have a trivial fix — the problem was always that the message is written for technicians and the forum guides are for a different version. With AI you get an explanation for your specific situation, at your own pace. And thanks to point 4, you also learn what the forums leave out: where the line is, past which it's smarter to call a professional. It pairs with the tip [photograph it and it's a task](/tipy/foto-do-akce-claude-mobil) — a phone with AI doubles as a repair technician in your pocket.
