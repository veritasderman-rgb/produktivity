The hardest part of an unpleasant email is starting — the first line of a rejection, a complaint, or bad news can stall writing for half an hour, even when the content itself is clear. So don't start alone: let AI write the first draft, and you just polish it into your own voice.

All it takes is one clear instruction, and AI hands you a skeleton that's much easier to build on than a blank page.

## A typical scenario

A sales rep has to reply to a client asking for a deadline that just isn't feasible, and the email has been sitting open for an hour with a single sentence written, because she doesn't know how to phrase it so it doesn't come across as an excuse. Without this tip, she writes it in fragments between other work, doesn't like the phrasing, rewrites it, and the email finally goes out late. With this tip, she pastes the incoming email into AI along with a clear instruction, gets a finished skeleton of a reply in seconds, and only tweaks two sentences to make it sound like her.

## How to do it

1. Paste the incoming email you're replying to into AI, and clearly state what you want to say: “Decline, thank them, suggest a September date.”
2. Add the tone you want: “brief and friendly,” “formal,” “apologetic, but without overdoing the self-blame.”
3. Have it suggest a subject line too, if you're writing a new email rather than a reply.
4. Always edit the result in your own words — AI provides the skeleton and structure; the voice and final call stay with you.
5. For more sensitive messages, like a complaint or ending a working relationship, read the draft out loud once more — you'll catch phrasing that could land harsher than you intend.
6. If you're replying to a whole thread, paste the earlier messages into AI too, not just the last one — the history often changes how a reply should sound, and what absolutely can't be left out.

## The best tools

- **ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini directly in your browser** — paste the email text, get a draft reply within seconds.
- **An AI assistant built into your email client** (Copilot in Outlook, Gemini in Gmail) — the draft appears right in the window where you're writing the email, no copying between apps.
- **A saved prompt with your typical request** (see the prompt library) — for recurring types of replies, like complaints or turning down a proposal.
- **Text snippets for the most common situations** — once AI has helped you dial in a reply for a typical case, save it right away as a snippet for next time.

Over time you'll find AI helps you most with that hardest first sentence — the rest of the email tends to flow easily after that.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** fifteen minutes of wordsmithing shrinks to a minute of editing a finished draft.
- **Sanity:** no more staring at a blank window with a blinking cursor over an unpleasant message.
- **Consistent tone:** the same instructions produce similarly styled replies across all your emails.
- **Faster response times:** you reply to emails before they pile up and become even more unpleasant.

Even a short, two-line email is worth letting AI draft, if it would otherwise hold you up longer than it should.

## Pro tip

Save a prompt that fits your style and reuse it — a consistent instruction gives you a consistent tone. If you're forwarding someone else's email into AI, consider stripping out third-party personal details first, like phone numbers, addresses, or contract numbers, that aren't needed to draft the reply.
