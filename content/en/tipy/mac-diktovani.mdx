You can dictate a first rough draft of an email three times faster than you can type it — speaking is naturally faster than typing, and your brain slows down less while doing it because it's not hunting for exact words the way typing forces you to. Dictation on macOS handles languages other than English surprisingly well too, including punctuation on voice command.

## A typical scenario

You need to write a longer reply to a colleague, but you're stuck — staring at a blank page, trying to figure out how to start the sentence. Typing only slows down the process of sorting your thoughts, because you're focused on phrasing instead of content. Double-tap the shortcut to start dictation and just talk through what you want to say — the text appears as you speak, and even with imperfect punctuation you have a rough draft ready in a fraction of the time typing would take.

## How to do it

1. Go to **System Settings → Keyboard → Dictation**, turn it on, and choose a shortcut to trigger it — the default is usually double-pressing the <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> key, or a dedicated microphone icon key, or <kbd>fn</kbd>.
2. Click into any text field where you want the text inserted — it works in email, notes, documents, and web forms, anywhere a text cursor is blinking.
3. Press the shortcut and start speaking at a natural pace.
4. Dictate punctuation out loud as part of the sentence — say "comma," "period," or "new line" exactly where they should appear in the text.
5. Once you're done dictating, press the shortcut again (or click away from the microphone), and the text stays in the field for you to keep editing with the keyboard as usual.
6. For longer dictation, consider switching to **Enhanced Dictation** in settings — it processes entirely on-device, works without an internet connection, and handles longer passages better.

## The best tools

- **macOS Dictation (built in)** — free, works in every app with a text field, nothing to install.
- **Windows Dictation (Win + H)** — the equivalent built-in feature for Windows users.
- **Otter.ai** — for transcribing longer recordings, meetings, or notes with higher accuracy and automatic speaker separation.
- **Audio-to-text transcription tools** — good for anyone who needs to process longer recordings outside of real-time dictation; if you're transcribing sensitive content, pay attention to which tools and data handling you're using.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** a rough draft of a longer piece of text can come together three times faster by dictation than by typing — for anyone writing emails regularly, that's real minutes saved every day.
- **Less writer's block:** speaking sidesteps that "staring at a blank page" feeling, because you phrase thoughts the same way you'd say them out loud to a colleague.
- **Accessibility:** for anyone dealing with wrist pain, hand fatigue, or a temporary injury, dictation is a full replacement for typing, not just a nice-to-have.
- **Faster first drafts:** even an imperfect dictated text is quicker to edit than writing from nothing — you have something to work from.

## Pro tip

Dictate the rough content without worrying about small mistakes or misrecognized words, then clean up the text by hand afterward — trying to dictate perfectly the first time (constantly correcting yourself out loud) is slower than getting the whole thought out quickly and polishing the mistakes at the end.
