In big meetings, one iron rule applies: whoever isn't speaking should be muted. But then your moment comes — you click the microphone icon, talk, and have to click it again once you're done answering. Half of people forget that second click, and end up broadcasting their typing into the call. Zoom and Teams both have a more elegant mode that works like a walkie-talkie: **hold a key, talk, release** — and you're muted again. Muted becomes the default state you automatically return to, not a task that's easy to forget.

## How to do it

**Zoom:**

1. In audio settings, check the **Press and hold SPACE key to temporarily unmute** option — it's usually already on.
2. When muted in a meeting, hold <kbd>Space</kbd>, say your piece, and release. The Zoom window has to be active.

**Teams:**

1. Open **Settings → Privacy** and turn on the **keyboard shortcut for unmute**.
2. In a meeting, hold <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Space</kbd> (on a Mac, <kbd>Option</kbd> + <kbd>Space</kbd>) for as long as you're talking. Here too, the Teams window needs focus.

**Google Meet** has a similar push-to-talk option in the call settings; where it's missing, the mute toggle <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd> and camera toggle <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>E</kbd> at least help.

## A typical scenario

A meeting with thirty people, you're half-listening and taking notes. A question comes up about your numbers. Instead of panicking — “where's my mouse, where's that window, where's the icon” — you just hold Space and answer; release the key and keep typing right after, without your typing clattering into everyone's ears. No “you're muted!”, and no microphone accidentally left on the opposite way when your kid starts a cartoon next to you.

## What you get out of it

Both classic video-call embarrassments disappear at once: talking silently into a muted mic, and accidentally broadcasting background noise. You also lower the bar for chiming in — when holding a key is all it takes, you'll toss in a short reaction even in places you wouldn't have bothered hunting for the icon.

## Pro tip

One catch: the key only works while the meeting window is **active**. If you're taking notes elsewhere during the call, click into the meeting window first (or switch to it with Alt+Tab) before holding the key to answer. After a few meetings it becomes a reflex — and it's worth it: push-to-talk is the most reliable safeguard against a microphone left in the opposite state from the one you think it's in.
