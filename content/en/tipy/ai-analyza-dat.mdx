Until recently, the gap between “I have data” and “I know what it means” was Excel skill — advanced formulas, pivot tables, at least a bit of statistics. Today you just need to know how to ask a question in plain English. Upload a spreadsheet, describe what you want to know, and AI hands back an answer and a chart. It's not a replacement for an analyst, and for big, business-critical decisions you still need one, but for a quick overview and first orientation it's more than enough.

## A typical scenario

Jana runs a small online store and wants to know why her revenue dropped in June, but she has neither the time nor the patience for pivot tables and filtering in Excel. Without AI, she'd spend an afternoon clicking through columns and formulas, possibly with an error at the end, and still not be sure she hadn't missed something. With AI, she just exports the accounting data to CSV, uploads it to a chat, and asks — she has an answer and a chart of top products within minutes, and spends the rest of the day solving the problem instead of hunting for it. When a specific product or month catches her attention, she simply asks a follow-up instead of building a new pivot table.

## How to do it

1. Export your data from your online store, accounting software, or CRM to CSV or XLSX.
2. Upload the file to ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini — usually by dragging it into the chat window or using the attach-file button.
3. Ask naturally: “Summarize revenue by month. What stands out? Chart the top 10 products.”
4. Keep going with follow-up questions — “And how did the same period look last year?”, “Is there seasonality in this?”
5. Always verify the conclusions: “Show me the calculation — which rows does that number come from?” A good answer can show you the source data, not just assert a number.
6. Ask for a plain-language explanation of the output too — “what should the company do about this?” — so the numbers turn into a recommendation, not just a table.

## The best tools

- **Chat AI with file upload** (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) — a built-in feature that handles CSV and XLSX and can even draw a chart; enough on its own for everyday analysis.
- **Microsoft Copilot in Excel** — if you have Microsoft 365, you can ask questions right inside the spreadsheet without exporting or leaving the app.
- **Julius AI** — a tool specialized in data analysis, good for more complex statistics and repeated queries against the same dataset.
- **Google Sheets with Gemini** — for people who work primarily in Google Workspace and want the analysis right next to the data.
- **Power BI or Looker Studio** — if you need the analysis regularly and want to turn it into a live dashboard rather than a one-off answer.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** an hour of work in Excel shrinks to a few minutes of asking and waiting for an answer.
- **Sanity:** no more googling pivot table syntax or functions like SUMIFS, and no more debugging why a formula returns the wrong number.
- **Quality:** AI flags outliers and patterns in the data that you'd likely miss scanning a spreadsheet by hand.
- **Impression:** a fast, well-supported answer to “how are we doing” even without advance preparation — useful in a meeting and for a quick reply to your boss.

## Pro tip

Anonymize sensitive data before uploading — replace customer names, emails, and card numbers with IDs or made-up labels so the data structure stays the same and the analysis still produces a meaningful result. Use a company AI account for this, not a personal one, and check whether your organization has confirmed that uploaded data isn't used to train the model. For truly sensitive business numbers, consider whether you need to upload the whole spreadsheet at all, or whether an aggregated excerpt would do.
