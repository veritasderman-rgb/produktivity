A longer WhatsApp message — instructions for a client, a summary of an agreement, a list of items — usually ends up as one solid block of text, where the important part gets lost. Yet WhatsApp has supported formatting for a long time: bold, italics, strikethrough, and in newer versions even bullet points, numbered lists, and quotes. Just wrap the text in the right characters — no menu-hunting required. Anyone who knows this sends messages that look like a structured document — and the recipient spots what matters at a glance.

## How to do it

Type the characters tight against the text, with no space between the character and the word:

1. `*bold*` — asterisks: **highlight a key piece of information** (a deadline, an amount).
2. `_italic_` — underscores: extras and notes.
3. `~strikethrough~` — tildes: what's no longer valid (“meet ~at 9:00~ at 10:00”).
4. `` `monospace` `` — backticks: codes, passwords, exact filenames where every character matters.
5. Lists: start a line with a dash and a space (`- `) for a bullet, or `1. ` for a numbered list — further items number themselves. Start a quote with `> `, and wrap a longer code block in three backticks.
6. Don't want to memorize the characters? **Select the text you've written** and choose bold, italic, and more from the menu that appears above it (on a computer) or after a long press (on a phone). The characters are faster, though — you type them right inline, no stopping.

## A typical scenario

A photographer sends a client a recap: “*Wedding 9/12* — meet _at 8:30 by the ceremony hall_. Bring: - the deposit confirmed by email - the list of family photos - the coordinator's contact. The gallery will be ready within ~thirty~ *fourteen* days.” The client sees the structure instantly: the deadline in bold, tasks as bullets, the outdated figure visibly struck through with the correction. The same message without formatting would be an eight-line wall of text, and at least one detail would get lost.

## What you get out of it

Work communication on WhatsApp stops looking like text messages and starts behaving like a document: what matters is highlighted, steps are numbered, what's no longer valid is visibly crossed out. There's less of the “I missed that” kind of misunderstanding — and you come across as more professional even in a channel that started out for vacation photos. Formatting pays off most in messages the recipient comes back to: instructions, recaps, lists. That's where the investment of three asterisks pays for itself every time someone reopens the message.

## Pro tip

Nearly identical markers work elsewhere too: Slack knows `*bold*` and `_italic_` the same way, and most chat apps understand related characters. Try the characters before hunting for buttons — they're usually faster. In Slack, [code and quote formatting](/tipy/slack-format-kod) is also worth knowing.
