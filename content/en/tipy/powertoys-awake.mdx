You kick off an hour-long video export, go to lunch, and come back to find the computer fell asleep after twenty minutes and the export stalled. The classic fix — changing the sleep timeout in power settings — has a well-known flaw: you forget to switch it back, and your laptop spends a week needlessly heating up your bag. The Awake module from the free PowerToys suite solves this elegantly: it keeps the computer awake for a set time only, then returns to its normal behavior on its own.

## How to do it

1. Install **PowerToys** (Microsoft's free toolkit) and turn on the **Awake** module.
2. An Awake icon appears in the notification area of the taskbar (near the clock) — right-click it.
3. Pick a mode: **keep awake indefinitely**, or **for a set time** — say 1 or 2 hours, depending on how long the job runs.
4. Decide whether the display should stay on too, or whether the screen can turn off while only the system stays awake — for exports and downloads, the second option is usually enough and saves power.
5. When the time is up, Awake switches itself off and the computer returns to your normal power plan. Nothing to reset, nothing to forget.

## A typical scenario

Marketa edits video, and every big export means 40 minutes of rendering. She used to either sit at the computer “babysitting” it, or leave sleep permanently disabled — and her laptop would regularly fall asleep in her bag with a hot day ahead of it. Now, before starting an export, she clicks Awake, picks one hour with the display off, and walks away. The export finishes, and the computer goes back to sleeping normally afterward.

Same story for a big upload to storage, a backup run, a long install, a download over a slow connection, or a build nobody's around to babysit. The common thread: the job runs longer than the sleep timer, and it dying halfway through means starting over.

## What you get out of it

- **Jobs that finish:** exports, backups, and downloads stop dying halfway through because of sleep mode.
- **No permanent trade-offs:** you don't have to sacrifice power-saving settings forever just for an occasional long task.
- **Peace of mind:** you walk away from the computer knowing it'll be done when you're back — not wondering.

## Pro tip

Awake is handy in meetings too: when you're presenting a document and the room just talks about it for five minutes, the screen loves to go dark at the worst possible moment. “Keep awake including display” for an hour covers the whole meeting — and everything returns to normal on its own afterward.

One closing caution: an awake computer is also an unlocked computer. Use Awake for tasks, not as a permanent substitute for sleep mode — and when you step away from the machine in the office, [lock it with Win+L](/tipy/win-l-zamknuti); the screen lock works normally alongside Awake and won't interrupt a running job.
