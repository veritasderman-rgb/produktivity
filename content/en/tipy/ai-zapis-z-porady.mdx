Meeting notes are exactly the kind of work a machine should do — a mechanical summary of what was already said, no creativity required. And that's precisely why people keep putting it off: it gets done in the evening, when half the details are already gone, or it doesn't get written at all.

## A typical scenario

Lenka, a manager, runs three meetings a week and promises after each one that she'll send notes "later today." In practice she doesn't get to it until the next day, by which point she no longer remembers exactly who committed to what — and has to ask participants again, which is awkward and slows down the whole team, since nobody knows what they're supposed to be doing.

With a meeting transcript and AI, the notes are ready within five minutes of the meeting ending. The recording gets transcribed automatically, the transcript goes into AI with a clear instruction, and out comes a structured text with decisions, tasks, and deadlines. All that's left is checking names and hitting send — instead of writing the notes from memory, starting from nothing.

## How to do it

1. Record the meeting with everyone's consent — both Teams and Google Meet can run a transcript directly in the tool, no external service needed.
2. After the meeting, take the full transcript and paste it into AI with a clear structure: "Structure: 1) decisions, 2) tasks — who, what, by when, 3) open questions for next time."
3. If the transcript contains sensitive information (salaries, personal data, trade secrets), consider whether to leave it out before pasting it into the AI tool, or remove it from the transcript by hand — especially if you're using a public AI chat without a corporate data processing agreement.
4. Review the draft notes and check names and deadlines — speech-to-text transcription sometimes mishandles similar-sounding names or numbers.
5. Add context missing from the transcript (document links, project numbers) — AI obviously can't know these from an audio recording alone.
6. Send the notes out as soon as possible after the meeting, not the next day.

## The best tools

- **Transcription in Teams or Google Meet** — a built-in feature, no extra installation, just turn it on before the meeting.
- **AI chat (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini)** — paste the transcript with clear formatting instructions, works with no extra tool.
- **Otter.ai** — a tool specialized in transcribing and summarizing meetings, useful if you want a history of every meeting in one place.
- **Fireflies.ai** — similar to Otter, and it also automatically distributes the notes and extracts tasks into a shared overview.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** an estimated 20–30 minutes saved on every meeting where notes used to be written by hand from memory or scattered notes.
- **Accuracy:** tasks and deadlines don't get forgotten or lost between the meeting and writing up notes that evening.
- **Faster team start:** when notes arrive within half an hour, people start on their tasks the same day, not after a reminder.
- **Fewer awkward questions:** you don't have to ask "wait, what did you actually promise" — it's right there in black and white, straight from the meeting.

## Pro tip

Send the notes within 30 minutes of the meeting — while everyone still remembers the context, they'll correct any mistakes in tasks or deadlines right away in their reply, instead of the misunderstanding dragging on into the next meeting. Archive the notes in one fixed place too (a shared document, a channel in your team tool) — after a few months, they build into a clear history of decisions you can return to any time.
