Procrastination is rarely laziness — it's usually a lack of clarity. When you don't know exactly where to start, your brain files the task as too big and puts it off, even if you have plenty of time and ability for it. AI can work around this: it breaks a task into pieces and suggests a first step so small and specific that it's hard to avoid.

## A typical scenario

A student has a term paper due in a week, knows only the topic so far, and for three days has been tidying his desk and checking his phone instead of writing. Without this tip, the longer he puts it off, the bigger and scarier the paper looks, and he only gets down to writing in the final days, under stress, with the least room left for quality. With this tip, he describes the task and where he's stuck to AI, gets an outline broken into small steps, and manages the first one right away because it's designed to be easy — and once he's started, the rest follows on its own.

## How to do it

1. Describe the task you're putting off to AI as specifically as possible — exactly what you need to do and by when.
2. Say why you're stuck too — you don't know where to start, there's too much of it, you're afraid you won't do it well. This information helps AI pick a suitable first step.
3. Use a prompt along the lines of: “Break this into steps of no more than 25 minutes each. Make the first step so easy I can do it right now.”
4. Actually do just that first, small step — don't try to tackle the whole outline right away.
5. Once you finish the first step, ask for the next one — by then you're usually already continuing on your own, because the hardest part was starting.
6. If your motivation fades again after the first step, that's fine — next time just repeat the process from the start instead of beating yourself up over it.

## The best tools

Combining AI's task breakdown with a simple way to check off steps works better than either tool on its own.

- **ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini with a task-breakdown prompt** — just describe the situation, nothing else to install.
- **The Pomodoro technique** (a 25-minute timer) combined with AI's step — helps keep momentum after the first step.
- **A to-do app** (Todoist, Things) for logging the suggested steps as individual tasks — you can see your progress without having to remember the outline.
- **A physical list on paper or a whiteboard** — for people who need to literally see the checkmarks in front of them, not just in an app.

The trick also works on small, unpleasant tasks outside of work — a bureaucratic errand, a phone call you keep putting off — the small-first-step principle is universal.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** starting sooner means fewer days lost to procrastination and more actual work getting done.
- **Sanity:** the dread of a “huge,” unfinished task fades once you see it broken into manageable pieces.
- **Quality:** work spread out ahead of time usually turns out better than work rushed at the last minute.
- **A habit:** you gradually learn to break tasks down yourself, even without AI, because you've seen how it's done, and it gets faster next time.

## Pro tip

If you get stuck in the middle of the work too, not just at the start, repeat the same process — describe where you are and what's holding you up, and ask for just the next step, not the entire rest of the plan at once.
