Leaving an unlocked computer is like leaving your office door wide open. Emails, documents, logged-in accounts, an open company system — all of it is accessible to anyone passing by, whether it's a curious coworker, cleaning staff, or, worse, someone who has no business being there at all. The fastest defense against that is a single keypress.

## A typical scenario

Hana, an accountant, steps away from her desk for five minutes to get coffee and leaves the computer unlocked because “it's just for a moment.” She happens to have the payroll system open, with sensitive salary data for the entire team. A coworker walking by doesn't even have to try — the data is right there on screen until she gets back.

If she'd pressed <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>L</kbd> before getting up from her chair, the screen would have gone dark and locked in under a second. When she gets back, she unlocks it with a PIN or a fingerprint — same speed as stepping away, just with the certainty that nobody saw anything in the meantime. The same reflex is handy at home too, where there's no coworker but maybe a kid who could accidentally wipe out unsaved work with a single keystroke.

## How to do it

1. Before you get up from your computer, press <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>L</kbd> — the screen locks instantly (L as in *lock*).
2. When you're back, unlock with a PIN, fingerprint, or face recognition if you have Windows Hello set up — with it, unlocking takes under a second and is faster than typing a password.
3. A locked computer keeps running in the background — running apps, downloads, or computations continue; only the screen and system access are blocked.
4. If you don't have Windows Hello set up yet, you'll find it under **Settings → Accounts → Sign-in options** — worth the one-time investment of a few minutes, since it saves time on every unlock afterward.
5. The shortcut works identically regardless of which app was active — nothing needs to be saved or closed first.
6. On a company laptop, locking is often also enforced automatically after a period of inactivity, but relying on that timeout alone means leaving the screen unlocked longer than necessary — your own shortcut is always faster.

## The best tools

- **Windows' built-in shortcut** (<kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>L</kbd>) — instant locking, works on any Windows computer with nothing to configure.
- **Windows Hello** (PIN, fingerprint, face recognition) — speeds up unlocking enough that locking stops being annoying and becomes a real habit.
- **Automatic lock on inactivity** (Settings → Sign-in screen → timeout) — a backup safeguard for moments you forget to lock.
- **Dynamic Lock** (Settings → Accounts → Sign-in options) — the computer locks itself automatically when a Bluetooth-paired phone moves out of range.

## What you get out of it

- **Data security:** sensitive information — salaries, contracts, logged-in company systems — stays protected even during a brief absence from your desk.
- **Peace of mind in an open office:** you don't have to worry about whether you left something private or work-sensitive visible on screen.
- **Compliance with company policy:** many companies require screen locking as part of their security policy — this shortcut satisfies that without effort.
- **Speed without compromise:** unlike shutting down or signing out, it doesn't slow you down — locking and unlocking both take seconds.

## Pro tip

Turn it into a reflex tied to standing up from your chair: stand up = <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>L</kbd>. Try overdoing it deliberately for the first week — lock even for ten seconds away from your desk — after a week you'll do it automatically, without even thinking about it.
