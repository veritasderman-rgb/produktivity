Not understanding something after the first lecture is normal. Staying stuck there is unnecessary. Unlike a teacher at the front of a room, AI is never in a hurry, has no problem explaining the same thing five different ways, and never gets annoyed that you're asking again. It works like a private tutor you can reach any time.

## A typical scenario

Honza, a student, is stuck on derivatives, and the lecture only gave him a definition and a handful of formulas — he understands each individual step but can't see why he's doing it. Without AI he has two options: reread the course notes (same text, same confusion) or wait a week for office hours.

Instead, he asks AI: "explain derivatives like I'm a high schooler who's never heard of them, then explain them again with an everyday analogy." He gets an explanation through a car's speedometer — suddenly a derivative makes sense as "rate of change," not just a formula. Then he asks for five check-up questions and finds out exactly where it still doesn't click — without waiting for office hours.

## How to do it

1. Ask for a tailored explanation: "explain derivatives like I'm a high schooler, then with an everyday analogy, then with a concrete numerical example."
2. If the first explanation still doesn't land, say so directly — "I don't understand this step, expand on it" — AI adjusts to exactly where the understanding breaks down.
3. Ask for check-up questions: "give me 5 questions from easy to hard and grade my answers."
4. Answer in your own words, not by copying back what you just read — active recall is learning, passive reading isn't.
5. When you get something wrong, ask not just for the right answer but why yours was wrong — that's where real understanding hides.
6. If you're studying something sensitive (your own diagnosis, real company data), consider whether anything in the prompt should be anonymized or rephrased more generally.

## The best tools

- **Whatever AI chat you already have** — ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini, works with no extra setup.
- **Khanmigo** — an AI assistant built specifically for teaching, and it actively guides you toward the solution rather than just handing over the answer.
- **Socratic (Google)** — a mobile app focused on explaining schoolwork from a photo of the assignment.
- **Notion AI or a similar AI-enabled notes tool** — useful when you want to save explanations and check-up questions directly into your own notes for later review.
- **Anki or another spaced-repetition app** — the check-up questions you get from AI can go straight onto flashcards and come back to you over the following days, not just once.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** an hour of confusion over course notes often shrinks to 15–20 minutes of targeted explanation.
- **Deeper understanding:** analogies and examples stick better than bare definitions, especially for abstract topics.
- **Self-check:** check-up questions show you exactly where the gaps are, before you find out at the exam.
- **Less stress:** you don't have to wait for office hours or feel embarrassed asking a "dumb" question — AI never gets annoyed.

## Pro tip

Flip it around: **you** explain the material to AI in your own words and ask it to correct your mistakes and fill in what you left out. Teaching to learn is the strongest technique there is — if you can explain something clearly, you actually understand it. And wherever your explanation doesn't hold up, that's exactly where your attention should go for your next study session.
