A tab you closed by mistake isn't lost. One keyboard shortcut brings it right back, exactly where it was — same address, same history. Yet most people, in that moment, reach for their browsing history and manually hunt down what they just closed, instead of using the shortcut that solves it in a single press.

## A typical scenario

You have fifteen tabs open, including a half-filled-out form, a long article, a spreadsheet with numbers, and an email with an attachment you need. In a quick cleanup — click, click, click to close — you accidentally close the spreadsheet you actually needed to keep open. Without the shortcut, you're now digging slowly through your browser history trying to remember exactly when you opened that page, hoping you can recall what you'd filled into the form on it. With <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>T</kbd>, the tab comes back instantly, exactly as you left it, right down to how far you'd scrolled down the page.

## How to do it

1. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>T</kbd> (on Mac, <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>T</kbd>) — the last closed tab reopens instantly at the same address.
2. You can press the shortcut repeatedly — the browser keeps bringing back tabs you closed, one after another, in the order you closed them, from most recent to oldest.
3. The same shortcut also works after a browser crash or a full shutdown: the next time you launch the browser, it restores the entire window with every tab you had open, so you don't have to reopen them one by one.
4. If you need to find a specific page from further back in your history and stepping back repeatedly would be too slow, open your browser history directly (usually via <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>H</kbd>) and search for it there.
5. The shortcut's memory for restoring tabs isn't unlimited — it typically covers roughly the last several dozen tabs closed in that session, so for anything older, your history is the more reliable option.

## The best tools

- **The built-in Ctrl+Shift+T shortcut** — works with zero setup in every major browser and covers 95% of situations.
- **Browser history (Ctrl+H)** — for finding pages closed further back than the reopen shortcut's memory reaches.
- **Tab sync across devices** (enabled in your browser account settings) — lets you find and reopen a tab you closed on a different device, like your phone.
- **Tab manager** (an icon in the top-right corner in browsers with lots of open tabs) — a clear overview of every open and recently closed tab in one place.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** instant tab recovery instead of digging through history — a few dozen seconds saved on every slip-up, which happens to nearly everyone daily during active browsing.
- **Peace of mind:** knowing an accidentally closed tab isn't gone for good reduces the stress of rapid tab closing and cleanup.
- **Preserved progress:** on pages with forms or a lot of scrolling, your position on the page is often restored too, so you don't have to hunt for where you left off.

## Pro tip

The shortcut also works on entire closed windows, not just individual tabs — if you accidentally close a whole browser window with all its tabs, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>T</kbd> brings the whole thing back the next time you reopen the browser. This also comes in handy after an unexpected system crash or restart, when an entire set of work-in-progress tabs would otherwise be gone.
