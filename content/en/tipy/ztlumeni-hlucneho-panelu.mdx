Music suddenly starts playing, or a video starts talking, from your computer — and you've got twenty-five tabs open with no idea which one it is. What follows is panicked clicking around while the ad happily blares into the open-plan office. Yet the browser has been marking the culprit the whole time: the tab that's playing sound has a small speaker icon in its title. And what's more — you can mute the sound directly on the tab, without even opening the page.

## How to do it

1. Scan the tab bar: the one playing sound has a **speaker icon** next to its title.
2. **In Firefox and Edge**, just click the speaker icon — the tab mutes (click again to unmute it). You don't need to open the page or hunt for a pause button on it.
3. **In Chrome**, right-click the tab and choose **Mute site**. Chrome then silences not just this tab but every other tab from the same site too — and remembers the setting until you allow the site again the same way.
4. Muting only affects sound: the video keeps playing, the page works normally. It's handy the other way too — leave background music playing and mute everything else.
5. You can tell a muted tab by its crossed-out speaker icon. So if a video you actually want to hear seems to have “no sound,” check the tab's title first — it's often just a long-forgotten mute.

## A typical scenario

You're at an online meeting, sharing your screen. Some tab in the background finishes loading and starts playing an ad with sound — right over your presentation. Instead of desperately clicking around, you spot the speaker icon at a glance, one click, silence. The meeting continues without losing its thread.

Second classic case: news sites and online stores that autoplay video on their own. Mute a site like that once in Chrome via right-click, and you're free of its noise for good — you keep reading articles, but nothing shouts at you anymore. The same trick is handy for a parked tab with radio or a media player: it plays in the background, and when you need quiet, you switch it off from the tab bar without hunting for a pause button somewhere on the page.

## What you get out of it

You find the source of unexpected sound with a glance instead of a minute of clicking around — which matters most exactly when something embarrassing plays into a meeting or an open office. And sites that don't deserve the habit of playing sound get silenced once and for all.

## Pro tip

Finding a noisy tab is even faster with [searching tabs via @tabs](/tipy/chrome-zavinac-hledani) — but the speaker icon is quicker still: you don't need to recall the page's name, just scan the bar with your eyes. If you have more tabs across multiple windows, check the other windows too — the icon lights up separately in each one.
