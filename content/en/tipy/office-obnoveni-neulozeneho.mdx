Click “Don't Save” instead of “Save,” an app crashes, the battery dies — and an hour of work seems gone. In reality, it usually isn't: Word, Excel, and PowerPoint continuously save backup copies of open files in the background, even ones you've never saved and that don't even have a name yet. You just need to know where to look for those copies — they're hidden two clicks deep in the File menu.

## How to do it

1. Open the app where you lost your work and choose **File → Info → Manage Document** (in Excel, **Manage Workbook**; in PowerPoint, **Manage Presentation**).
2. Click **Recover Unsaved Documents** (or Workbooks/Presentations). A folder of automatic backups opens — files you closed without saving stay there for several days.
3. Pick the file by its last-modified time, open it, and immediately save it with **Save As**.
4. For a document that already had a name, also check **File → Info** — under Manage Document you'll often find a version labeled “(when closed without saving).”
5. After a crash, the **Document Recovery** pane frequently opens on its own the next time you launch the app, showing the latest auto-saved version — just pick the most recent entry. On a Mac, that automatic recovery is the whole story: reopen the app and Office offers you the recovered version itself.

## A typical scenario

The night before a deadline, a student is finishing up a presentation when the computer restarts for an update. Panic. Instead of starting over, they open PowerPoint, go through File → Info → Manage Presentation → Recover Unsaved Presentations — and find a version eight minutes old. They lost two slides, not the whole evening.

Another classic: an accountant opens last year's workbook “just to check something,” spends an hour doing calculations in it, and out of habit clicks Don't Save on the way out. File → Info helps here too — for a named file, both Word and Excel keep a version labeled “when closed without saving”; just open and restore it.

## What you get out of it

On an ordinary day, nothing — and once in a while, everything. Knowing this path means “accidentally unsaved” is a two-minute annoyance instead of a disaster. It's worth walking through once now, so you know exactly where to go when the crisis actually hits.

## Pro tip

You can shorten the backup interval: **File → Options → Save**, the “Save AutoRecover information every X minutes” field — set it to, say, 5 minutes, and leave “Keep the last autosaved version if I close without saving” checked. And the surest safeguard of all: files on OneDrive or SharePoint with AutoSave turned on save themselves continuously and keep a [version history](/tipy/sheets-historie-verzi) much like Google Docs.
