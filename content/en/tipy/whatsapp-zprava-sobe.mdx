You need to quickly save a house number, snap a photo of a receipt “so I don't lose it,” or get a link from your phone onto your computer. There's a dedicated app for all of it — except you already own the fastest tool: WhatsApp lets you message **yourself**. A chat only you can see works like any other — text, photos, voice messages, documents — and thanks to WhatsApp on the computer, it syncs automatically between devices. No hunting for “how do I send this to myself”: you just write it where you're typing all day anyway.

## How to do it

1. In WhatsApp, tap **new chat**, and right at the top of the contact list you'll find **yourself** — an entry marked “(You)” next to your name. Tap it and type as you would to anyone else.
2. **Pin** the conversation to the top of your chat list (long-press the chat → pin), so it's always within reach.
3. On your computer, open WhatsApp (the app or web.whatsapp.com) — the same chat is waiting there, including everything you sent from your phone. It naturally works the other way too, from computer to pocket.
4. You can also send yourself **documents** (the attachment icon) — a PDF or spreadsheet moves between devices with no cable and no email.
5. In an older chat, search with the magnifying glass right inside the conversation — the same search works here as everywhere else in WhatsApp. For anything truly important, use the star (long-press a message → Star), so you can find it even six months later.

## A typical scenario

A tradesman does his rounds: at a client's, he photographs the water meter and dictates a voice note with measurements into his self-chat; in the car, he adds the next site's address. In the evening at his computer, he opens WhatsApp and has everything laid out on the day's timeline — he pulls the photos for the job and turns the notes into a quote. A parent, meanwhile, collects things through the day: a screenshot of a class schedule, a link to shoes on sale, “buy Emma a present” — and reviews it all at a glance in the evening.

## What you get out of it

One place for everything fleeting, with zero friction — a note is done in five seconds in an app you have open anyway. And moving between phone and computer stops being an operation: whatever you send yourself is on the other device within seconds. Compared to emailing yourself, the chat has one crucial advantage — it doesn't clutter your inbox, and there's nothing to “deal with”; it's simply a stream of notes ordered by time.

## Pro tip

A self-chat is also an ideal testing ground: before sending a client a message with [formatting](/tipy/whatsapp-formatovani-textu), try it out there first to see how it will look. You'll find the same “chat with yourself” in Telegram (Saved Messages) and in Slack too — the principle works everywhere you spend time.
