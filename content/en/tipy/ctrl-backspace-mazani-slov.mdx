A typo at the start of a word and you're tapping <kbd>Backspace</kbd> eight times in a row? Deleting letter by letter is the slowest way to fix text there is, and yet almost everyone does it automatically because they never got used to any other way. Both Windows and macOS have a shortcut that deletes an entire word in one press.

## A typical scenario

Adéla, a copywriter, is writing a long piece and notices a typo in the middle of a word — she typed "productivityy" instead of "productivity." With classic letter-by-letter deletion, she has to press <kbd>Backspace</kbd> as many times as there are extra letters to remove, land exactly on the right spot, and then retype the word from that same point. For a longer word, that's ten or more presses just for one small fix.

With <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Backspace</kbd>, the whole wrong word disappears in one press and she retypes it in full. Instead of counting letters and pressing precisely, she wipes out a whole chunk of text at once and keeps going — a fix that used to take ten keystrokes now takes two.

## How to do it

1. Place your cursor right after the word you want to delete (or anywhere within it).
2. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Backspace</kbd> — it deletes the entire word to the **left** of the cursor, not just one letter.
3. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Delete</kbd> — it deletes the entire word to the **right** of the cursor, useful when you're fixing text ahead of you rather than behind you.
4. The shortcut repeats — holding it down and pressing repeatedly deletes multiple words in a row, just like classic <kbd>Backspace</kbd>.
5. It works almost everywhere you type text: Word, browsers, email, Slack, and note-taking apps — it's standard system behavior, not a feature of a specific app.
6. On Mac, <kbd>Option</kbd> + <kbd>Delete</kbd> does the same thing instead of <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Backspace</kbd>.

## The best tools

- **The system's built-in shortcut** — works on both Windows and macOS with no setup at all, just start using it.
- **Text editors with custom shortcuts (VS Code, Word)** — some programs let you customize word-based deletion further, say to delete a whole sentence or paragraph at once.
- **Key-remapping tools (PowerToys Keyboard Manager, Karabiner on Mac)** — useful if you want to assign this and similar shortcuts to other keys, for a more comfortable hand position.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** an estimated 2 minutes a day saved on repeatedly tapping <kbd>Backspace</kbd> during everyday typing and fixes.
- **Smoother writing:** corrections interrupt your train of thought less, since they don't take as long and don't require precise letter counting.
- **Less finger fatigue:** for people who type a lot of text daily, small but frequent actions like deleting add up to a noticeable difference.
- **Fewer mistakes while fixing:** deleting letter by letter makes it easy to press one time too many and delete a bit of correct text along with it — deleting by word carries less of that risk.

## Pro tip

The same logic applies to cursor movement, not just deletion: <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>→</kbd> jump the cursor by whole words instead of single letters. Combining both — jumping and deleting by word — makes fixing text almost twice as fast. Add <kbd>Shift</kbd> to the movement (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>←</kbd>/<kbd>→</kbd>) and you select the whole word at once, handy when you want to retype it rather than just delete it.
