The classic advice “clear your cookies and cache” works, but it comes at a brutal cost: it logs you out of every site at once. Because of one misbehaving online store, you then have to re-enter passwords for your mail, your bank, and every other service you use. There's a more surgical option, though — the browser can clear data for just one specific site, right from the address bar, without touching any of your other logins.

## How to do it

1. Open the problem site and click the **icon on the left side of the address bar** (a padlock, or a sliders icon, depending on the browser).
2. **In Firefox** you'll see the option **Clear Cookies and Site Data…** right away — confirm it and you're done, no further submenus.
3. **In Chrome**, in the menu that opens, click **Cookies and site data**, then **Manage on-device site data**, and delete the entries with the trash icon. Edge works the same way, via its cookies item.
4. Reload the page afterward (feel free to use a [hard refresh](/tipy/tvrde-obnoveni-ctrl-f5)). The site will see you as a new visitor: you'll have to log in again and dismiss the cookie banner — but only there, everything else stays exactly as it was.
5. When to use it: a site gets stuck in a login loop, the cart behaves erratically, a page remembers the wrong setting (language, branch, currency), or it refuses to cooperate after a password change. Saved passwords aren't affected — they stay in your password manager and offer themselves automatically the next time you log in.

## A typical scenario

A parent is trying to log in to the school system, but after entering the password it just bounces back to the login page. Again and again, with no error message — a classic symptom of a corrupted login cookie. The advice from the internet says “clear your browser data,” which would also log them out of mail and banking, turning the evening into a login marathon. Instead: the icon by the address bar, clear data for just the school site, refresh, log in. Two minutes, one password.

A freelancer, meanwhile, finds that a client portal stubbornly shows an old account they thought they'd logged out of. Clearing one site's data is a reset of the relationship with that one site — a clean slate with no collateral damage.

## What you get out of it

You can fix a broken site without logging out of everywhere else — and without spending the evening logging back into everything. The dreaded advice “clear your cookies” turns into a two-minute, targeted fix instead of a blanket demolition.

## Pro tip

The same menu by the address bar icon also hides a site's permissions: camera, microphone, location, or notifications. If you ever accidentally clicked “block” on the microphone and video calls have been silent ever since, one toggle here brings it right back.
