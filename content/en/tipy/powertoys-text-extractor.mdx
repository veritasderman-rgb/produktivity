An error message in a screenshot a colleague sent. A tax ID on a scanned invoice. A paragraph in a presentation you only have as images. An order number in a paused video call. All of these are text you can see but can't select and copy — so you retype them by hand and hope you don't make a typo. The Text Extractor module from the free PowerToys suite fixes this: you select an area of the screen just like taking a screenshot, but instead of an image you get text straight onto your clipboard.

## How to do it

1. Install **PowerToys** (Microsoft's free toolkit) and confirm the **Text Extractor** module is turned on in the settings.
2. Wherever you see text on screen, press <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>T</kbd> — the screen dims like it does for a screenshot.
3. Drag your mouse to select the area with the text. That's it: the recognized text is on your clipboard, ready to paste with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>V</kbd> wherever you need it.
4. Recognition relies on Windows language packs — to read text accurately with diacritics, make sure the relevant language is installed on your system (Settings → Time & language → Language & region).
5. Keep in mind OCR isn't infallible — double-check numbers, IDs, or codes against the original.

## A typical scenario

Support sends you a screenshot of an error message with a “just google it” attached. The message is four lines of technical text. Instead of retyping it by hand, you press Win+Shift+T, drag over the message, and paste it into a search engine — ten seconds instead of three minutes and one typo that would have made the search find nothing.

A student version: a lecturer shares slides as images inside a PDF. A quote you need for your notes doesn't have to be retyped — you pull it straight off the screen, punctuation and all. And when researching old scanned course materials, you copy a paragraph into your notes in seconds, even though the PDF has no text layer at all.

## What you get out of it

- **No more retyping:** text in images, screenshots, scans, and videos stops being dead data — whatever you can see, you can get onto your clipboard.
- **Fewer errors:** manually retyping numbers and codes is a gamble; OCR followed by a quick check is both faster and more reliable.
- **Works everywhere:** it works across anything on screen — the app doesn't need to support copying, and it doesn't even need to be a file.

## Pro tip

Newer versions of Windows 11 can do a similar trick without PowerToys: in the [Snipping Tool (Win+Shift+S)](/tipy/win-shift-s-vystrizek), you'll find a text-extraction action right next to a snip. Text Extractor is a step faster, though — it skips the intermediate screenshot and returns text directly. On a Mac, [Live Text](/tipy/mac-live-text) plays the same role.
