A lot of small file tweaks don't need an app opened at all. Finder's context menu has a **Quick Actions** section that handles things people needlessly install converters or hunt for online tools to do: stitching several images into one PDF, converting HEIC to JPEG, rotating a photo, or even removing the background from an image. All two clicks away, right over the files.

## How to do it

1. Select one or more files in Finder and right-click.
2. In the menu, hover over **Quick Actions** and pick what you need:
3. **Create PDF** — turns several selected images into a single PDF, in selection order. Ideal for scanned pages or materials for a client.
4. **Convert Image** — changes the format (HEIC, JPEG, PNG) and optionally the file size too. Finally, a way to turn iPhone photos into JPEGs that even a recipient on an older Windows machine can open.
5. **Remove Background** — on newer versions of macOS, creates a copy of the image with the object cut out and a transparent background, no graphics editor required.
6. **Rotate Left** and **Markup** — a quick photo rotation and annotation without opening Preview.
7. The actions work in bulk too: select thirty HEIC photos and convert all of them at once with a single click.
8. Video files get two more actions, **Trim** and **Convert**: trimming a screen recording or shrinking it into a smaller file also happens without opening an editor.
9. You can customize the menu: at the very bottom of Quick Actions is a **Customize…** item, where you turn actions on and off — and any custom actions you build in the Shortcuts app show up here too.
10. The original is always preserved: conversions and background removal create a new file next to the original, so feel free to experiment.

## A typical scenario

A marketer needs to send three product photos to the printer as a single PDF, plus a logo with the background removed. She used to open an online converter she'd rather not upload company materials to, then cut out the background in a graphics editor. Now she selects the photos, right-clicks and chooses Create PDF, then clicks the logo and chooses Remove Background. A minute later she has both files — and nothing left her computer, which her company's security policy appreciates too. She uses the same approach for material from a photographer: thirty HEIC files from an iPhone get converted to JPEG all at once, so an outside agency on older machines can open them too.

## What you get out of it

You skip installs and questionable online tools for tasks you do a few times a week — conversions, merging into a PDF, rotating. And because everything runs locally, your documents never go to someone else's server. For more involved PDF edits, follow up with [Preview and its annotation tools](/tipy/mac-preview-anotace).
