How an exam period shapes up is often decided not while studying, but weeks earlier — in the minute registration for exam slots opens in the school's system. Whoever's there gets to space their exams out and pick times when their head actually works. Whoever remembers a day late scrapes together the leftovers: three exams in four days and an oral exam Friday at 4:40pm. The difference isn't diligence — it's one calendar reminder set to the exact moment registration opens.

## How to do it

1. Find out exactly when registration opens — the date and time are usually in the semester schedule, or the faculty announces it by email; often it's the same moment across the whole faculty.
2. Create a calendar event for that exact moment and give it **two alerts: one day ahead and 10 minutes ahead**. The day-ahead alert is so you can work out the evening before which slots you actually want — by the minute it opens, there's no more deciding, only clicking.
3. In the event notes, write down your priorities: “Statistics as early as possible, Law after it, ideally a week apart.” And backup choices too, in case the first slots are already gone.
4. Once you have your slots, add them to your calendar with the time and room — and for the important exams, add a “start studying” event with a sensible lead time. An exam in the calendar is a fact; when studying starts is a decision nobody makes without a reminder.
5. The same trick works for anything that opens at a set time and fills up: course and seminar-group registration, dorm assignments, PE slots.

## A typical scenario

Registration opens Monday at 5:00pm. A student has had her preferred order figured out since Sunday, and her phone beeps at 4:55pm. By 5:02pm she has four exams registered exactly to plan: the hardest subject in the very first week while she's still fresh, and always at least four days between exams. A classmate remembers to register Tuesday morning — and by then, the faster students have already planned his exam period for him: two exams back to back and the last slot right when he wanted to be at his part-time job.

## What you get out of it

An exam period spaced out on your own terms is noticeably less stressful and produces better results than a schedule made of leftovers. The cost is one calendar event per semester. And a side effect: registering thoughtfully also leaves you with a rough study plan already in place. How to use it is covered in the [Exams and Deadlines](/prirucka/zkousky-a-deadliny) chapter.

## Pro tip

Give the same care to the other end: the **withdrawal** deadline for an exam. For each exam, set a reminder for the day before the withdrawal deadline ends, with the question “are you actually ready for this?” — if not, it's your last chance to move to a later slot without burning an attempt. A decision that otherwise gets made late and in a panic becomes just another ordinary line in the calendar.
