Writing text with a reference beside it. Comparing two spreadsheets. Watching a call while taking notes. Half of working at a computer is "two windows side by side" — and with a mouse it takes forever: grab a window by its title bar, drag it to the edge of the screen, wait for the snap hint, release, then repeat the same dance with the second window.

## A typical scenario

You need to copy numbers from one spreadsheet into another, or read a PDF reference while writing a summary in Word. Without knowing the shortcut, you drag the window around with the mouse, miss the edge of the screen, the window doesn't snap, and you have to try again — and the same with the second window, which often ends up the wrong size too and needs further adjustment by dragging its corner.

With <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>→</kbd>, all you need is for the window to be active, then press two keys — it snaps instantly to exactly half the screen. Windows then immediately offers thumbnails of the remaining open windows to fill the other half, so the whole layout is done in seconds.

## How to do it

- Click the window you want to place so it's active, then press <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>←</kbd> — the window snaps to the left half of the screen.
- Press <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>→</kbd> to snap it to the right half. Once one half is occupied, Windows automatically offers thumbnails of your other open windows to fill the other half — just click the right one.
- <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>↑</kbd> maximizes the window to full screen, while <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>↓</kbd> minimizes it or restores it from maximized.
- The combinations work repeatedly and let you split the screen into quarters too — see the Pro tip below.

## The best tools

- **The built-in Win + arrow shortcuts** — free, work instantly on any Windows computer, the fastest way to split the screen.
- **Snap Layouts (Win + Z)** — for layouts more complex than just halves, offering grids and thirds of the screen at a glance.
- **PowerToys FancyZones** — a free add-on from Microsoft for custom, named layout zones, ideal for large or multiple monitors.
- **Rectangle (macOS)** — a similarly free keyboard-shortcut window splitter for Mac, which lacks the same built-in feature Windows has.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** roughly 2–3 minutes a day on tasks that would otherwise take slow mouse dragging — this adds up to dozens of hours a year if you often work with multiple windows.
- **Precision:** windows always align exactly to half the screen, with no annoying resizing by dragging an edge.
- **Smoother multitasking:** comparing documents, copying data, or watching a call while taking notes happens without constantly switching windows via <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Tab</kbd>.
- **Less frustration:** no more "missing" the edge of the screen with the mouse, which used to often mean trying again.

## Pro tip

The combinations also work in sequence: <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>←</kbd> followed by <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>↑</kbd> puts a window in the top-left corner as a quarter of the screen — repeat this with four different windows and corners to get four windows neatly arranged side by side on one screen.
