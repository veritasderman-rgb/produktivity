A familiar moment: you swipe the notification shade away with your thumb, and in the last flicker you catch that something important was in there — a message from school, a payment confirmation, a courier code. And it's gone. Android has a little-known safety net for this: notification history, which keeps everything from the last 24 hours, including notifications dismissed by a swipe. The only catch — it has to be turned on ahead of time. Do it now, thank yourself later. The tip applies to Android (version 11 and up); the iPhone has no comparable feature, a swiped-away notification there is gone for good.

## How to do it

1. Open Settings → **Notifications** → **Notification history** (on some manufacturers' phones the path is Settings → Notifications → Advanced settings).
2. Turn on the **Use notification history** toggle. From this moment everything gets recorded; unfortunately nothing from before that point can be recovered.
3. Next time you accidentally swipe something away, go back to the same place — you'll see **Recently dismissed** and, beneath it, a complete overview of the last 24 hours grouped by app.
4. A faster route to history: pull down the notification shade and tap **History** (the link shows up at the bottom if you've turned the feature on and some notifications have arrived).
5. Tapping an item in history takes you to the relevant app, as if the notification still existed.

## A typical scenario

You're scrolling through your phone on the subway and swipe away five notifications in one motion — four were clutter, but the fifth was a meeting moved to tomorrow. You remember it that evening with a vague feeling that "something was there." Without history you'd have to go through every app and hope; with history you open the overview, find the calendar notification, and you're done. Parents get double the value from history: messages from school apps have a habit of arriving at the worst possible moment and vanishing under a thumb mid-cooking.

## What you get out of it

The whole category of small losses — "I know something came in, but I don't know what" — disappears, along with the pointless blind trawl through every app. History also works as a log: when you're not sure whether a payment went through or whether an app even sent anything, you have a black box for the last 24 hours. And since the feature only records what was arriving anyway, it costs you nothing — turn it on once and forget about it.

To have even less to dig through, pair history with [muting annoying notifications right from the shade](/tipy/mobil-ztlumeni-z-oznameni).

## Pro tip

History has two limits worth knowing: it only stores 24 hours back, and it doesn't record content the app has since downloaded or changed — so for disappearing-message apps you'll see that something arrived, but not necessarily what. Don't rely on notifications alone for anything important because of this; history is a safety net, not an archive.
