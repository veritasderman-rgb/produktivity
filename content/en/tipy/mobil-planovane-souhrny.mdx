Every notification is a small tug away from whatever you're doing. Most of them don't need to arrive right away. App notifications are designed to pull you out of whatever you're doing — even for things that genuinely don't matter whether you see them now or three hours from now. A scheduled summary separates the truly important notifications (calls, messages from people) from everything else that can wait.

## A typical scenario

Honza had his phone set up exactly the way apps “wanted” it — every like, every new podcast episode, every app store update interrupted him instantly, with a vibration and a lit-up screen. On a given day that added up to well over a hundred notifications, maybe five of which actually needed an immediate reaction. His work fell apart into fragments, because every buzz meant refocusing afterward — and according to attention research, returning to an interrupted train of thought can take tens of seconds, regardless of how brief the interruption itself was.

After setting up a scheduled summary for 12:00 and 18:00, non-urgent notifications now quietly collect and wait for their time. His phone only buzzes today for calls and messages from people — everything else arrives at once, twice a day, as a digest he skims through in a minute instead of being pulled away by it piece by piece all day long.

## How to do it

1. **iOS:** Settings → Notifications → Scheduled Summary — choose which apps fall into the summary and set delivery times, say 12:00 and 6:00 pm.
2. **Android:** Settings → Notifications — set silent delivery (no sound or vibration) for individual non-urgent apps, or use your manufacturer's delayed-notification feature if it offers one.
3. Include apps in the summary that you're fine having delayed — social media, shopping apps, game notifications, news.
4. Leave instant notifications only for things that genuinely need a quick reaction: calls, messages and chats from specific people, calendar reminders.
5. Pick summary times that fit your day — typically when you'd take a break anyway (lunch, end of the workday), not in the middle of focused work.
6. After a few days, check whether you've grouped in anything you'd actually rather see sooner — the settings can be adjusted anytime.
7. Feel free to skim the summary lightly — the point isn't to read everything, but to decide on the handful of items that genuinely matter and wave off the rest with a single glance.

## The best tools

- **iOS Scheduled Summary** — a built-in system feature, works with every app with nothing extra to install.
- **Android Digital Wellbeing and silent delivery** — a similar built-in feature, its location varies by phone manufacturer.
- **“Do Not Disturb” with exceptions** — on both systems, you can also set select contacts to get through even in silent mode.
- **Focus modes (iOS)** — go even further, letting you set different notification rules for work and for personal time.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** roughly 20–30 minutes a day that would otherwise go to small interruptions and refocusing after each one.
- **Focus:** longer uninterrupted work blocks, since unimportant things simply don't ring.
- **Peace of mind:** less of a “must check this right now” feeling — you know the important things will still get through.
- **Clarity:** a twice-daily summary gets skimmed faster and with more perspective than a hundred individual interruptions scattered across the day.
