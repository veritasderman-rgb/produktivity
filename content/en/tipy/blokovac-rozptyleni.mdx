Willpower is a depletable resource. A blocker isn't. Relying on "I just won't look at social media" works for the first hour — and then a moment of tiredness or boredom hits, willpower gives out, and your finger reaches for your phone or the browser tab on its own. A distraction blocker removes that fight entirely: you decide once, ahead of time, and in the moment your willpower is weak, there's no decision left to make.

## A typical scenario

Filip, a student, sits down to write his thesis with every intention of focusing for two hours. Twenty minutes in comes the first thought of "just a quick look at my phone," then a second one an hour later, and before he knows it, the two-hour block has dissolved into forty minutes of actual work scattered among dozens of detours to social media and messages.

When Filip sets up a block the evening before for his morning writing hours, no decision has to happen in the morning at all — social media and news sites simply aren't available, full stop. He doesn't have to fight the temptation, because the temptation isn't within reach. The two-hour block stays a two-hour block of work.

## How to do it

1. Install a distraction blocker — Cold Turkey or Freedom for your computer, LeechBlock as a free browser extension.
2. Make a list of your "time sinks" — the sites and apps where you know you lose the most attention: social media, news, YouTube, or specific forums.
3. Set up a recurring block for your work periods — say, 9am to noon every weekday, based on when you need focus the most.
4. Some tools (Cold Turkey) let you set a block so it can't be turned off early, even with your own consent — that helps in exactly the moment willpower alone wouldn't be enough.
5. Beyond a fixed schedule, use an on-demand one-time block too, whenever you sit down for an important task outside your usual block.
6. Review your block list regularly — if you notice a new site or app eating your time, add it right away, not a month later.

## The best tools

- **Cold Turkey** — one of the strictest blockers, lets you set a block that can't be turned off before the timer ends.
- **Freedom** — works across devices (computer and phone at once), so it blocks distractions even wherever you'd otherwise escape to.
- **LeechBlock** — a free browser extension, a good start with no need to install a separate app.
- **Built-in app limits on your phone** (Digital Wellbeing on Android, Screen Time on iOS) — free and requires no installation, works well as a companion to a desktop blocker.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** an estimated 30–45 minutes a day that would otherwise disappear into small detours to social media and the news.
- **Deeper focus:** working in a block with no way out leads to a faster path into a state of full concentration.
- **Less guilt:** instead of fighting temptation and then beating yourself up over "procrastinating," the temptation simply isn't within reach.
- **A more predictable day:** you know exactly when you'll have access to distractions and when you won't, instead of constant internal negotiating.

## Pro tip

The key is to set the block **ahead of time** — the evening before for the next morning, not the morning of for that same morning — because by morning, willpower may not even be enough to start the blocker in the first place. If you're not sure how strict a block you can handle, start with something shorter and looser and extend it gradually — a lasting change in habit works better than one extremely strict attempt you abandon after a week.
