You know the juggling act: copying numbers from a spreadsheet into a calculator, but clicking into the spreadsheet hides the calculator. So you switch, shuffle windows around, make room. All you really needed was to tell Windows: “Keep this window on top, always.” That's exactly what the Always on Top module from the free PowerToys suite does — one shortcut, and the selected window stays visible above everything else, no matter where you're working.

## How to do it

1. Install **PowerToys** (Microsoft's free toolkit) and check in the settings that the **Always on Top** module is turned on.
2. Click into the window you want to keep on top and press <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>T</kbd> — the window gets a colored border, and from that moment no other window can cover it.
3. Keep working normally: click into other apps, switch, type — the pinned window stays in view.
4. The same shortcut, applied to the active pinned window, turns the mode off again. You can customize the border (color, thickness, and a sound cue) in the PowerToys settings.
5. You can pin more than one window at a time — notes and a calculator simultaneously, for instance.

## A typical scenario

You're transcribing figures from a paper original, following a template sitting in a small notes window. Without pinning, the notes hide every time you click into the form, and you go hunting for them with Alt+Tab — twenty times an hour. With Always on Top, you pin them into a corner and they're simply there.

Another classic: a video call you need to work through. You pin the small call window, shrink it into a corner, and see exactly when someone speaks up or a question comes your way — instead of half-listening in the background and nervously switching over to check if anything's happening.

## What you get out of it

- **No more hunting for windows:** a reference window — a template, a brief, a calculator, a timer — stays visible without a single Alt+Tab.
- **Fewer interruptions:** every window switch is a micro-pause where you can lose your train of thought. A pinned window eliminates it entirely.
- **Control over calls:** a meeting running in the background stops being a black box; you see the chat and hands raised for questions.
- **Universal reach:** only some apps (media players, the calculator) have their own “keep on top” option — Always on Top gives it to every window in the system, including corporate apps that were never designed with your convenience in mind.

## Pro tip

Combined with [arranging windows side by side](/tipy/win-sipky-okna-vedle-sebe), you cover two different scenarios: splitting the screen suits actively working in both windows; Always on Top wins when you're only monitoring the second one — it takes up just a corner of the screen, leaving the main app the rest.
