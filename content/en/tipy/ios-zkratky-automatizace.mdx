The best habits are the ones your phone handles for you. Beyond one-off sequences of steps, the Shortcuts app can also run automations — rules that trigger themselves based on time, location, or what you're currently doing on your phone, with zero taps from you.

## A typical scenario

Every morning at the office you do the same thing: silence your phone, turn on a focus mode, and open your task list to see what's planned for the day. Doing it manually takes half a minute, but more importantly, it's easy to forget, especially when you're rushing or dealing with something else. With an automation tied to arriving at the office (or to a specific time), all three steps happen on their own while you're still taking off your jacket.

## How to do it

1. Open the **Shortcuts** app and switch to the **Automation** tab.
2. Tap **+** and select **Create Personal Automation**.
3. Choose a trigger: a specific time, arriving at or leaving a location, opening a particular app, connecting to a Wi-Fi network, and plenty more.
4. Add one or more actions to run — turning on a focus mode, sending a message, starting music, opening an app.
5. Turn off **Ask Before Running** if you want the automation to run completely without confirmation — otherwise it will ask you every time it fires.
6. Save the automation; from then on it runs in the background by itself until you turn it off or edit it.

## The best tools

- **Shortcuts app (iOS)** — built in, free, handles most everyday automations without writing a single line of code.
- **Google Assistant Routines / Tasker (Android)** — similar functionality for Android, with Tasker going even further for power users.
- **IFTTT** — for automations that reach beyond the phone, like connecting to smart home devices or web services.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** you're not doing a repeating sequence of steps by hand every day — for a three-step morning routine, that's only a few dozen seconds daily, but consistency is the bigger win.
- **Reliability:** the automation never forgets, whether you're in a rush or your head is full of other things.
- **Less mental load:** you don't have to think about the steps of the routine, your phone does them for you at the right moment.
- **Flexibility:** the same principle works for anything that repeats — leaving work, arriving home, opening a specific app.

## Pro tip

A tried-and-tested automation: at 8:30 on weekdays, turn on a Work focus mode and open your task list right away. Start with one simple automation, confirm it works reliably, and only then add more — the more you set up at once, the harder it gets to figure out which one does what.
