An insurance policy, a warranty card for the washing machine, a boiler inspection report, a lease, a vehicle title. Documents you need once a year — but when you need them, it's urgent: water's leaking, the fridge broke, the insurer is on the phone. And that's exactly why you can never find them; the other person saw them last, sometime last year. The fix is a digital first-aid kit: one cloud folder both partners can see, where every new document goes the moment it exists.

## How to do it

1. In Google Drive, OneDrive, or iCloud, create a folder called **Household** with four subfolders inside: **Insurance**, **Warranties & Receipts**, **Housing** (lease, utilities, inspections), **Car**.
2. Share the folder with your partner with edit rights — the whole folder, not individual files. Whatever either of you adds, both of you see.
3. Fill it once, in one go: pull out the binder and spend half an hour scanning with your phone — see the tip [scan paperwork on the spot](/tipy/skenujte-telefonem) for how. You don't need to scan everything, just what you'd be searching for in a crisis.
4. Name files using a *year–thing–detail* pattern: "2026-washer-warranty-card," "2025-home-insurance-policy." That way search still works years later.
5. From then on, one rule applies: a new contract, a receipt for a pricier appliance, or an inspection report gets scanned the day it arrives. Feel free to leave the paper original in the binder — you'll never need to hunt for it again.

For appliances, one file per item works well: the receipt and warranty card photographed into a single PDF. For a claim, you then send a single attachment.

## A typical scenario

On a Saturday evening the boiler stops heating. The technician on the phone wants the model, install year, and date of the last inspection. It used to mean digging through the binder, arguing over who last saw the inspection report, and finally giving up. Now you open Household → Housing on your phone, and a minute later you're reading the technician the details from the inspection report — and forwarding it to him on the spot.

Same scenario: a flooded apartment, and the insurer wants the policy and photos of the damaged items with receipts. All three taps away, even if you're on a business trip and only your partner is home.

## What you get out of it

Crisis situations shrink from hours of searching to minutes. Claims stop falling apart because "we don't have the receipt anymore." And the household stops depending on the one person who's the only one who knows where everything is — which you'll appreciate most exactly when that person can't be reached. ## Pro tip

Promote a few documents to "always carry with me": your insurance card, kids' vaccination records, your car insurance policy number. Star them in your cloud app or make them available offline — in an accident or at the doctor's, you'll have them even without signal, no searching required.
