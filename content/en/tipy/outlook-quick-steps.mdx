If you're doing the same sequence of clicks five times a day, it deserves to be one button. Quick Steps in Outlook can bundle several actions at once — moving to a folder, marking as read, forwarding, categorizing — into a single click or keyboard shortcut, instead of you carrying them out one by one every time.

## A typical scenario

Simona, a department lead, regularly hands off a certain type of email to her assistant to handle — she forwards it, adds a note, moves the original to a "Delegated" folder, and marks it as read. Without Quick Steps, that's four or five separate clicks per message, and with dozens of emails a week it adds up to hours of pointless manual work. With a Quick Step set up, she just clicks the "Delegate" button once and all four actions happen together — the message is forwarded, noted, moved, and marked done.

## How to do it

1. On the **Home** tab of the ribbon, find the **Quick Steps** gallery and click **Create New**.
2. Build the sequence of actions that should run together — typically moving to a specific folder, marking as read, assigning a category, or forwarding to a specific address.
3. Name the Quick Step after its purpose, not the actions inside it — "Delegate" or "Handled" sticks in memory better than a technical description of the individual steps.
4. Assign the Quick Step a keyboard shortcut, such as <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>1</kbd>, and use it without touching the mouse, straight from the keyboard on a selected message.
5. Build out more Quick Steps over time based on whatever action sequences you find yourself repeating most in your mail — you don't need to invent them all upfront, just add a new one whenever you notice yourself doing the same thing over and over.
6. You can edit an existing Quick Step at any time — add or remove an action — without deleting it and building it from scratch, which comes in handy as your workflows gradually change.

## The best tools

- **Quick Steps in Outlook** — built in, free, available right on the Home tab without needing any other tool.
- **Outlook Rules** — for fully automatic sorting without clicking anything manually, a good pairing with Quick Steps for things you still want to control by hand.
- **Quick Parts** — for quickly inserting repeated text into emails, a complement to Quick Steps for sending.
- **Sharing Quick Steps across a team** — if your whole team handles a similar type of mail the same way, it's worth setting up the same Quick Steps for everyone so mail gets handled consistently.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** roughly ten minutes a day from collapsing repeated click sequences into a single button or shortcut.
- **Consistency:** the same type of email gets handled the same way every time, with no risk of forgetting a step.
- **Less mental load:** you don't need to remember the whole sequence of steps, just the name of the Quick Step or its shortcut.
- **Faster mail handling under pressure:** on high-volume mail days, the difference between one click and five clicks across dozens of messages is noticeable.

## Pro tip

The classic move: a "Delegate" Quick Step that pairs forwarding to your assistant with moving the message to a Delegated folder — one click instead of six. It's just as worthwhile to build a "Handled" Quick Step for emails you just need to archive and mark done.
