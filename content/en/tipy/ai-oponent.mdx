Colleagues are polite — before they tell you your proposal has a hole in it, they'll think it over ten times and may end up saying nothing at all, because they don't want to spoil the mood or wade into details that aren't their concern. AI doesn't have to be polite. When you ask it to, it can be a tougher critic than you'd find anywhere in your company, and it'll catch weaknesses before they show up in front of a client or in a meeting, when it's too late to fix them.

## A typical scenario

A salesperson has finished a client proposal and is happy with it — colleagues in the hallway just said “looks good,” having skimmed it between other things. Without this tip, the proposal goes out and a weak spot — say, a missing warranty or unclear pricing for add-ons — only gets caught by the client, who uses it to ask for a discount or delays signing by a week. With this tip, before sending it, the salesperson has AI play the role of a demanding client and gets a list of questions the client might genuinely ask — then fills in the answers in advance, so they don't look caught off guard in the meeting.

## How to do it

1. Paste your finished draft into AI — a proposal, a strategy, a request email, anything that's about to go out and needs to hold up.
2. Assign the critic role: “You're a demanding critic, a CFO, or a customer. Tear this apart: weak points, missing arguments, questions I'll get asked.”
3. Push AI to be specific — if the answer is generic, follow up: “Which exact point does that objection apply to?”
4. Work the strongest objections directly into the document, so you don't have to respond to them for the first time when a real client or leadership raises them.
5. For important documents, repeat the exercise with a different role — once as a CFO, once as a lawyer — each position exposes different weaknesses.
6. At the end, ask for a summary of the three most serious risks, so you know what to focus on if you don't have time to fix everything.

## The best tools

For truly critical documents, it's best to combine an AI critique with at least one round of human feedback.

- **ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini with a role in the prompt** — a built-in capability, no special tool needed.
- **A saved “harsh critic” role in your custom instructions or project** — set it up once, reuse it without writing the prompt again.
- **A colleague from another department for a second round** — an AI critic is a fast first wave; human review still matters for truly big decisions.
- **A fresh, separate conversation for the critic role** — if AI helped write the document in the original conversation, a new conversation without that context tends to be more critical.

## What you get out of it

- **Quality:** documents sail through approval or a client meeting more smoothly, because you caught the weaknesses yourself.
- **Sanity:** less chance of an awkward moment where the other side is the one who points out the hole in your proposal.
- **Time:** faster prep for a defense than improvising counterarguments on the spot, when you can be caught off guard.
- **Confidence:** you walk into the meeting having already tested the toughest version of yourself against yourself, with an answer ready.

## Pro tip

It works in reverse too — before you send tough feedback, have AI play the recipient and predict how the message will land. If the document contains sensitive business data, like prices, margins, or client names, consider replacing it with more general figures for the purposes of the critique — spotting weaknesses in the argument usually doesn't require the exact data.
