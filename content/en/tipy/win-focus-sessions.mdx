You don't need to install a Pomodoro timer — it's already in the system. Windows 11 hides a feature called Focus sessions inside the plain old Clock app, one most people don't know about even though it does exactly what they'd otherwise download a separate app for: it counts down time, blocks distractions, and shows how much you actually got done in one stretch today.

## A typical scenario

Filip, a student, is trying to study for an exam, but his phone next to the laptop beeps at every new message and he can't resist checking it. Fifty planned minutes of studying fall apart into twenty minutes of actual work and thirty minutes of interruptions and re-starting.

With Focus sessions, he sets the timer to fifty minutes, links it to a specific task in Microsoft To Do, and starts it — Windows automatically switches on Do Not Disturb for the duration of the session, so notifications simply don't arrive. When it ends, he sees in the app how many such blocks he's finished that day, which works as a small motivation to keep going tomorrow too. He can leave the phone right next to him, but the computer itself is now guarding against attention leaking away in small pieces.

## How to do it

1. Open the **Clock** app, which ships with Windows 11, and switch to the **Focus sessions** tab.
2. Set the session length — typically 25 or 50 minutes, depending on how long you can hold focus without a break — and press start.
3. For the duration of the session, Windows automatically turns on **Do Not Disturb**, so no notifications from apps or the system show up on screen.
4. When a session ends, the app offers a short break before you start the next block — the break length can be adjusted in settings too.
5. The app keeps a history of completed sessions over time, so you can look back and see how much focused work you put in over a day or a week.
6. You can adjust both the session and break length anytime based on what actually works for you — the classic 25 minutes is a suggested starting point, not a fixed rule.

## The best tools

- **The Clock app in Windows 11** — built in, free, no installation, tied directly into the system's Do Not Disturb mode.
- **Microsoft To Do** — linking it to Focus sessions lets you pick the specific task a session is tied to, so time spent shows up in the context of your tasks too.
- **Forest or a similar mobile app** — an alternative for your phone, where a “growing tree” visualization discourages you from picking it up during a session.
- **Toggl Track or another time tracker** — if, beyond Pomodoro itself, you want more detailed tracking of time spent on different projects.
- **Focus mode in the browser or operating system** — extra blocking of distracting sites and apps for the duration of a session, for people who need more than Do Not Disturb alone.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** structured focus blocks can easily squeeze more than twenty extra minutes of real work out of an otherwise scattered day, just by cutting down interruptions.
- **Fewer distractions:** Do Not Disturb turning on automatically means you don't have to rely on willpower to ignore notifications — the system does it for you.
- **Insight into your own productivity:** you can see, in black and white, how many focused blocks you managed in a day, which helps you gauge your real capacity going forward.
- **A lower barrier to starting:** the feature is already part of the system, so there's no decision to make about which app to download and whether to trust it.

## Pro tip

Linking to Microsoft To Do lets you pick the specific task a session is tied to — so in the evening you can see exactly what your focused time went toward, not just how many minutes you spent “sitting at the computer.”
