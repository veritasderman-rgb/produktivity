You've been taking notes for years. Meeting minutes, decisions, ideas, client notes, things you read somewhere. Then comes the question “what did we decide about pricing last year?” and you spend twenty minutes flipping through Notion, trying three different keywords, and finally just ask a colleague because it's faster. The note exists. You just can't find it. **The value of notes isn't realized when you write them — it's realized when you recall them, and that's exactly where most systems fail.** AI connected to your notes fills in that last step: you ask in your own words, and the answer comes back from your own writing, with a link to where it came from.

## A typical scenario

Philip runs a three-person studio and has written everything down for eight years, now in Notion. He has over two thousand pages there and is proud of it — except he actually uses about twenty of them. The rest is an archive he can't get into, because full-text search only finds what he happens to phrase exactly the way he did back then.

He connects Claude to Notion and asks: “What did we decide about hourly rates last year, and why?” The answer arrives in half a minute, citing two meeting notes — including a reason he'd completely forgotten. Next he tries “summarize everything I have on the client Novak” and gets an overview drawn from seven pages spanning three years, which would have taken him half an hour to assemble by hand. And the one that surprises him most: “have I ever solved the invoice-export problem before?” — yes, two years ago, laid out in three steps. He hadn't gone looking for it, because he didn't remember ever dealing with it.

## How to do it

1. **Connect AI to wherever your notes live.** For Notion, the simplest route is a connector: you grant permission, and AI sees into your workspace without you copying anything. How connectors work and what you can hook up through them is covered in the tip on [MCP connectors](/tipy/mcp-konektory-usb-c-pro-ai). If your notes live in Obsidian or as files on your computer, give AI access to the relevant folder — Obsidian is just a folder of text files, so it can be read directly.
2. **Only expose what it should actually see.** Grant permission to a specific space or folder, not everything. Keep a personal diary, health notes, passwords, and client personal data out of it. Read anything sensitive only through a **paid or business account with contractual data protection**, not a freely available chat. You grant the permission, and you can revoke it anytime — check periodically what's actually connected.
3. **Ask like a person, not a keyword.** This is the whole difference. Full-text search looks for strings; this looks for meaning. Questions like “what did we decide about…,” “summarize everything on client X,” “have I ever dealt with…” all work. Phrase it however you actually remember it — imprecisely is fine.
4. **Require a source reference.** Add a line to your prompt: “for every claim, state which note it comes from, and if it's not in the notes, say so explicitly.” Without this, AI may fill a gap with general knowledge, and you'll think it's your own decision. Verifying is one click.
5. **Write notes so they can be found later.** Recall works better when there's something to work from: the date, who was involved, and above all **why** something was decided. Cross-linking helps here too — [double brackets](/tipy/obsidian-dvojite-zavorky) in Obsidian, [linked views](/tipy/notion-propojene-pohledy) in Notion.
6. **Make it your first reflex.** Next time you think “we've dealt with this somewhere before,” ask your notes first. The habit forms within a few weeks, and the archive turns from a pile into a resource.

## The best tools

- **[Claude](https://claude.ai)** — a Notion connector and file reading in a shared folder; answers cite the specific page or file.
- **[Notion](https://notion.com)** — notes in structured databases that are easy to search, by both machine and human.
- **[Obsidian](https://obsidian.md)** — notes as plain text files; the choice if you want your data physically under your own control.
- **Claude Cowork** — a desktop-app mode for working with files without writing code; you give it access to your notes folder, and Claude reads, sorts, and builds overviews from it.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** half an hour of searching your own archive simply disappears. The answer arrives before you'd have opened a second tab.
- **Return on effort:** years of notes finally start paying off. What used to be a cost becomes a resource.
- **Continuity:** you stop forgetting why you decided what you decided — invaluable in client dealings.
- **Peace of mind:** writing notes stops feeling pointless, because you know you'll actually be able to find them again.

## Pro tip

Try a question full-text search could never answer: **“where do I contradict myself in my notes from the past year?”** or “which topics keep coming back and I never actually resolve?” These are questions about patterns across hundreds of pages, and the answer tends to be uncomfortably accurate. And the site's rule still applies — **AI proposes, the person approves**: don't give the tool permission to rewrite or delete notes. Reading is enough.
