Squinting at tiny text gets you nowhere. Plenty of people deal with small web text by leaning closer to the monitor or taking off their glasses — instead of simply zooming the page in. Browser zoom is one of the oldest features on the web and, at the same time, one of the least used, even though it takes a single keyboard shortcut and the readability difference is instant.

## A typical scenario

You're reading a long article or working with a table on a site where the text is small and gray on white — a classic problem on older corporate intranets and less polished websites. Without zoom, you either squint and read more slowly, or open a system magnifier, which throws off the whole page layout. With <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>+</kbd> you zoom the page to a comfortable size within a second — the text stays right where it is, just bigger and easier to read. When you're done, press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>0</kbd> and you're back at the default scale, ready for the next page, which might not have tiny text at all.

## How to do it

1. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>+</kbd> zooms the page in, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>−</kbd> zooms it out. Holding <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> and scrolling your mouse wheel does the same thing — handy when one hand is already on the mouse.
2. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>0</kbd> instantly resets the scale to the default 100% — no need to click your way back down one step at a time.
3. The browser remembers the zoom level for each site separately. Once you set a scale on a given page, it's applied automatically the next time you open that page again.
4. Your current zoom level is visible in the browser menu (usually next to the three dots or under View), where you can also set the scale with the mouse if you prefer clicking over the keyboard.
5. On a laptop trackpad, the pinch-to-zoom gesture with two fingers works too, just like on a phone or tablet — handy when you don't have a keyboard with a numeric keypad handy, or you're working from the trackpad alone.

## The best tools

- **The browser's built-in zoom (Ctrl+/−/0)** — works in every major browser with zero setup, it's the first choice and requires no add-ons or extensions.
- **Setting a default zoom level in the browser** — in the display settings (Chrome: Settings → Appearance → Page zoom) you can permanently set a larger default scale for every site at once.
- **System zoom (magnifier)** — for situations outside the browser, like desktop icons or other apps, where web zoom won't help.
- **Reader mode** (available in many browsers, usually an icon in the address bar) — beyond font size, it also unifies the page's formatting and strips out ads, which is great for longer articles.

## What you get out of it

- **Eye comfort:** less fatigue during longer reading sessions, especially with small text on unpolished sites.
- **Speed:** zooming in and back to normal each take a second, so it's worth using even for a quick read.
- **Fewer mistakes:** in tables and forms with small text, it's easier to tell similar-looking characters apart (like 0 and O, 1 and l).

## Pro tip

If small text bothers you all the time and not just on one site, set a higher default page zoom right in your browser settings — you'll save yourself the hassle of zooming in on every new visit. It's especially worth it on a large, high-resolution monitor, where the browser's default font tends to be set smaller than is comfortable to read.
