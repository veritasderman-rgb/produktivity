General AI knows a little bit of everything — and sometimes fills in gaps without admitting it. Google's NotebookLM takes the opposite approach: it knows exactly what you gave it, and nothing more. You upload your own sources, and the tool answers exclusively from them, citing the specific passage the answer came from. For studying from lecture notes, prepping for an exam, or getting your bearings in hundreds of pages of contracts, that's a fundamental difference from chatting with a general-purpose AI.

## A typical scenario

Medical student Adam has three days until his exam and three hundred pages of lecture notes he never properly read during the semester. Asking a general AI “what's important to know about X” is risky — it might answer generically based on the internet, not on what a specific professor will actually expect. Instead, Adam uploads exactly those lecture notes, the presentation slides, and his own notes into NotebookLM. Then he asks: what do the notes say about a given topic, and has it generate practice questions from specific chapters. Every answer comes with a link to the exact page or paragraph, so he can instantly verify the AI didn't make anything up. In three days, he gets through material that would otherwise take him a week.

## How to do it

1. Go to notebooklm.google.com and create a new notebook.
2. Upload the sources you want the AI to draw from — PDFs, Google Docs, websites, and other formats are all supported. The more relevant and complete your sources, the better the answers.
3. Ask specific questions tied to the uploaded material, like “what do the notes say about this topic” or “generate practice questions from chapters 3 through 5.”
4. Every answer cites the source it came from — click the citation to instantly confirm the answer checks out, and see the surrounding text in context.
5. Before uploading anything with personal or sensitive information (say, a contract with real names, ID numbers, or internal company data), anonymize the data or strip it out of the document. Uploaded content is processed by an external service, and even though it's built for working with your own sources, it's always smarter to handle sensitive data with care.

## The best tools

- **NotebookLM (Google)** — free, the best choice for working with your own texts and getting citations right inside the answer.
- **ChatGPT with uploaded files** — a solid alternative if you already work in ChatGPT and don't want to switch tools, though source citations aren't as clear.
- **Google Docs / Notes** — for preparing your sources before uploading, so your notes are in a clean, organized shape.
- **Anki or another spaced-repetition app** — for locking generated questions into long-term memory through spaced repetition.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** an hour or more per exam, since you're not manually digging through hundreds of pages and the AI generates questions in seconds.
- **Reliability:** answers come only from your own materials, not the general internet, so they actually match what your professor or employer expects.
- **Verifiability:** citations let you see exactly where an answer came from, so you don't have to take the AI's word for it blindly.
- **Peace of mind:** instead of feeling like you didn't get through everything, you end up with a clear, verified summary of the whole subject.

## Pro tip

NotebookLM can also generate a summary, a mind map, or an overview of the key themes across an entire notebook from your sources — useful as a quick first pass through the material before you dive into detailed questions.
