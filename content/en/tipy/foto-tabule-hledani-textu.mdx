Anyone can photograph a whiteboard at the end of class. The problem shows up six weeks later: your gallery has three hundred photos, and the one with the formula derivation gets hunted for by scrolling through by hand until you give up. Few people know that Google Photos recognizes text in images and lets you search by it — type a term that was written on the board into search, and the photo turns up. Photographing whiteboards stops being a dumping ground and becomes a second memory.

## How to do it

1. Photograph whiteboards, projected slides, and paper handouts the normal way with your camera; photos back up to Google Photos (the app runs on both Android and iPhone).
2. When you need a photo, open Photos and type into **search** the word that was on the board: "integration by parts," "the Marshall Plan," "mitosis." The recognition even handles handwriting, as long as it's at least somewhat legible.
3. You can pull the text straight off a photo you find: in Photos, tap **Lens** and copy the text — more on that in the tip [text from paper straight into a text field](/tipy/mobil-text-z-fotky).
4. Just in case, set up an **album per subject** at the start of the semester and drop photos into it weekly. Even if search fails, you're flipping through thirty photos instead of three hundred.
5. Shoot straight-on and capture the whole board; what matters is sharp words, not artistic composition. Neither you nor the search will be able to read a blurry shot.

The same text-based search works in Photos on iPhone too (via Live Text) — the search field in the Photos app also scans text in images.

## A typical scenario

A week before a biochemistry exam, a student remembers that the lecturer drew the citric acid cycle diagram on the board differently — and more clearly — than the textbook. When was that? No idea. He types "citrate" into Photos and two seconds later has a photo from mid-October, including the notes the lecturer added to the arrows at the time. Without search he'd be scrolling through hundreds of photos, probably give up, and study from a worse source.

## What you get out of it

Photos from class turn from a digital junk drawer into a searchable archive — the more you photograph, the more valuable it gets, because search scales and scrolling doesn't. You'll find things you didn't even know you'd need. And it works retroactively: recognition runs over photos that have been sitting in your gallery for years.

## Pro tip

The same search works on screenshots too — photographed slides from an online lecture, an assignment from the school system, a schedule sent in a group chat. A screenshot is a photo like any other, so typing "due date" into Photos search will find "when's this due." For handwriting, expect limits: block-letter writing on a board is found reliably, a lecturer's fast cursive less so — so it helps to also have key terms in print somewhere in the photo, like a slide's corner label.
