Text just won't align, there's a gap between paragraphs you can't delete, a heading jumps to the next page for no obvious reason. The document “behaves strangely” — except it isn't behaving strangely at all, it just contains invisible characters you can't see: double spaces, tabs, page breaks, line breaks left over from pasting from the web. Word can reveal all of them with one button, and the mystery turns into a trivial cleanup.

## How to do it

1. On the **Home** tab, click the **¶** button (Show/Hide) — you'll find it in the Paragraph group. The keyboard shortcut is <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>8</kbd>; it may not register on some keyboard layouts, but the button always works.
2. The document lights up with markers: a dot in the middle of a line is a space, an arrow is a tab, ¶ marks the end of a paragraph, a bent arrow is a soft line break (Shift+Enter), and a “Page Break” shows up as a labeled line.
3. Now you can see the actual cause of the problem: two dots side by side = a double space, a column of ¶ marks stacked up = empty paragraphs padding out the text, a tab where there should have been a space.
4. Just delete the unneeded characters like regular text. When you're done, the same button turns the marks back off — they never make it into print or a PDF.
5. In Google Docs, the equivalent lives under **View → Show non-printing characters**.

Once you've made peace with the marks, you can pick a middle ground: in **File → Options → Display**, you can permanently show just some of them — say, paragraph marks and tabs — while dots for spaces stay hidden. The document's structure is visible at all times without the text getting overgrown with symbols.

## A typical scenario

A designer receives materials from a client “in Word, ready for layout.” The text looks aligned, but falls apart with every edit: indents are built out of five spaces each, paragraphs are separated by triple Enters, and one heading stays on its page only thanks to a manual page break left from an old version. Without the marks turned on, he'd be fixing the fallout and scratching his head; with them, within a few minutes he sees the whole construction and cleans it up systematically — spaces via [bulk find and replace](/tipy/word-ctrl-h-nahrazovani), empty paragraphs by deleting them.

## What you get out of it

An end to fighting an invisible opponent. Instead of randomly deleting and testing, you see exactly which character is causing the problem — and fixing a “broken” document shrinks from a frustrating half hour to a few minutes. Anyone who works with other people's text should know this button first.

## Pro tip

The most common source of chaos is documents assembled from text pasted in from websites and emails. A habit that solves the problem at the source: paste foreign text [without formatting via Ctrl+Shift+V](/tipy/docs-vlozeni-bez-formatu).
