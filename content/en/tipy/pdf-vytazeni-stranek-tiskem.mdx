You need to send just a few pages from a long PDF — one chapter of a contract, two pages of a price list, an attachment without the rest of the document. Splitting a PDF sounds like a job for a special tool, but a capability every computer already has is enough: the virtual print-to-PDF driver. In the print dialog, you specify a page range and “print” to a new file instead of paper — out comes a PDF containing exactly the pages you picked.

## How to do it

1. Open the PDF anywhere that supports printing — in your browser, Acrobat Reader, Preview — and press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>P</kbd> (on a Mac, <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>P</kbd>).
2. Choose **Microsoft Print to PDF** or “Save as PDF” as the printer (on a Mac, the **PDF → Save as PDF** button at the bottom left).
3. In the page field, enter what should stay in the new file: a range like `12-14`, a list like `1,5,30`, or a combination like `1,12-14`.
4. Confirm, name the new file — and you have a PDF with only the pages you selected. The original document stays untouched. The same trick deletes unwanted pages: “print” everything except them.

One limitation: printing produces a “flat” copy — clickable links and fillable form fields don't carry over. That's fine for sending excerpts of ordinary documents; split forms with a tool like PDF24 instead.

On a Mac there's an even slicker route that skips printing entirely: open the PDF in Preview, turn on the thumbnail sidebar, select the pages you want (hold <kbd>Cmd</kbd> for non-consecutive ones), and drag them out to the desktop — they become a new PDF file with all their properties intact.

## A typical scenario

An accountant has a forty-page PDF of a framework agreement from a vendor and needs to send a colleague just the two pages with the pricing schedule — the rest contains terms that have no business leaving the company. Ctrl+P, pages 36-37, print to PDF, send. Thirty seconds, no installs, no uploading the contract to a web service — and the colleague doesn't have to page through forty sheets to find two.

## What you get out of it

The ability to split or trim a PDF anytime, anywhere — even on a work computer where you can't install anything, and even with a sensitive document you don't want uploaded to the internet. It's one of those skills that takes a minute to learn and then pays off for years — and the recipient gets exactly what they need, not thirty pages of padding around it.
