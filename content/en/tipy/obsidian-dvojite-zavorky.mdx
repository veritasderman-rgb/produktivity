The value of your notes isn't in the individual texts, but in the connections between them. A note about a single concept, isolated on its own, is just a digital scrap of paper. That same note linked to ten others it relates to is part of a knowledge network you can browse from connection to connection — and that's exactly what double square brackets in Obsidian make possible.

## A typical scenario

Law student Barbora takes a separate note for every concept she covers — one note per legal institute, one law, one court ruling. Without links between them, these are isolated islands of text, and when reviewing before an exam she has to manually flip between files hunting for connections she wrote down elsewhere herself earlier. With linked notes, she just types the name of a related concept in double brackets anywhere in the text, and the notes connect. When she comes back to one concept before the exam, the backlinks panel immediately shows her everything related to it — and material she studied scattered across months suddenly comes together into a coherent whole.

## How to do it

1. Anywhere in a note, type two square brackets in a row and start typing the name of the note you want to link to.
2. Obsidian suggests existing notes based on what you type — pick the right one with the arrow keys and <kbd>Enter</kbd>, or keep typing your own title.
3. If a note with that title doesn't exist yet, the link is created anyway, and the note itself gets created the first time you click that link — you don't need to create empty files in advance for future concepts.
4. The **Backlinks** panel, usually in the sidebar, shows every place across your entire notes vault that links to the currently open note.
5. As your links accumulate over time, it's worth occasionally opening **Graph view** — a visual map of all your notes and their connections, showing which concepts are central and which remain isolated.
6. Hovering over a link without clicking it shows a preview of the target note right on the spot in most settings — handy for a quick reminder of a connection without having to leave the note you're currently in.

## The best tools

- **Double-bracket links in Obsidian** — a built-in, free feature that works instantly with no plugin needed.
- **Graph view** — a built-in visual display of your notes network, useful for spotting isolated or overcrowded nodes.
- **The Dataview plugin** — for more advanced work, it can automatically list notes based on what they link to or what properties they have.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** an estimated ten minutes a week from not hunting for connections manually and instead letting the backlinks panel surface them.
- **Better retention:** linking concepts as you write forces you to actively think about connections, which by itself helps you remember the material.
- **Visibility into gaps:** an isolated note with no links stands out at a glance and flags a topic you haven't yet connected to anything else.
- **Reusable notes:** a note written once can be linked from any number of other places, so it never has to be written again.

## Pro tip

Don't be afraid to link to notes that don't exist yet — creating them with the first click on the link is a natural way to gradually build your note structure around what you actually need, instead of planning the whole structure out in advance.
