You photograph a whiteboard after a meeting, a paper document, or a product — and then go through a whole ordeal getting the photo onto your computer: emailing it to yourself, through a “saved messages” chat, or with a cable. Yet Windows has a built-in solution: the Phone Link app. After a one-time pairing, you can see photos from your phone right on your computer just seconds after taking them — plus messages, notifications, and on many phones even calls placed from the computer.

## How to do it

1. On your computer, open the **Phone Link** app (it's built into Windows — find it via the Start menu).
2. On an Android phone, install the **Link to Windows** app and scan the pairing QR code from your computer's screen. Both devices need an internet connection.
3. After pairing, grant the requested permissions on your phone — photos, messages, notifications. You can decline any of them; the link still works in a reduced form.
4. In the computer app you'll then find tabs: **Photos** (recent shots from your phone — drag a photo straight into a document or email with the mouse), **Messages** (type texts with your computer keyboard), and **Notifications**.
5. iPhone support is more limited — it handles messages, calls, and notifications over Bluetooth, but without access to photos. iPhone owners are better served by a cloud photo gallery.

## A typical scenario

You're putting together a report and need a photo of an installation your technician took with your phone an hour ago. The old way: unlock the phone, find the photo, email it to yourself, switch to mail, download it, insert it. The new way: open Photos in Phone Link and drag the shot straight into the document. The phone can stay in your bag the whole time.

A parenting scene: you're deep in focused writing at work, your child's after-school activity ends, and they text asking who's picking them up today. You reply from your computer keyboard mid-sentence, no reaching for the phone, no losing your focus.

## What you get out of it

- **Photos without detours:** getting a photo from phone to document shrinks from five steps to a drag with the mouse.
- **Less reaching for your phone:** texts and notifications get handled from the computer — and the phone doesn't pull you into the mindless scrolling that often follows.
- **One workflow:** everything happens on the one screen you're sitting at anyway.

## Pro tip

In the app's settings, filter which notifications from your phone are allowed to show up on your computer — keep messages and calls, turn off social media and games. Otherwise you'll import every distraction that comes with the phone too. Combined with [Do Not Disturb](/tipy/win-nerusit-pravidla), the ideal setup is: the phone sits in your bag, what matters reaches you on screen, and everything else waits.
