People write a prompt, get a mediocre answer, and walk away disappointed. That's a mistake — the best results from AI don't come on the first try, but in the second or third round, when you dial the answer in to fit exactly what you need. Conversation is a faster path to a good result than trying to write the perfect prompt on the first attempt — just like when you first sketch out a task for a colleague and then refine it together.

This is especially true for requests where you're not 100% sure yourself what exactly you want — it's the answer itself that helps you figure it out.

## A typical scenario

A colleague has AI write a client summary, gets a generic, padded-out text, decides “AI just can't do this,” and writes it from scratch himself, even though it costs him another twenty minutes. Yet a small nudge would have been enough: “expand on point 3, cut the rest, make the tone less formal” — and thirty seconds later he'd have a text that never would have come out of a single prompt. The difference between disappointment and satisfaction is one more round of feedback, which takes less time than it would even take to just answer it yourself from scratch.

That's exactly how AI works best — like a partner who might not nail it on the first try, but almost always does on the second.

## How to do it

1. Start with a rough request — don't polish the wording, just send whatever comes to mind.
2. Treat the first answer as raw material, not the finished product.
3. Correct it specifically: “Expand on point 3. Cut the rest. Make the tone less formal.” The more specific the instruction, the better the edit.
4. If the result still isn't right, describe exactly what's off — “this sounds too formal,” “it's missing a number,” “this paragraph is unnecessary.”
5. After two or three rounds, you usually have a text you're happy with — and it took less time than trying to write the perfect prompt on the first attempt.
6. Don't be afraid to step back either — “go back to the previous version and only change this part” works just as well as moving forward.

## The best tools

- **ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini** — all built around conversation; iterating is their natural way of working, not an exception.
- **Editing selected text** (selecting a passage and editing it directly) — instead of rewriting the entire answer, you adjust just the chosen part.
- **Conversation history** — you return to a text in progress and keep refining it even after a break, without re-typing the request.
- **Saved favorite answers or messages** — if the app can flag a specific version as a reference, it's easier to come back to it later.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** a faster path to usable text than repeatedly writing a new prompt from scratch.
- **Sanity:** the end of “AI can't do this” — most flaws get fixed with one sentence of feedback, not a whole new request from the top.
- **Quality:** text refined step by step tends to be more precise and more distinctly yours than the first automatic answer.
- **A habit:** over time you learn which edits work best, and iterating gets faster even on brand-new topics.

## Pro tip

When you're not sure exactly what bothers you about an answer, try asking AI for several variants at once: “Give me three versions — one shorter, one more formal, one with an example.” It's easier to pick the direction that fits than to have to formulate it yourself, and the differences between the versions will often show you what you were actually looking for.
