Emailing a presentation from your Mac to your iPhone is a pointless detour — you have to write yourself an email, wait for it to send, then download it on your phone, all over the internet even though both devices are sitting side by side on the same desk. AirDrop sends the file directly between devices with no compression, no size limit, and no need to even be connected to the internet.

## A typical scenario

You finish a presentation on your Mac and need it on your iPhone so you can show it to a client straight from your phone. Over email that means writing yourself a message, attaching the file, waiting for it to send, switching to your phone, opening the email, downloading the attachment — and for larger files, running into an attachment size limit. Over AirDrop, you just select the file in Finder, share it, and confirm receipt on your phone within seconds — the file travels directly between devices, not through some third-party server.

## How to do it

1. In Finder, right-click the file (or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + click) and select **Share → AirDrop**.
2. Make sure both devices have **Bluetooth** and **Wi-Fi** turned on — AirDrop needs both, even though the transfer itself doesn't go over the internet.
3. In the AirDrop window, select the target device, which appears in the list once both devices are close enough to each other.
4. On the receiving device, an acceptance request pops up — confirm it; between your own devices signed into the same Apple ID, it's accepted automatically without confirmation.
5. AirDrop works the same way in reverse — straight from a photo on your iPhone into a folder on your Mac, or between two Macs.
6. If a device isn't showing up in the list, check **Settings → General → AirDrop** to make sure receiving isn't restricted to contacts only, which the other party may not be part of.

## The best tools

- **AirDrop (built into macOS and iOS)** — free, no internet required, no file size limit, works between any Apple devices.
- **Nearby Share** — the equivalent for Android and Windows devices, if you're sending a file outside the Apple ecosystem.
- **Shared iCloud Drive folder or Documents** — for files that multiple people need ongoing access to, not just a one-time transfer.
- **Universal Clipboard** — if you just need to move a short bit of text or a link rather than a whole file, copy and paste between devices without AirDrop at all.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** transferring a large file takes seconds instead of minutes spent uploading and downloading through email or the cloud.
- **Quality:** no compression of photos and videos the way email or most messaging apps do — the file arrives exactly as it was.
- **No dependence on the internet:** it works even without a Wi-Fi connection to a router, as long as both devices have Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on.
- **Less email clutter:** you stop accumulating dozens of "to self" emails with attachments you'll delete anyway.

## Pro tip

If AirDrop isn't working reliably between your own devices, make sure both are signed into the same Apple ID and have **Handoff** turned on in Settings — without it, automatic receiving between your own devices may not kick in.
