Research without sources is just an opinion. A classic AI chat gives you an answer, but you have no way to check where it came from or whether it's current. AI search engines with citations solve exactly that — they search the web, answer, and attach a link to every claim that you can click and verify yourself.

## A typical scenario

Klára, a student, is writing a term paper and needs to compare three approaches to a topic. When she asks a regular chat with no search enabled, she gets a smooth, convincing-sounding answer — but has no way to verify whether the numbers and claims match reality, or whether the model partly made them up. She doesn't dare put it in her paper without further checking, which costs her another hour of hunting on her own.

With an AI search engine that cites sources, she gets the same answer, but every sentence links to a specific page or study. Klára clicks through three or four key citations, confirms they check out, and uses the citation directly in her paper. Research that would otherwise take an hour of manual searching is done in ten minutes, verification included.

## How to do it

1. Choose an AI tool that can search the web and cite sources — not just answer from memory.
2. Ask specific questions, not general ones — "compare tools X and Y for a team of 10" gets a better result than "which is better, X or Y."
3. For key claims and numbers, always click through the citation and confirm the source actually says what the answer claims — models sometimes interpret a source more loosely than they should.
4. If an answer only hints at something, follow up specifically: "expand on source 3 — what exactly did that study measure, and on what sample?"
5. Pay attention to the date of the source — for fast-moving topics (prices, product versions, current figures), how old a citation is matters as much as its content.
6. Collect citations as you go into your notes with the link attached, so you don't have to hunt them down again when writing the paper or report.

## The best tools

- **Perplexity** — the best-known AI search engine built directly around citations, a good default starting point for any research task.
- **ChatGPT with search** and **Claude with search** — everyday chat tools now let you turn on web search and cite sources right in the answer.
- **Google AI Mode / Gemini with search** — useful when you want sources tied directly into classic Google search.
- **Elicit or Consensus** — specialized in academic studies, useful for students and research where the main concern is scientific sources.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** an estimated 20–30 minutes on a single research task compared to manually searching and comparing sources one by one.
- **Verifiability:** claims you can actually stand behind, because you know where they came from — important for both term papers and work decisions.
- **Lower risk of error:** a classic chat without citations sometimes states things confidently that don't hold up; citations let you catch that right away.

## Pro tip

Don't take the first answer as final — dig deeper into whatever matters most to you. "Expand on source 3" or "find more recent data" moves you from a surface-level overview to research you can actually rely on. And if an answer sounds too smooth and clear-cut on a topic that's actually contested, that's a signal it deserves independent verification outside the AI tool itself.
