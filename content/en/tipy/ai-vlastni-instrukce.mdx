If you find yourself telling AI "shorter," "in English please," or "skip the opening paragraph" every single time, tell it once, for good. Custom instructions are a few sentences that AI remembers and automatically applies to every new conversation — you don't have to type them again and again.

## A typical scenario

Martin, a project manager, opens dozens of AI conversations a day — for planning, for emails, for quick summaries. Every time, it first answers in a tone that's too formal when he wants something punchier, then in a long paragraph instead of short bullet points, and finally tacks on a few extra pleasantries. Martin manually corrects the same things every day: "shorter," "less formal," "no intro" — and this small correction repeats itself in nearly every new conversation, because AI doesn't remember anything between separate chats.

Once he sets up custom instructions, AI knows from the very first reply that he's a project manager, wants concise answers in bullet points, and no fluff around them. He saves dozens of small corrections a day — and above all, that annoying first answer he always ended up throwing away anyway.

## How to do it

1. In the AI's settings (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini), find the **custom instructions** section — it's usually labeled custom instructions, personalization, or "About me."
2. Describe yourself: your profession, field, what you typically work on.
3. Describe your preferences: what language you want answers in, how long they should be (short bullet points, or more detailed), what tone works for you.
4. Mention what annoys you — unnecessary opening lines like "great question," excessive emoji use, or overly cautious phrasing.
5. Save it — from that point on, the instructions apply to every new conversation without you having to repeat them.
6. The instructions aren't set in stone — once you notice something missing or something that bothers you, go back and update them. It's a living document, not a one-time setup.

## The best tools

- **Custom instructions in ChatGPT** — the personalization section in settings, works across all conversations.
- **Claude Preferences / Styles** — a similar feature in Claude, which also lets you save a specific writing style.
- **Gemini — Gemini personalization** — in your Google account settings, and it carries over into Gemini in Gmail and Docs too.
- **A project's system prompt** — if you work in Projects/Custom GPTs, set instructions right there for a specific type of work (say, just for writing code).
- **A separate profile for work and personal use** — if you use AI for very different purposes, consider separate accounts or projects with their own instructions for each, so the preferences don't get mixed up.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** you skip repeatedly typing the same corrections — for people who use AI daily, that's an estimated 5 minutes or more saved every day.
- **Consistent output:** every answer already has the right language, length, and tone, no tuning needed.
- **Less annoyance:** you don't have to re-explain who you are and what you want every time — AI already knows.

## Pro tip

Write the instructions as if you were briefing a new colleague on their first day at work — specific and concrete, not general. "Answer concisely" helps less than "answer in bullet points, five maximum, no opening sentence." The more concrete the example of behavior you give, the more reliably AI will stick to it — general instructions tend to get "diluted" over a longer conversation, while a specific rule stays valid even after the twentieth message.
