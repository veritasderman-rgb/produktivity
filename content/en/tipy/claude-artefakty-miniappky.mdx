The same scene keeps repeating in companies: someone needs a simple tool — a calculator, a form, a spreadsheet that computes something — and the answer is “a developer would have to build that, put it on the backlog.” Three months later everyone's forgotten about it, and people keep calculating things in Excel, each with a different version. Artifacts skip that queue entirely. **Claude can build a working mini-app or page right inside the chat, and you share it with a link — no installation, no developer, no waiting.** An hour of conversation instead of a day of coding. Where this way of working fits among the other levels of using AI is covered in [the chapter on AI and automation](/prirucka/ai-a-automatizace).

## A typical scenario

Tomáš, a sales director, has six salespeople and one persistent problem: everyone calculates a quote price their own way. Base rate, express surcharge, volume discount, shipping. The result is three different prices for the same job and roughly two complaints a month along the lines of “but my colleague quoted me a different amount.” Tomáš had wanted an internal calculator for two years; IT estimated a week of work and it never made it up the queue.

He spends an afternoon with Claude. He describes the rates, the discount rules, and what the output should contain. In ten minutes he has a first version of the calculator right in the chat — enter the parameters, out comes the price. He spends the next half hour refining it: adds a shipping field, disables discounts above twenty percent, adds a summary he can paste straight into a quote. Then he sends the salespeople a link. From Monday on, the whole team calculates the same way, and the pricing disputes disappear from the agenda.

## How to do it

1. **Describe the tool in words, not a developer spec.** Say what it's for, what the user enters, and what should come out. “I want a quote calculator: I enter the quantity, product type, and deadline, it calculates the price according to these rules, and writes a summary I can paste into an email.”
2. **Supply real rules and one example.** Rates, discount thresholds, exceptions — and one sample calculation with its result. Claude uses it to check its math is right, and you'll spot a mistake immediately.
3. **Iterate in conversation.** The first version won't be final, and it isn't meant to be. Give feedback the normal way: “add a shipping field,” “don't let the quantity go negative,” “round the result to whole dollars.” Each tweak is one sentence, and the new version is ready to try right away.
4. **Test the edges.** Enter zero, an enormous number, a nonsensical combination. Nobody will use a tool that breaks on the first typo.
5. **Share it with a link.** Send colleagues the finished artifact as a link. Nothing to install — it opens in a browser, on a phone too.
6. **Mind your data.** Don't put personal, financial, or health information into the artifact — work with rates and rules, not a client database. When you do need to work with sensitive data, do it only on a paid or business account with contractual data protection, and anonymize everything that can be anonymized.

## Where this comes in handy most often

- **An internal calculator** — quote prices, margins, return on investment. The whole team calculates the same way.
- **An onboarding quiz** — ten questions on policy or the product lineup, with results at the end.
- **A simple tracker** — an overview of process steps, a pre-launch checklist for a campaign, a habit counter.
- **An interactive explainer** — a page where the viewer moves a parameter themselves and watches the result change; invaluable for training sessions and for defending a proposal.

## The best tools

- **Artifacts in Claude** — the tool is built right in the conversation and shared with a link; nothing to install.
- **Projects in Claude** — save rates, price lists, and rules here; Claude knows them in every conversation, and the artifact is built on top of them.
- **Claude Code** — once the tool outgrows a single page and needs to live in a company repository; it works with files and git.
- **Forms and spreadsheets (Google Forms, Sheets)** — an honest alternative wherever collecting responses is all you need, with no extra logic.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** a tool that would take a developer a day comes together in an afternoon hour. The IT queue gets shorter by everything that never should have been on it.
- **Consistency:** instead of six versions of a spreadsheet, one link that everyone shares.
- **Independence:** you don't have to ask anyone or wait for capacity to free up. You make the tweak yourself, in the conversation.
- **Better specs:** even when a proper app gets built eventually, the artifact serves as a prototype that shows you what you actually want.

## Pro tip

Before you send the tool out, have Claude list the rules it's calculating by and go through them line by line. A mistake in a rule is silent — it doesn't crash, it just quietly returns the wrong number. And hold the line that applies to every automation: **an artifact may calculate and suggest; the decision and sending the quote stay with a human.** When more than one person uses the tool, add the date rates were last updated — it'll save you the unpleasant discovery that the team's been calculating with last year's price list for six months.
