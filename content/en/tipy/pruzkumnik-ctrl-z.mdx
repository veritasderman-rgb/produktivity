Everyone knows Ctrl+Z as undo while typing text. Few people know the same shortcut also works in File Explorer — and undoes file operations. Accidentally dragged a folder inside another folder and have no idea where? Renamed the wrong file? Deleted something you didn't mean to? Ctrl+Z puts it all back the way it was, no searching, no panic.

## How to do it

1. If you click wrong, press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd> in an Explorer window (or on the desktop) — the last file operation gets undone.
2. It works on a **move** (the file returns to its original folder), a **copy** (the copy gets deleted), a **rename** (the original name comes back), and a **delete to the recycle bin** (the file is restored to its place).
3. Pressing it repeatedly undoes further back — Windows remembers a history of the last several operations.
4. If you're not sure exactly what will get undone, right-click an empty spot in the folder: the **Undo** entry also shows a description of the action, like “Undo Move.”
5. There's a redo too: <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Y</kbd> repeats the undone action.

## A typical scenario

A classic on shared drives: you go to click the “Invoices” folder, but your mouse twitches and the folder vanishes — you accidentally dropped it inside one of the neighboring folders. Which one? Nobody knows. Without Ctrl+Z, that's followed by a quarter hour of searching nearby folders, or a sheepish question in the team chat asking if anyone “sees Invoices.” With Ctrl+Z, you press two keys and the folder's back like nothing happened.

The same lifesaver works at home: a kid “tidied up” the desktop, you renamed the wrong file, or during a quick cleanup you selected one file too many for deletion. One shortcut, no stress.

## What you get out of it

- **A rescue at an unpleasant moment:** a lost folder move on a shared drive is one of the most common small disasters of office work — and it's fixed with two keys instead of a quarter hour of searching.
- **Confidence for bulk actions too:** undo works on whole batches — bulk-renaming a hundred files or moving several folders at once undoes just as reliably as a single file.
- **The courage to tidy up:** knowing every step can be undone means you sort and move files faster, without anxious hesitation.
- **Time:** one shortcut instead of searching through folders and restoring files one by one from the recycle bin.

## Pro tip

Watch for one exception: Ctrl+Z won't undo a permanent delete via <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Delete</kbd>, which bypasses the recycle bin. And on shared drives, undo works most reliably right after the action — the sooner you press Ctrl+Z, the better. If it's too late, try the recycle bin, or version history on OneDrive or SharePoint.
