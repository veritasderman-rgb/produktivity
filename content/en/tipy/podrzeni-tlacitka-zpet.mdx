You clicked your way five pages deep from a search result and now want to get back to the start. Click, wait for it to load, click, wait, click… But the back button hides a faster path: **hold it down** instead of clicking, and the tab's whole history unfolds — you jump straight to where you need to be in a single pick. It works in Chrome, Edge, and Firefox, and almost nobody knows about it.

## How to do it

1. Click the back arrow in the top left and **hold the mouse button down** for about a second (right-clicking works too).
2. A list unfolds of every page you visited in this tab — newest at the top, with site icons for quick orientation.
3. Pick the target page and you're there: one jump instead of five, one page load instead of five.
4. The forward arrow works the same way — hold it down to see the pages you navigated back from.
5. There are also two keyboard shortcuts that skip the trip to the button with your mouse: <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>←</kbd> steps back, <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>→</kbd> steps forward (on a Mac, <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>→</kbd>).
6. One catch to watch for: history belongs to the tab. A link opened in a new tab starts with an empty history — if the back button doesn't respond, you're in a new tab and your original trail is still sitting in the old one.
7. The unfolded list shows roughly the last ten stops; when you need to go further back, there's a link to the full browsing history at the bottom, where you'll find absolutely everything.

## A typical scenario

You're shopping for a laptop: from a comparison site you opened a detail page, from there a review, from the review a discussion thread, and from that some specs. Now you want back to the comparison page to try another model. Four clicks on back means four waits for pages full of ads. Holding the button down instead: a list appears, the comparison page is fifth from the top, click — and you're there.

The same applies working with an internal system or documentation: clicking through takes you deep, and getting back “to the hub” is something you do all day. Once you get used to holding the back button instead of clicking it, returns stop feeling like work.

## What you get out of it

Going back several pages shrinks from a string of clicks and waits down to a single jump. It's a small thing, but you do it many times a day — and Alt+arrows add a return trip with zero mouse movement at all.

## Pro tip

When you need to get back to a page you closed along with its whole tab, the back-button list won't help — that's a job for [Ctrl+Shift+T](/tipy/ctrl-shift-t-obnoveni-panelu), which restores the whole tab, history included. Together, the two techniques cover practically every “wait, how did I even get here?”
