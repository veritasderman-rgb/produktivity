When you want to show a coworker how to do something, a video says more than five paragraphs of text. Describing in words where to click and in what order is slow, and readers often get it wrong anyway. macOS actually has screen recording built right in — you don't need to install any extra app just for a short tutorial.

## A typical scenario

A colleague from accounting asks, for the third time this month, how to export a report in the internal system. Writing it out as text means fifteen minutes of typing, and it still usually ends with "I don't see that button anywhere." Calling and sharing your screen means having to line up both of your calendars.

Instead, it takes two minutes: start recording, click through the steps while narrating out loud, and send a link to the video. Your colleague watches it whenever it suits her, and the follow-up questions mostly disappear — she can see exactly where you're clicking.

## How to do it

1. Press <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>5</kbd> — a control panel appears at the bottom of the screen.
2. Choose what you want to capture: a still image of the whole screen, a still image of a selection, or video — of the whole screen or just a selected area.
3. In the **Options** menu, set where the file should be saved (Desktop, Documents, Clipboard), whether to record audio from the microphone, and a countdown before starting (no delay, 5 or 10 seconds) — useful when you still need to switch to the window you want to record.
4. Click **Record** (for an area selection, drag first to mark its boundaries) to start recording.
5. Stop recording anytime by clicking the stop icon in the menu bar at the top of the screen (it looks like a square in a circle).
6. The video saves to the chosen location as a .mov file and opens in a preview, from which you can trim or rename it right away.
7. The same panel can be brought up again and again — it remembers the last settings used (recording type, microphone, location), so your second and later recordings go even faster.

## The best tools

- **Built-in screen recording** (<kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>5</kbd>) — free, works with no installation, plenty for quick tutorials.
- **QuickTime Player → New Screen Recording** — the same feature available from the app's menu too, handy if you forget the shortcut.
- **Loom** — a popular tool for sharing short videos with an automatic link, also handles a webcam in the corner of the recording and quick comments under the video.
- **CleanShot X** — a paid tool for those who record often and want automatic blurring of sensitive information or a longer recording history on top.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** one short video often replaces several rounds of explaining over email or chat — easily tens of minutes a week for recurring questions.
- **Fewer misunderstandings:** your coworker sees exactly the screen and the sequence of steps you see, with no room for guessing.
- **Reusability:** once you've recorded a walkthrough, you can send it to anyone else who asks the same thing, instead of explaining it fresh every time.
- **A lighter calendar:** no more scheduling a shared call with screen sharing just for a short explanation that an asynchronous video handles just as well.

## Pro tip

Before recording, check in **System Settings → Control Center** that the screen-recording icon is added to the menu bar — then you can start recording even without the keyboard shortcut, which comes in handy on someone else's computer or when you can't remember the shortcut.
