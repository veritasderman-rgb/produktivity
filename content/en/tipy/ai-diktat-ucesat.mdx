Talking is faster than typing — we typically speak around 130 words a minute, but barely type 40. The catch is that a spoken stream of thoughts tends to be chaos: tangents, repetition, unfinished sentences that nobody would want to read as written text. That's exactly where AI shines — you supply the content and the energy, it supplies the structure and readability.

## A typical scenario

Petr has a meeting in twenty minutes and needs to prepare a summary of the quarterly results, but there's no time to write it out. Without this tip, he'd sit at the keyboard, form sentences slowly, delete and rewrite until there's no time left for anything else, and walk into the meeting with a half-finished draft. With this tip, on the way to the meeting he dictates into his phone everything that comes to mind about the results, projects, and risks, then has AI organize the text with a single prompt — by the time the meeting starts, he has a finished, readable draft, having spent no more than a few minutes at the computer on final touches.

## How to do it

1. Start dictation — <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>H</kbd> on Windows, the built-in dictation feature on Mac, the microphone right in the keyboard on mobile.
2. Speak freely, without worrying about structure — jump between topics, repeat yourself, leave sentences unfinished. The goal is to get the thoughts out, not to sound like you're on the radio.
3. Paste the transcribed text into AI with a prompt like: “Organize this into structured text. Keep my words and ideas, only fix grammar and punctuation, group related thoughts under headings.”
4. Specify the output format as needed — bullet points, continuous text, an email, meeting notes.
5. Review the result and adjust any spots where AI changed the meaning. Five minutes of talking turns into a usable document with just a few extra minutes of work.
6. If the first version doesn't match your tone, try one round of refinement — “make it less formal” or “add bullet points for the main items” — instead of rewriting the text by hand yourself.

## The best tools

- **Your system's built-in dictation** (<kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>H</kbd> on Windows, dictation on Mac, voice input on the mobile keyboard) — free and ready to go, no extra app to install.
- **ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini** — for polishing the transcript into a final, readable text; any of them can sort your thoughts under headings.
- **Otter.ai** — good for longer recordings and meetings, can distinguish between multiple speakers and offers a transcript right away.
- **Note apps with dictation** (Notes on iPhone, Google Keep) — for quickly capturing an idea on the go, before you process it later at home.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** an email or draft that would take twenty minutes to write, you can dictate and clean up in five.
- **Sanity:** no more blank-page paralysis or hunting for the first sentence — talking flows on its own, even when you're not sure exactly how to start.
- **Quality:** thoughts captured in the moment tend to be more natural and specific than ones squeezed out at the keyboard under pressure.
- **Reclaimed time:** a drive, a walk, or a wait in line turns into productive time that would otherwise be lost.

## Pro tip

Dictate even half-formed ideas you're stuck on — often just describing the problem out loud shows you what's actually missing, without having to put it in writing. If you're dictating work content with sensitive details, like client names or contract numbers, review the transcript before feeding it to AI and replace specific identifiers with a more general description — organizing the text doesn't depend on them.
