A PDF form lands in your inbox — an application, a questionnaire, a request. The reflex kicks in: print it, fill it out with a pen, scan it (read: photograph it on your phone under bad lighting), and send it back. That whole paper loop is usually unnecessary: a PDF opened in your browser can be filled in right on screen and saved. Even when the form has no clickable fields at all — Edge and Firefox can type text anywhere on the page.

## How to do it

1. Open the PDF in your browser — just drag the file into the window, or click straight on it if it arrived as a link or email attachment.
2. If the form has **active fields**, just click into them and type; checkboxes and dropdowns work too. Tab moves you comfortably between fields. Chrome, Edge, and Firefox all handle this.
3. If the form **has no active fields** (it's just an “image” of a form), use Edge or Firefox: in the toolbar above the document you'll find **Add text** (Edge) or **Text** (Firefox). Click a spot in the document and type — font size and color are both adjustable.
4. Save the finished document using the **download/save icon in the PDF toolbar** — that's what writes the filled-in data into the file. To be safe, reopen it after saving and confirm the data stuck.
5. Handle the signature with the drawing tool (Edge: **Draw**, Firefox: **Draw**) — it's clunky with a mouse, but good enough for an ordinary application form; on a touchscreen it's comfortable.

## A typical scenario

The evening before the deadline, a parent is filling out a camp registration form. The printer's out of toner — of course. Instead of panicking, they open the PDF in Edge, fill in the fields, add a note about medication with the Add text tool, draw a signature, save, and email it off. Ten minutes, no paper — and the organizer gets a legible document instead of a photo of a crumpled sheet.

A teacher fills out self-assessment forms and reports the school sends as PDFs the same way — and a freelancer handles client questionnaires and disclosures the same way. A form filled in once also stays on the computer: next year, they just reopen it and update the date.

## What you get out of it

The print–pen–scan loop disappears for most forms anyone sends you. The result is legible, saved, and reusable — and you don't need a printer, a scanner, or any paid PDF editor.

## Pro tip

When the other party insists on a handwritten signature, you don't have to print the whole document just for that: sign with a black marker on plain paper, [photograph the signature with your phone](/tipy/skenujte-telefonem), and drop the image into the PDF (Firefox does this via its add-image tool). Once you've made a signature, you can reuse it again and again.
