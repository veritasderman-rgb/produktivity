Paper stops existing the moment you photograph it properly. The Notes app, which every iPhone has had built in from the factory, hides a full-featured document scanner — a straight photo gets automatically cropped, straightened, and brightened like a scanned page, not like an ordinary photo from your camera roll.

## A typical scenario

You get a paper invoice from a vendor, or a signed contract you need to send to your accountant. Photographing it "quickly" with your phone means a crooked corner, a shadow from your own hand, and a file nobody wants to open. Through the scanner in Notes, you photograph the same document and the app finds the edges on its own, straightens the shot into a right angle, and sharpens the text — the result looks like it went through an actual desktop scanner, and it's a PDF right away, not a photo from your camera roll.

## How to do it

1. Open the **Notes** app and create a new note (or open an existing one).
2. Tap the camera icon in the toolbar and choose **Scan Documents**.
3. Point your phone at the document — the app detects the edges on its own and takes the photo automatically after a moment, or press the shutter manually if you want control over the exact moment of capture.
4. The scan is cropped and straightened right away; if the result isn't quite right, you can drag the crop corners with your finger to adjust them manually.
5. For a multi-page document, keep photographing additional pages — they'll all end up in one file.
6. When you're done, tap **Save** — a PDF is created right inside the note, which you can then share via AirDrop, email, or message.

## The best tools

- **The scanner in Notes (iOS)** — built in, free, no other app needed.
- **Google Drive / Google Lens** — a similar auto-cropping scanner for Android users or anyone who wants the scan straight in the cloud.
- **Adobe Scan** — for more demanding needs: text recognition, automatic sorting into folders, and export directly to searchable PDF.
- **Microsoft Lens** — a good alternative for anyone working in Microsoft 365 who wants the scan straight into OneDrive.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** no hunting for a scanner or waiting to get to a printer with scanning built in — you have the document done in seconds, wherever you happen to be standing.
- **Output quality:** a straightened, cropped, legible document instead of a crooked, glare-filled photo nobody wants to print or forward.
- **Organization:** multi-page documents end up in one file, not ten separate photos in your camera roll.
- **Independence from paper:** you can toss or file away the original once it's scanned — the digital copy is just as good.

## Pro tip

Scan on a flat surface with contrasting background (a dark desk under a white sheet of paper) — the algorithm finds the edges far more reliably than on a surface that's a similar color to the document. And if you'll need the scan somewhere other than Notes, share it straight from there — the note stays behind as a backup.
