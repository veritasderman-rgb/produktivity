Two things damage a lithium battery the most: heat, and sitting at 100% charge for long stretches. A laptop that spends the workweek plugged into a dock or charger has both — and after two or three years it holds half its original capacity, even though you "barely ran it on battery." Manufacturers know this, which is why most laptops and phones have an option that stops charging somewhere between 60 and 85 percent. It's just hidden, and off by default. Turn it on and the battery ages much more slowly — while you save the full 100% for the days you actually need it.

## How to do it

1. Find the option for your laptop — it's usually in the manufacturer's app and named differently every time: Lenovo's Vantage app has **Conservation Mode** or adjustable charge thresholds, ASUS's MyASUS has **Battery Health Charging**, Dell's Power Manager has charge management, Surface has **Smart charging**. If you're not sure, search the manufacturer's app for "battery" or "charging."
2. Set the limit around 80% (some manufacturers use a fixed value). The laptop charges up to the limit and holds there.
3. On a Mac, leave **Optimized Battery Charging** turned on (System Settings → Battery) — the system learns your routine and keeps the battery at 80% on its own until you actually need a full charge.
4. Phones work the same way: newer iPhones have a settable limit right in **Settings → Battery → Charging**, and Samsung has **battery protection** in battery care. A phone that charges overnight is exactly the case where a limit pays off.
5. Before a trip or a day full of meetings, turn the limit off temporarily and charge to full — that's exactly what the switch is there for.

## A typical scenario

Graphic designer Tom works from home, and his laptop has sat permanently docked for two years. A battery that used to last eight hours suddenly barely makes it through two — precisely because it spent two years charged to 100% and warm. His colleague with the same model turned on the charge limit in her first week; two years later her battery still holds close to new, and she just charges to full the night before a business trip.

## What you get out of it

On today's thin laptops and phones, the battery is essentially unrepairable without a service visit — a charge limit is the simplest way to delay an expensive replacement, and the day your device can't make it through a morning unplugged. It costs you nothing: you won't notice the difference at your desk, and you can turn on full capacity any time you're planning to need it.

## Pro tip

You can check your battery's current health: on a Mac, System Settings → Battery shows battery condition; on iPhone, capacity as a percentage sits in Settings → Battery. On Windows, generate a detailed report with `powercfg /batteryreport` from the command line — it produces an HTML file with capacity history, where the aging shows up in black and white.
