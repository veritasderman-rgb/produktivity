A normal mouse selection in Word runs by lines: from wherever you start to wherever you stop, including everything in between. But sometimes you need to select a vertical strip instead — say the dashes at the start of twenty lines, a column of numbers in text pasted from an email, or the first few characters of every line. Holding down <kbd>Alt</kbd>, Word selects a rectangle instead: only what's inside the drawn box, regardless of lines.

## How to do it

1. Hold <kbd>Alt</kbd> (<kbd>Option</kbd> on a Mac) and only then start dragging with the mouse across the text.
2. Instead of the usual line-based selection, a rectangular area gets selected — a vertical strip of characters across lines.
3. You then work with the selection normally: <kbd>Delete</kbd> removes it, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>C</kbd> copies it, and you can even change its formatting all at once — bold the first word of every line, for instance.
4. Mind the order: hold Alt first, then click and drag. If you click first, Word interprets the gesture differently.
5. If you don't want to let go of the keyboard, you can also turn on block selection without the mouse: <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>F8</kbd> starts vertical-selection mode, arrow keys expand the rectangle, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> exits the mode.

You can also type directly into a selected block — whatever you type replaces the highlighted rectangle on the first line. Most often, though, you'll simply delete the block or reformat it.

## A typical scenario

Tom receives an emailed list where every line starts with “> ” from being forwarded. There are forty lines. Deleting them one by one means forty clicks and forty presses of Delete; [find and replace](/tipy/word-ctrl-h-nahrazovani) would work too, but he'd need to get the pattern exactly right. With Alt+drag, he selects the vertical strip of every “> ” at once and one press of Delete leaves the whole list clean.

The same trick is a lifesaver for text pasted from a PDF or terminal, where line numbers or spaces ended up at the start of every line — a rectangular selection grabs them all at once.

## What you get out of it

Edits that would mean dozens of repeated clicks get done in one motion. It's not something you need every hour, but when it comes up — broken pasted text, a column of unwanted characters — it saves you five minutes of mechanical work and, more importantly, keeps you from losing patience by line forty. And because the same gesture is known to developer editors too, it's a skill that carries over between tools.

## Pro tip

Word has other little-known selections too: <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + click anywhere in a sentence selects the whole sentence (handy when rewriting a complex one), and a triple click selects an entire paragraph. Together with Alt+drag, they cover most situations where a plain mouse drag is needlessly slow.
