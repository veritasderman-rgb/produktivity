A status nobody sets is a status nobody respects. Most people in Teams either leave the green dot on from morning to night, or leave Do Not Disturb permanently on, which coworkers stop paying attention to after a few days — because they can't tell if it's really active right now, or just a forgotten setting from yesterday.

## A typical scenario

You need an uninterrupted hour to finish a report. You turn on Do Not Disturb, but forget about it, and the status stays set even after lunch, once you've long been available again. Coworkers gradually learn that your Do Not Disturb doesn't mean anything, and start messaging you anyway — the feature meant to protect your focus stops working entirely.

With a set duration, the status returns to normal automatically the moment you're actually available again — you don't have to remember anything, and coworkers see the exact time they can expect you back.

## How to do it

1. Click your profile avatar in the top left and choose your status from the menu.
2. Look for the **Duration** option in the status menu — instead of a permanent switch, this lets you set how long the status should apply.
3. Choose **Do Not Disturb** with a duration, say “1 hour,” for a specific focused work block. Once that time is up, the status reverts automatically to what it was before (usually Available).
4. For a recurring need — say, a focus block every morning — consider also setting a focus block directly in your calendar, which links to your Teams status automatically.
5. Set your priority contacts (your manager, a backup, a key client) under **Settings → Privacy → Manage priority access** — their messages and calls get through even with Do Not Disturb on, so you don't risk missing something genuinely urgent.
6. For longer absences (vacation, illness), use the separate **Schedule an out of office message** feature, which additionally auto-replies to anyone who messages you.

## The best tools

- **Teams' built-in status with duration** — the fastest and most natural option, works without any additional setup.
- **Priority access** (Settings → Privacy) — a safeguard so Do Not Disturb doesn't block genuinely important contacts.
- **Focus blocks in your Outlook calendar** — link automatically with your Teams status and handle focus systematically, not just one-off.
- **Out-of-office messages** for vacation or illness — inform coworkers even beyond the moment they notice your status.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** an estimated 20–40 minutes a day of extra uninterrupted work, since coworkers see a clear, trustworthy signal and don't message unnecessarily.
- **A status people actually trust:** coworkers take a status with a duration seriously, because they can see exactly when it expires — unlike a permanent Do Not Disturb, which they'll eventually stop respecting.
- **Less context switching:** shorter, less frequent interruptions mean fewer returns to unfinished work where you have to pick up the thread again.
- **Peace of mind for the other side too:** coworkers don't have to guess whether to interrupt you or wait — your status tells them for you.

## Pro tip

Alongside Do Not Disturb itself, add a short reason and time to your status text — something like “focusing until 12:00, then available.” Even though the status icon alone only shows so much, that extra text in the personal message field helps coworkers judge whether it's worth waiting or better to just send an email.
