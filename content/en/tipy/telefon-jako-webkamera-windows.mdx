A laptop's built-in webcam is usually the worst thing its maker left inside it — grainy image, bad colors, dim light. Yet the best camera you own is sitting in your pocket: your phone's camera. Windows 11 can connect an Android phone wirelessly as a full-fledged webcam — it shows up in the camera list next to the built-in one, and Teams, Zoom, Meet, and anything else can pick it. No third-party apps to install, no cable.

## How to do it

1. Open **Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Mobile devices** and turn on **Allow this PC to access your mobile devices**.
2. Click **Manage devices** and add your phone — Windows walks you through pairing via the **Link to Windows** app, which you install from the Play Store. You need Android 9 or newer, and both devices on the same Wi-Fi network.
3. On the paired phone, turn on the **Use as connected camera** toggle.
4. In your video call app, choose the phone as your camera in settings — the same way you'd pick an external webcam. During a call you can switch between the front and rear camera, or pause the feed.
5. Prop or mount the phone on a small tripod next to your monitor, roughly at eye level, and plug it into a charger — streaming the camera draws a fair amount of power and the battery might not survive a long meeting. Turn on do-not-disturb on it too, so notifications don't pop up in the shot.

## A typical scenario

Consultant Mark has an important presentation for a client. His built-in webcam produces a gray silhouette with a window behind him; the phone's rear camera, by contrast, delivers a sharp, well-lit picture even in an ordinarily lit home office. Mark turns on the connected camera, props the phone on a tripod behind his monitor, and switches the camera in Teams to the phone. The difference is obvious at a glance — and the client sees him, not a silhouette. A teacher gets the same benefit during online lessons, using the rear camera to show an experiment or a worksheet on the desk.

## What you get out of it

A noticeably better image on every call without buying an external webcam — sharper detail, truer colors, and much better results in low light. Switching to the rear camera also gives you a “second camera” for showing things on your desk, something a regular webcam can't do. And because the phone is freely repositionable, you can pick a better angle than the one a camera bolted to the top edge of a laptop display offers.

## Pro tip

The feature is part of newer Windows 11 builds — if you don't see the Mobile devices section in Settings, let the system update. On a Mac there's an equivalent for iPhone: see the tip [iPhone as a webcam](/tipy/mac-iphone-webkamera).
