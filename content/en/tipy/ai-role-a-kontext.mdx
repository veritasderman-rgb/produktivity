“Write me something about productivity” gets you filler — a generic text that would fit any blog and helps no one in particular. A good request gets you actual work. The difference between a bad and a great AI answer usually isn't the model — it's three missing pieces: who AI should be, what exactly you need, and what the output you get back should look like.

This holds for writing, analysis, and simple questions alike — those three missing puzzle pieces matter more than which specific model you happen to use.

## A typical scenario

An entrepreneur gets an office lease to sign and just tells AI “review this contract for me” — she gets a generic summary like “leases tend to carry risk around the notice period,” nothing specific to her actual text, essentially just what she could have guessed herself. Without context, the answer is useless. With a role, context, and a format — “you're an experienced commercial lawyer, I'm reviewing an office lease for five people with a budget of 60,000, return a table: risk, where in the contract, and a suggested fix” — she gets a specific, usable analysis of her own document that she can take straight to the landlord.

## How to do it

1. Set the role: “You're an experienced commercial lawyer…” — AI adapts its perspective and vocabulary to that role.
2. Add context: “…I'm reviewing an office lease for 5 people, budget 60,000…” The more specific the situation, the more specific the answer.
3. Specify the output format: “…return a table: risk, where in the contract, suggested fix.” Without a specified format, you get continuous text that's harder to use afterward.
4. Add what you don't want too: “No generic advice, just specific points from the text” — it narrows the room for filler.
5. If the first answer still contains vague spots, follow up specifically: “On point 2 — which exact clause in the contract does that refer to?”
6. Once a combination of role, context, and format works well for you, remember it or save it — next time you just tweak the details instead of the whole request.

## The best tools

- **ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini with a prompt that includes role, context, and format** — works in all of them; the difference comes from the request, not the tool.
- **Saved custom instructions in the AI app's settings** — if you use a particular role and format often, set it once and it applies to every conversation.
- **A prompt template saved in your prompt library** — for recurring types of analysis, like contract review or copy revisions, you prepare the three-part skeleton in advance.

Getting into the habit of framing requests this thoroughly pays off outside AI too — it's just as effective a way to hand tasks to colleagues.

## What you get out of it

- **Answer quality:** a specific request returns specific, usable output instead of generic phrases.
- **Time:** fewer rounds of follow-up questions, because the needed context is in the request the first time.
- **Clarity:** output in the format you asked for, like a table or bullet points, can be used right away without reformatting.
- **A habit:** you gradually learn to frame better requests for other tools and situations too, not just for AI.

## Pro tip

Always add what you don't want too: “No generic advice, just specific points from the text.” If the context includes sensitive data, like exact amounts or the names of contracting parties, consider using rounded or made-up values with the same structure instead of real numbers and names — that's usually enough for a contract risk analysis.
