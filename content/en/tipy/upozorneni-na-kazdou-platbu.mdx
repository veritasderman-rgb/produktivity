Most people have notifications in their banking app either turned off, or set to trigger only for larger amounts — “so it doesn't keep beeping.” But it's exactly the small, recurring charges that slip through: a subscription you meant to cancel six months ago, a trial that quietly rolled into a paid plan, or a fraudster's first cautious test (criminals typically start with a small amount to verify a stolen card works). A notification for every payment is the cheapest financial audit there is: it runs constantly and reports on its own.

## How to do it

1. In your banking app, find **Settings → Notifications** (sometimes “Alerts”) and turn on push notifications for **all card transactions and outgoing payments** — with no minimum amount.
2. If you have multiple accounts or cards (personal, business), go through the settings for each one. A rarely used card is the most common blind spot.
3. When a notification arrives that you don't recognize, act right away: for an unfamiliar charge, **lock** the card in the app (one tap, and just as easy to undo) and call the bank; for a forgotten subscription, take two minutes right then and cancel it while it's on your mind.
4. Make sure alerts don't get buried: grant the banking app an exception if you use focus mode or notification summaries.

The first few days it might beep unusually often — that's not a bug, that's information. Most people have no idea how many recurring charges they actually have.

## A typical scenario

At 9:14 on a Tuesday, a phone beeps: a charge for a streaming service the whole family stopped using back in the spring. Without the notification, the payment would have gone through quietly — like the five before it that nobody noticed on the statement. This way, dad cancels it right in his phone while waiting for coffee. A month later, a payment beeps in from an online store nobody in the family shopped at: the card can be locked instantly, and the damage stops at the one charge the bank disputes — not a drained account at the end of the month.

## What you get out of it

Forgotten subscriptions stop living off your inattention — every charge reminds you of itself once a month. You catch card fraud within minutes, which dramatically improves your chance of getting the money back. And incidentally, you get a real feel for how much you spend day to day — no spreadsheets, no extra work. ## Pro tip

Turn on notifications for **incoming payments** too. A freelancer knows instantly that a client paid an invoice — without having to open online banking every other day or send a reminder to someone who paid ages ago. In a family, it also kills the “has your paycheck landed yet?” question: the phone announces it before anyone has to ask.
