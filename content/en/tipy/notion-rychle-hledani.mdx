Clicking through the page tree in the left sidebar stops working around your twentieth page in a workspace — and in a real company or personal Notion, there will quickly be hundreds. Quick search is the only navigation that scales: it doesn't matter how deeply a page is nested or how many subpages it's buried under, you'll find it from the keyboard in a few seconds.

## A typical scenario

Filip has dozens of project pages in his company's Notion, each with its own subpages — brief, timeline, meeting notes. When he needs to quickly find the meeting notes for a specific project from last month, clicking through the tree means opening the project, then the subpage, then the right month — four or five clicks and a guess at exactly where he filed it back then. With quick search, he just presses the shortcut, types a few words from the title or even the content of the page, and the note he's looking for shows up in a moment, even if it's hidden five levels deep.

## How to do it

1. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>P</kbd> (on Mac, <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>P</kbd>) anywhere in Notion, no matter what page you're currently on.
2. Start typing the title of the page you're looking for, or even just text you know appears in it — Notion searches content too, not just titles.
3. Results filter as you type each character, so usually a few letters are enough for the page you want to show up near the top.
4. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to open the highlighted result, or use the up and down arrows to move through the options offered.
5. Recently visited pages show up at the top even without typing a search term, so the shortcut also works as a quick switcher between pages you were just working on.
6. It also works across shared workspaces — if you have access to multiple team spaces at once, search covers all of them you have permission for, so you don't have to switch between them manually first.

## The best tools

- **Quick search in Notion (Ctrl+P)** — a built-in, free feature that works instantly with no setup.
- **Notion AI search** — for more complex queries, when you're not sure of the exact page title but remember roughly what it was about.
- **Favorites** — for pages you return to regularly, it's worth pinning them to the sidebar as well, for an even faster route.
- **A page structure with clear titles** — no search, however good, replaces consistent naming, which lets you instantly recognize the right page among the search results.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** several minutes a day you'd otherwise spend clicking through the page tree and guessing where you filed something.
- **Less frustration:** an end to the feeling of “I know I wrote this down somewhere, but I don't know where.”
- **Smoother work:** your hands stay on the keyboard, so searching doesn't break your focus the way switching to the mouse and clicking through panels does.
- **A workspace that scales:** it works just as well with twenty pages as with five hundred, so it's worth building the habit early, not once the tree has grown out of control.

## Pro tip

The same shortcut also works as a quick switcher between your most recently opened pages — press it and you'll see your history right away, without typing anything. Handy when you're bouncing back and forth between two or three pages.
