You need to get a file onto the computer of someone sitting a meter away from you — so you email it to yourself across half the internet, or go hunting for a flash drive. Mac owners have AirDrop for this; less well known is that Windows has the same thing. It's called Nearby Share, and it sends files and links directly between nearby computers over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi — no cable, no attachments, no cloud storage.

## How to do it

1. On both computers, turn on **Settings → System → Nearby sharing** and choose a visibility level: **My devices only** (sharing between your own computers signed into the same Microsoft account), or **Everyone nearby**.
2. Make sure Bluetooth and Wi-Fi are both on — they're what negotiate and carry out the transfer. It's fastest when both computers are on the same Wi-Fi network.
3. Right-click a file and choose **Share**. After a moment, the sharing window shows the names of computers in range — click the right one.
4. The recipient gets a notification with an accept button; the file saves into their Downloads folder (the destination can be changed in sharing settings).
5. Just like files, you can share a link from the Edge browser too — handy when you want to “toss” an open page to a coworker.

## A typical scenario

At a meeting, a colleague needs your presentation along with its videos — 300 MB. Email won't take it, the company file storage is asking to sign in again right now, and the flash drive is “somewhere in a drawer.” Nearby Share: right-click the file, Share, click his laptop, he accepts. The file travels directly between the two computers and lands in place moments later — while the meeting carries on uninterrupted.

Second scene: you have a work laptop and a home computer. Instead of emailing files to yourself, you set both to “My devices only” and shuttle files between them in two clicks, as if it were one machine.

## What you get out of it

- **No more emailing yourself:** the file travels a meter through the air, not across the internet and someone else's servers.
- **Large files with no attachment limits:** a video or a batch of photos gets through where email long ago gave up.
- **Speed in the room:** handing off a file during a meeting stops being a five-minute logistical exercise.
- **Fewer copies on other people's servers:** whatever's handed off directly doesn't have to sit in inboxes and shared drives — for internal materials, that's cleaner from a data-hygiene standpoint too.

## Pro tip

Nearby Share works between Windows computers. For a computer-to-phone pair, reach for the [Phone Link app](/tipy/win-propojeni-s-telefonem), which transfers photos and messages too — and if you mix Windows and Mac at home, classic cloud storage serves as neutral ground.
