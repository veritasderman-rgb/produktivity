You don't need a mouse or arrow keys to read. Your browser has a built-in shortcut that almost nobody knows, even though it's perfectly suited to the thing we do most in it — reading long pages, articles, and study materials. Just keep your hands on the keyboard and the page scrolls itself, exactly one screen at a time, not some random amount like scrolling with a mouse wheel.

## A typical scenario

You're studying for an exam from course notes open as a long web page or a PDF in your browser. Your hand keeps moving between the keyboard (taking notes) and the mouse (scrolling the text) — every switch costs a sliver of attention, and it adds up over a long study session. A mouse wheel also easily overshoots, scrolling further than you meant, and you have to scroll back.

With Space, your hands stay on the keyboard the whole time. You read a screen, tap Space, read the next one — smooth, uninterrupted, and you never lose your place. It works just as well outside of studying — reading long documentation, a software manual, or a lengthy news article, where a mouse wheel would otherwise carry you further than you wanted and force you to scroll back.

## How to do it

1. <kbd>Space</kbd> — scrolls the page down exactly one screen. Works in every major browser (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari), as long as your cursor isn't in a text field or form.
2. <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Space</kbd> — scrolls the page up one screen, for when you need to go back to the previous paragraph.
3. <kbd>Home</kbd> — jumps straight to the top of the page, <kbd>End</kbd> to the very bottom — handy on long documents when you want to get to the start or the footer quickly.
4. The shortcut also works in PDFs viewed directly in the browser, so it's just as useful for course notes, contracts, or longer articles.
5. If you accidentally click into a text field (say, the on-page search box) and Space suddenly just types spaces, click outside the field or press <kbd>Esc</kbd> — the shortcut goes back to scrolling.
6. Pairing it with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>F</kbd> (find on page) is a useful combo: find the keyword first, then use Space to read the rest of the paragraph around it, without ever reaching for the mouse.

## The best tools

- **The browser's built-in shortcut** — works with nothing to install in Chrome, Firefox, Edge, and Safari, and covers 90% of your reading needs on its own.
- **Reader Mode** — in Safari and Firefox, it strips out ads and sidebars, so Space scrolls through clean text without distracting jumps.
- **Vimium (Chrome and Firefox extension)** — for power users, adds more keyboard shortcuts for navigating pages and links, inspired by the Vim editor.
- **Pocket or Instapaper** — if you read a lot of long articles, saving to a reader strips out the page's visual clutter entirely, and Space then pages through pure text.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** a few minutes a day that would otherwise go to switching between keyboard and mouse — for people who spend hours reading study materials, that adds up to hours over a semester.
- **Focus:** hands stay on the keyboard, attention stays in the text — fewer small interruptions mean smoother reading.
- **Precision:** the page moves exactly one screen at a time, no random overshoot from a mouse wheel and no hunting for where you left off.
- **Comfort:** works the same on a laptop with no mouse, on a trackpad, or with an external mouse — one shortcut, universal use.
