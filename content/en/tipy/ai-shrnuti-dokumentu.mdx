Reading forty pages start to finish is often the wrong order — you need the map first, then the terrain. AI can run through a long document in seconds and give you back an overview of the main points, complete with a reference to exactly where in the text to find them, so you know what to focus on when you actually read it, and what you can safely skim.

## A typical scenario

A manager gets a thirty-page market study before a meeting and has to comment on it within an hour, but there's realistically no time to read the whole thing. Without this tip, he skims the document diagonally, skips an important passage on page 22, and only speaks superficially in the meeting because the key number slipped past him. With this tip, he uploads the study to AI, has it produce an excerpt of the main points with page references, and reads only the three passages that actually matter for the decision — so in the meeting he speaks specifically, even though he never read the whole document start to finish.

## How to do it

1. Upload the document to AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) as a file, or paste the text directly into the conversation if it's short.
2. Give a specific prompt: “Summarize in 10 points. For each, note where in the document it is. List deadlines, amounts, and obligations separately.”
3. For contracts, ask targeted questions: “What in this contract is nonstandard or works against me?”
4. Read the key passages AI flags as important in the original — the excerpt is a map, not a substitute for the actual text at the points that matter most.
5. For long documents, or several at once — like multiple versions of a contract — also ask for a comparison: “What changed between the versions?”
6. If you're using the excerpt as the basis for a meeting or a decision, verify the most important number or deadline against the original — one click to the cited page saves you an unpleasant surprise.

## The best tools

Match the tool to the type of document — a one-off contract fits fine in an ordinary chat, while sources you use repeatedly are better suited to a specialized tool.

- **ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini with file upload** — a built-in feature, handles PDF, Word, and scanned documents.
- **Google's NotebookLM** — specialized for working with your own sources, sticks strictly to the uploaded document and cites passages.
- **Adobe Acrobat with an AI assistant** — if you already work with PDFs in Acrobat, summaries and questions happen right in the document viewer.
- **Your own notes alongside the AI excerpt** — even with an AI summary, it's worth writing the key points in your own words; you'll remember them better.

This approach works for anything long — annual reports, legal analyses, scientific studies, and internal policies alike.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** an hour of reading shrinks to ten minutes of focused reading on the key passages.
- **Better prep:** you speak specifically, not superficially, in a meeting or negotiation, because you know the key points and where to find them in the text.
- **Sanity:** less stress from a pile of unread documents waiting for a meeting.
- **Catching risks:** for contracts, AI flags nonstandard clauses that a quick read might have missed.

## Pro tip

For contracts, ask targeted questions: “What in this contract is nonstandard or works against me?” — that one question often reveals more than a general summary. If the document contains third-party personal data that isn't needed for the content analysis, like national ID numbers or addresses, consider redacting or removing it before uploading it to AI.
