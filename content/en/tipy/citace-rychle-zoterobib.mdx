Formatting citations is work nobody wants to do by hand — and at the same time isn't something you should hand off to a language model, since it can occasionally invent details or entire sources. Between the two extremes there's a third way: generators that build a citation from a source's actual metadata. The fastest is ZoteroBib — a website with no sign-up, where you paste a URL, DOI, or ISBN and get a finished citation in your chosen style. For journal articles there's an even shorter path: the "Cite" button right inside Google Scholar.

## How to do it

**ZoteroBib (zbib.org):**

1. Open **zbib.org** — no account, no install.
2. Paste in whatever you have: a web page URL, an article's DOI, a book's ISBN, or just its title. The site looks up the metadata and adds the entry to your list.
3. Switch citation styles at the top — there are thousands to choose from, including APA and the styles of specific journals.
4. Drop in all your sources one by one and copy the finished bibliography into your paper (it even exports to the clipboard with formatting intact).

**Google Scholar:** click **Cite** (the quotation-mark icon) next to any result — you'll get a citation in several styles ready to copy.

One rule to finish with: always skim a generated citation. The metadata is usually correct, but a website citation sometimes has a missing author or date — filling in two fields is still faster than writing the citation from scratch.

## A typical scenario

The evening before a paper is due, "just" the works-cited list is left: twelve sources, four books, five articles, three websites. Doing it by hand to a citation standard would mean an hour of tedious work and almost certainly inconsistent punctuation. With ZoteroBib, the student pastes in three ISBNs copied off the backs of the books, five DOIs, and four URLs — in ten minutes she has a uniformly formatted list, which she just checks and drops into the document.

## What you get out of it

Bibliographies stop being a reason to put off finishing the paper. Citations come out consistent, because a machine formats them from one template, not a tired person at eleven at night. And unlike citations requested from a chatbot, they're built from real metadata — no risk of an invented source. If you're writing something longer with dozens of sources, it's worth switching to full Zotero; for research with verifiable sources, there's the tip [AI search engines with citations](/tipy/ai-vyhledavani-citace).

## Pro tip

ZoteroBib only remembers your list in the browser where you created it. Before closing your work, use the link button to generate the address of your bibliography and save it with your notes — that way you can reopen the list on a different computer too. And if you write directly in Google Docs, it has a built-in citation helper under **Tools → Citations**, which inserts sources straight into the text, including in-text citations.
