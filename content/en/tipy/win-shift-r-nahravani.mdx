Explaining a computer procedure in text is a slog: “click top left, then the third tab, check the box there…” — and the recipient still gets it wrong. Video solves it in thirty seconds, yet plenty of people install special software just for that. No need: newer Windows 11 has screen recording built right into the Snipping Tool, complete with its own keyboard shortcut. Mark an area, click start, and save the result as an ordinary MP4.

## How to do it

1. Press <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>R</kbd> — the screen dims just like with the [Win+Shift+S screenshot](/tipy/win-shift-s-vystrizek), only in video mode. If the shortcut doesn't do anything, open the **Snipping Tool** from the Start menu and switch to the camera icon at the top; the feature is missing on older Windows versions.
2. Drag with the mouse to mark the area you want to record — the whole screen, or just the window of the app in question.
3. On the bar next to the selection, you can turn on the **microphone** (spoken commentary) and **system audio** before starting. Click start; a short countdown runs and then recording begins.
4. Stop the recording with the stop button — the video opens in a preview, from which you save it as **MP4** or copy it directly to paste into chat or email.
5. For games and longer recordings, there's a second built-in route: the Xbox Game Bar on <kbd>Win</kbd> + <kbd>G</kbd> can record the active app in the background.

## A typical scenario

An accountant asks for the fourth time where orders get approved in the system. Instead of a fourth phone call, you record a thirty-second video with commentary once: Win+Shift+R, mark the app window, turn on the microphone, click through the steps with spoken narration, save, send. She replays it whenever she's unsure — and the question never comes your way again.

The teacher's version: a short recording of “how to submit an assignment in Classroom” sent to students and parents saves dozens of repeated explanations — and it can be sent again at the start of the next school year.

## What you get out of it

- **One explanation instead of five:** the video can be replayed as many times as needed — the instructions stop being explained and start being sent.
- **Zero installs:** no recording software, no watermarks, no registration — it's all in the system.
- **Speed even for small things:** when recording takes a minute, you'll make a video even for a minor point that wouldn't justify installing a tool.

## Pro tip

Don't expect an editing suite — the built-in tool can trim the beginning and end at most. For captions, zoom, and montage you need a real editor. But for the vast majority of “click here like this” videos: record it in one take with commentary, send it, done — nobody expects perfection here.
