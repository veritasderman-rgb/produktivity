Notifications are probably the biggest thief of attention on a modern computer — a Slack message in the middle of focused work can shatter your concentration for long minutes. The problem isn't just the time spent reading the message, but mainly how long it takes to sink back into the interrupted train of thought. Focus modes on macOS can filter notifications based on what you're currently doing and let through only what's genuinely important. Set them up once, then just switch between them depending on the situation.

## A typical scenario

You're writing an important message with a deadline an hour away. Without filtering notifications, over that hour you'll get pinged by a social media notification, a reminder from a game app, and a message from a friend about the weekend — each one pulling you out of a half-finished sentence for a few seconds, and you have to rebuild your focus from scratch every time.

With Work mode turned on, only messages from colleagues and family you've pre-approved get through. Everything else waits until you turn the mode off — and you finish the message in one uninterrupted stretch. For colleagues who genuinely need you urgently, you can also allow so-called repeated calls — if the same person calls you twice in a row within three minutes, the call gets through even with the mode active.

## How to do it

1. Open **System Settings → Focus**.
2. Click the plus button and create a new mode — Apple offers ready-made templates (Work, Personal, Gaming, Sleep), or you can build your own from scratch.
3. For each mode, set **who** is allowed to contact you (specific people or groups from your contacts) and **which apps** can send notifications while the mode is active.
4. Turn a mode on by clicking it in Control Center in the top-right menu bar, or set up automation — based on time of day, location, or when a specific app launches.
5. Other notifications quietly pile up in Notification Center while the mode is on — nothing is lost, it just doesn't show up right away.
6. You can also set a custom auto-reply in Messages and Mail that automatically lets the sender know you have a focus mode on and will get back to them later.

## The best tools

- **Built-in Focus modes** — fully integrated into the system, free, works across your Apple devices.
- **Cross-device sharing** (in Focus settings) — a mode turned on on your Mac automatically silences your iPhone and Apple Watch signed into the same account.
- **Third-party distraction-blocking apps** (e.g. Freedom) — for anyone who needs to block entire sites and apps, not just notifications.
- **A Pomodoro timer combined with Work mode** — run both at once and you get a well-defined block of focused work.
- **Automation via Shortcuts** — you can set a specific mode to trigger automatically when you plug in headphones or open a particular app.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** dozens of minutes a day that would otherwise go into your attention ramping back up after every interruption — returning to full concentration after being pulled away typically takes several minutes.
- **Calm:** no more feeling like you have to react to every ping instantly.
- **Control:** you decide what's urgent and what can wait, instead of an app that wants your attention deciding for you.
- **Better work:** long uninterrupted blocks of focus lead to fewer mistakes and better results than working in constant attention-switching mode.

## Pro tip

Turn on **Share Across Devices** in Focus settings — without it, the phone sitting next to your keyboard will keep interrupting you even with a mode active on your Mac. With sharing on, your whole set of devices quiets down together.
