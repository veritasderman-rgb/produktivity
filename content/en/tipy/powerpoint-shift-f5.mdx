Clicking your way to slide 34 through an entire presentation is pointless, especially when you're just fine-tuning an animation or a line of text in one specific spot. PowerPoint has its own shortcut for exactly this case, one letter different from the usual one, and it saves dozens of clicks every time you're fine-tuning a longer presentation.

## A typical scenario

You've got a finished forty-slide presentation, and all that's left is fine-tuning an animation on slide 34 — text should appear one line at a time, not all at once. Without the shortcut, you start the slideshow with <kbd>F5</kbd>, which drops you on slide 1, and then you have to click through thirty-three more slides before you reach the one you need to test. You fix the animation, want to check it right away — and click through the whole thing from the start again. With <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>F5</kbd>, the slideshow jumps straight to the slide your cursor is on in the editor, so the "tweak → check" cycle takes seconds instead of minutes.

## How to do it

1. In editing view, open the slide you're currently working on by clicking its thumbnail in the left-hand panel.
2. Press <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>F5</kbd> — the slideshow starts in presentation mode right from this slide, no clicking through from the beginning.
3. Check how the animation or transition looks for real.
4. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> to return to editing on the same slide, make your adjustment, and press <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>F5</kbd> again for another test.
5. For comparison: plain <kbd>F5</kbd> always starts the presentation from the very beginning — good for a full dress rehearsal, not for tuning one spot.
6. The same logic works in Google Slides too: playing from the current slide is in the presentation menu, though without the same keyboard shortcut as PowerPoint.

## The best tools

- **The built-in Shift+F5 shortcut** — works directly in PowerPoint with nothing to configure.
- **The "From Current Slide" icon** on the Slide Show tab — the same feature with the mouse, in case you forget the shortcut.
- **Presenter view** paired with Shift+F5 — even testing from a specific slide, you still see your notes and the timer, so you can rehearse your spoken delivery at the same time.

## What you get out of it

- **Time:** roughly 2–3 minutes saved on every round of tuning on a longer presentation, where clicking through from the start would otherwise take longer than the edit itself.
- **Smoother iteration:** the "tweak, check, tweak again" cycle can run in quick succession, without losing focus waiting.
- **Less frustration:** on presentations with dozens of slides, you skip the annoying repeated clicking back to the same spot.
- **Focus on content:** the time you'd otherwise spend navigating stays on actually fine-tuning the animation or text.

## Pro tip

You'll appreciate the shortcut for a final check right before a meeting too — instead of replaying the whole presentation from the start (and risking running out of time), jump straight to the slides you're least sure look right live.
