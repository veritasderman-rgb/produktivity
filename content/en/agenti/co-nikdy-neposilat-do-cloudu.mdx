This part is short on purpose. It isn't an essay, it's a list you can print out.

Before we begin: the point isn't that AI providers are suspect. The point is that **sending data is an irreversible operation.** What's gone is gone. Deleting the conversation doesn't bring it back, a change in the terms doesn't bring it back, and regret certainly doesn't.

## The list

**1. Credentials.** Passwords, API keys, tokens, the contents of secrets files. Not even “just so you can check the format for me”. A key pasted into a chat is a key that needs to be revoked.

**2. Health data.** Your own and other people's. Medical reports, diagnoses, results. A special category of personal data with protection to match.

**3. Clients' personal data without their consent.** Names, addresses, birth numbers, account numbers, anything a person can be identified by. When you need to work with such a text, anonymise it **before** sending, not in the prompt.

**4. Unpublished company information.** Contracts in progress, pricing, payroll records, plans, acquisition documents, anything that hasn't gone out yet.

**5. Other people's confidential materials.** Documents someone sent you on the understanding that they're internal. They aren't yours, and it isn't your place to pass them on.

**6. Anything under a duty of confidentiality.** If you've signed an NDA, it covers AI tools too. The fact that it's a machine changes nothing about the obligation.

**7. Recordings and transcripts of meetings where you don't have everyone's consent.** A transcript of a meeting is the personal data of everyone who took part.

## The grey zone

Some things aren't forbidden, but they deserve a conscious decision:

- **Internal minutes without names.** Usually fine, but think through what can be inferred from them.
- **The code of a company project.** It depends on the company and on what's in the contract with the provider.
- **Your own writing in progress.** Technically nothing stands in the way, but some people don't want it that way.
- **Photos and scans of ID documents.** The text recognition happens somewhere — and everything else in the photo goes with it.

The rule for the grey zone: **if this turned up in public tomorrow, would it be a problem?** If yes, it belongs at home.

## Why rules in your head aren't enough

Because you won't remember them at the one moment it matters — in a hurry, on a Friday afternoon, when you need an answer fast. That's why it has to be arranged technically.

## A filter that stops a mistake

If you have the gateway from the third track, a simple check can be added to it: before a request leaves for an external provider, it's run against patterns. It isn't perfect, but it catches the most common mistakes.

What to look for:

- **Czech birth number:** six digits, a slash, three or four digits.
- **IBAN:** two letters and a long string of characters.
- **Payment card number:** sixteen digits, possibly with spaces.
- **API keys:** typical prefixes such as `sk-`, `ghp_`, `AKIA`.
- **Internal markings:** whatever your company uses to label confidential documents.

When the filter finds something, it **shouldn't quietly redirect the request to the local model** — it should stop and ask. Silent redirection teaches you to ignore the filter.

## When it has already happened

1. **Don't cover it up.** Lost time is worse than owning up.
2. **Revoke what can be revoked.** Keys and passwords immediately.
3. **Establish the extent.** What exactly went out, when and to whom.
4. **Look at the provider's terms** to see how long it keeps data and how to request erasure.
5. **For personal data, consider the notification duty.** Depending on the nature of the incident, it may be a personal data breach that has to be reported to the supervisory authority. Don't ask the internet about this; ask a lawyer.
6. **Change the setup, not just the behaviour.** If it could happen once, it can happen again.

## Pro tip

The best investment in peace of mind is a boring one: **set up two working environments.** One “internal”, with a local model as the default, no cloud keys, and the folders with sensitive data. The other “ordinary”, with the full cloud and no access to the first. Switching takes a second, and from then on you don't have to think about every single request — you decided on the split once, in advance and with a clear head.
