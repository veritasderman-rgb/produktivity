**What happened:** According to the release notes of 20 November 2026, Anthropic made **computer use** available in beta on **Team and Enterprise** plans. With it, Claude sees the user's screen and works in the desktop apps the user allows: it clicks and types the way a person would. It works in Claude Desktop on macOS 14 or later and on Windows. It can also be used from the web and mobile if the organization has "Computer access" turned on and Claude Desktop is open on a connected computer. The feature is **off by default**, is rolling out to organizations gradually and requires the new unified Claude experience ("Chat and Cowork unified").

**What it means for you:** Connectors and MCP cover tools that have an API. Computer use is aimed at the rest: an older accounting program, an internal system with no integration, a vendor's desktop app. Examples where it makes sense:

- **Retyping between systems.** "Open the order export in Excel and enter the first twenty rows into the inventory program. Stop before saving each record."
- **A repetitive clicking routine.** The monthly download of reports from an app that has no scheduled export, saved to the right folder.
- **Checking, not typing.** Claude goes through the settings in an app and writes up what differs from your internal policy. It changes nothing, it only reads.

Expect clicking around a screen to be slower and less reliable than a connector. Where an integration exists, it remains the better choice.

**How to get started:**

1. An organization owner (Owner or Primary Owner) opens Organization settings → Capabilities and confirms that "Chat and Cowork unified" is on.
2. Under Claude Desktop, they turn on **Computer use**. On Enterprise they can first clear the roles that shouldn't have it. On Team the setting applies to everyone.
3. Before switching it on, they set the app list: either "Block these apps" (the listed apps are denied) or "Allow only these apps" (everything else is denied).
4. Each user updates Claude Desktop, turns on **Enable computer use** on their computer and, on macOS, grants the Accessibility and Screen & System Audio Recording permissions.
5. Start with a task where nothing gets sent or deleted, and watch what Claude does.

**What to watch out for:**

- **No sandbox.** The help article states plainly that there is no separate environment between Claude and your apps. Content on the screen, such as a web page or an email, could lead Claude to take an unintended action. Through approved apps it can change and delete files outside connected folders, and "read only" settings don't apply to computer use.
- **AI proposes, you approve.** For anything that sends, pays or deletes, ask in your prompt for a stop before the final step. In addition, the user approves each app separately.
- **Screenshots are saved.** They stay with the chat and may contain personal or confidential information. Before you start, close the windows that don't belong there: banking, payroll, someone else's email. In a company, talk it through with whoever is responsible for GDPR.
- **The app lists have gaps.** They don't cover websites, browser extensions or apps inside a remote desktop. Turning the feature off reaches users in about an hour, not instantly.
- **Who it isn't for.** The beta is not available to organizations with a HIPAA-ready configuration, customer-managed encryption keys or zero data retention for Claude Code. The feature is part of paid plans, see the [plans overview](https://claude.com/pricing).
