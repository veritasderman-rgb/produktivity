**What happened:** On 10 November 2026 Google started expanding two Gemini features in Gmail to **all Google Workspace users aged 18 and older**. Until now they were limited to personal accounts and selected editions. The features are:

- **AI Overview** — a summary at the top of a long thread that pulls out the key points and replies from the whole conversation
- **Help me write** — a full email draft from a short prompt, or a rewrite of text you have already written (the basic version, without adapting to your personal style)

The rollout is gradual and, according to Google, can take up to 15 days in both Rapid Release and Scheduled Release domains. One important detail: the features are **on by default**.

**What it means for you:** If your company email runs on Google in one of the basic editions, AI in Gmail will show up with no add-on and nothing to install. In practice it helps most in two situations:

- **Coming back to a busy thread.** You return from holiday and the "Next year's budget" thread has 34 messages. The summary at the top tells you what people agreed on and what is still open. You then open the thread only where you need the exact wording.
- **Routine replies.** Instead of writing from scratch you type something like "thank them for the meeting, confirm delivery by the end of the month and ask for the invoicing details". You get a draft to polish and send.

The biggest saving is not in a single email but in the total. Ten routine replies a day at two minutes each add up to more than an hour and a half a week.

**How to get started:**

1. Open a longer thread in Gmail on the web and check whether a summary is offered above it. If not, first check that [smart features](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/15604322) are turned on in your Gmail settings. Google lists them as a requirement, and in Europe they may not be on automatically. Only then does a missing summary mean the rollout hasn't reached you yet.
2. In the compose window, look for the **Help me write** button and describe what the email should say.
3. Edit the draft to make it yours. Check names, amounts, dates and tone.
4. Don't want the features? Turn them off in Gmail settings under smart features. Be aware that, according to available information, the same switch also turns off other smart helpers.
5. Workspace admins will find the control in the Admin console. Google's help article on [managing access to Gemini features](https://support.google.com/a/answer/15698295) describes the steps.

**What to watch out for:**

- **On without asking.** If you are an admin or a business owner, make a conscious decision about whether to keep the features on. According to press reports, the admin setting overrides the user's choice. Tell colleagues that something new will appear in their inbox and what to do with it.
- **A summary is not the thread.** AI can miss a caveat hidden in the third reply or mix up who promised what. Before deciding about money, a contract or a deadline, read the original messages.
- **AI proposes, you approve.** Help me write doesn't send anything by itself, but a draft sounds confident even when it has made a detail up. The email you send is yours, not the tool's.
- **Languages.** The announcement doesn't say which languages are supported. If you write in a language other than English, check the quality on your own threads before relying on summaries.
- **Sensitive mail.** In companies with confidential communication (HR, legal matters, health data), talk to your admin about how Google handles data when Gemini is used.
