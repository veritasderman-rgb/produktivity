**What happened:** On 16 November 2026 Google announced that Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) can build an **interactive site** from the sources you uploaded. You type "make me a site" in the Notebook chat and describe what you need. The result is a page you can click around in: Google mentions an interactive dashboard on quarterly sales, a timeline, a visual explainer for exam prep and a tracker for tasks and deadlines. You open the site in a new tab and can share it. The feature is rolling out gradually (up to 15 days) to all Google Workspace customers and personal accounts, in each case to users aged 18 or older. For now it works on the web, with the mobile app to follow "soon".

**What it means for you:** Until now Notebook summarized your material, answered questions or turned it into an audio overview. Now it builds a tool you keep working with. Three situations where it helps:

- **A report people can click through.** Upload sales exports and meeting notes and ask: "Make me a site: a third-quarter sales dashboard with a region filter and the three main findings at the top." In the meeting you show a page, not a spreadsheet.
- **A tracker from a pile of documents.** A contract, a schedule and client emails become an overview of tasks and deadlines with checkboxes. According to the help article, the site remembers what you ticked and the notes you typed.
- **Onboarding a new colleague.** Turn internal guidelines into a timeline of the first month or a clickable walkthrough of a process. It reads better than a thirty-page PDF.

**How to get started:**

1. On a computer, open notebook.google.com and pick a Notebook you are allowed to edit. Three to five well-chosen sources are enough for a first try.
2. In the chat panel, type "make me a site" and add a description: who the site is for, what should be visible first and which controls you want.
3. Generation takes a while. You can keep working in the Notebook in the meantime.
4. The finished site appears in the Studio panel. Use the **More** menu and choose **Open in new tab** to try it full screen.
5. If the result isn't right, refine the request and have a new version built. A specific description works better than a generic "make an overview".

**What to watch out for:**

- **Check the numbers.** The site looks finished and convincing, which makes it tempting to trust. Neither the announcement nor the help article promises that every figure on the page carries a citation. Before you show a dashboard to management or a client, compare the key numbers with the source. The rule "AI proposes, you approve" applies in full.
- **Sharing a link means sharing the whole Notebook.** According to the help article, the recipient needs access to the entire Notebook, including every source in it. The link-sharing steps are described for personal accounts. If you don't want that, you can download the site as a file.
- **Everyone sees only their own inputs.** On a shared site, ticks and notes are stored separately for each person. It won't work as a team tracker where everybody sees the same status.
- **Limits and admin control.** Usage limits apply to creating sites depending on your plan, and a company admin can turn Gemini Notebook off for selected users. Higher limits are part of a paid plan, see the [Google Workspace plans overview](https://workspace.google.com/pricing).
- **Language.** Google doesn't list supported languages. If your sources aren't in English, try the feature on something unimportant first.
